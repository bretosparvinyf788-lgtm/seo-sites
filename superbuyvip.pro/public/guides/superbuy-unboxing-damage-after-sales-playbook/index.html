After the Superbuy Parcel Arrives: An Unboxing, Damage Evidence, and After-Sales Playbook

A practical method for receiving an international parcel, preserving usable evidence, separating shipping damage from seller defects, and submitting a decision-ready after-sales case.

PUBLISHED 2026-09-08INDEPENDENT BUYER RESEARCH

Delivery is an evidence event, not the finish line

The tracking page says delivered, the carton is finally at your door, and the natural impulse is to tear it open. That is exactly when a strong order record can collapse. International parcels pass through carriers and customs that Superbuy does not directly control, and Superbuy's public workflow expressly acknowledges risks such as delay, confiscation, tax, damage and loss. Your first job is not celebration or complaint. It is preserving the condition in which the parcel arrived. Treat receipt as a short inspection appointment. Have a phone, scale, clean surface, saved parcel record and warehouse photographs ready before opening. Ten disciplined minutes can produce far better evidence than a long explanation written after packaging has been discarded.

Save the parcel record before anything changes

Open My Parcel and capture the parcel number, tracking number, route, submitted items, declared information, estimated shipping deposit, final adjustment and any selected packaging or insurance services. Superbuy's official guidance says tracking remains available through User Center and My Parcel after dispatch. Save the last carrier scan and the delivery time as well. This creates a bridge between the digital shipment and the physical carton. If the label is damaged, an item is missing or the carrier later changes its status, you still have the original record. Keep warehouse photos and the item order numbers in the same folder. Evidence becomes useful when every image can be tied to one parcel, one item and one timeline.

Inspect the unopened carton from all six sides

Before cutting tape, photograph the shipping label and every face of the box. Include close views of punctures, crushed corners, moisture, retaping, open seams or unusually loose areas. Take one wider image that shows the entire carton and one close image for each defect. If the parcel is visibly wet, leaking or unsafe, avoid handling it casually and follow the carrier's local instructions. The purpose is to show condition before your actions could have caused it. Do not cover the label with your hand, and do not rely only on a video that moves too quickly to read details. Still photographs establish identity; continuous video establishes sequence. You usually need both.

Weigh first and film one continuous opening

If practical, place the sealed parcel on a scale and record the reading with the whole box visible. Compare it with the dispatched parcel data, but treat a difference as a clue rather than proof because scales and packaging records can vary. Start a continuous video before opening. Show the label, sealed edges, external damage and weight, then open the parcel without pausing and remove contents one layer at a time. Keep the camera view wide enough to show that items are not being added or removed off-screen. A continuous record is especially valuable for missing goods, swapped contents, internal breakage or an empty accessory compartment. Do not edit the original file.

Preserve every packaging layer until the count is complete

Set aside the outer carton, reinforcement, waterproof layer, filler, retail boxes, labels and small envelopes instead of throwing them away. Photograph how products were positioned before rearranging them. Packaging may help distinguish a carrier impact from insufficient protection, a factory defect or damage that existed in warehouse photographs. Small accessories are often hidden inside foam, shoe boxes, garment folds or separate bags, so complete the count before calling anything missing. If the carton contains several orders, create a simple receiving row for each item: expected, received, visible condition, accessories and evidence filename. This prevents one chaotic unboxing from turning into an inaccurate claim.

Reconcile four sources, not just the shopping list

Compare the physical contents against four records: the original product fingerprint, the paid order, the warehouse photographs and the parcel submission list. Each answers a different question. The listing defines the promised model and variation. The paid order confirms quantity and selected options. Warehouse evidence shows what was visible before international packing. The submission list shows what should have entered this parcel. A warehouse image cannot prove hidden contents or functionality, while a product page cannot prove what the warehouse received. When the records disagree, mark the exact point of divergence. That makes it possible to classify the issue instead of sending a vague message that the order is wrong.

Classify the problem before asking for a remedy

Use one primary category per affected item: parcel missing, item missing from a delivered parcel, transit damage, visible seller defect, wrong variation, packaging-service mismatch, customs action or functional failure. Then describe observable facts without guessing the cause. “Left corner crushed, plastic housing cracked and fragments inside the sealed retail box” is stronger than “the carrier destroyed it.” Classification matters because different evidence and responsible parties may apply. Superbuy notes that international delivery is operated by designated third parties and cannot be fully controlled. The platform can assist with issues, but your claim should not assume responsibility before the records are reviewed. Facts first, theory second, requested outcome third.

Separate warehouse-visible defects from transit damage

Place the warehouse image beside the arrival image at the same angle when possible. If a dent, stain or wrong color was already visible in storage, record that fact. If the warehouse image shows an intact corner and the delivered item is broken beside a crushed parcel wall, preserve both images as a comparison. If the relevant area was never photographed, say that it was not visible rather than claiming it was perfect. This distinction protects credibility. A strong case openly identifies what the evidence proves, what it suggests and what remains unknown. Do not enhance, crop away context or annotate the only copy. Keep originals and create separate marked versions for explanation.

Open the after-sales case with a compact evidence packet

Superbuy's shopping and forwarding guidance says that after receipt, buyers can select the relevant parcel and apply for after-sales service. Submit promptly through the matching parcel rather than scattering the story across unrelated enquiries. Start with parcel number, item order number, date received and a one-sentence problem statement. Add a numbered evidence list: delivery scan, unopened carton, label, continuous opening video, internal packaging, item condition, count sheet and warehouse comparison. State the remedy you are requesting, such as review for compensation, missing-item investigation or instructions for the next step. Keep the full-resolution originals available even if the form requires compressed uploads.

Do not destroy evidence or repair the item too early

Until the case is acknowledged and instructions are clear, keep the carton, labels, packing material, damaged item and detached pieces. Do not wash stained goods, glue broken parts, power on unsafe electronics or return anything to a local carrier without written direction. A repair may reduce the ability to assess original damage; discarding packaging may remove the strongest link to transit handling. At the same time, take reasonable safety precautions with sharp glass, swollen batteries, leaks or contamination. Photograph the hazard from a safe distance, isolate it appropriately and follow local safety rules. Evidence preservation never requires unsafe handling.

Close the loop even when the result is imperfect

Record the submission date, case number, requested remedy, each response, any carrier document and the final resolution. Separate reimbursement, account credit, coupon, repair cost and unrecovered loss rather than calling every outcome a refund. Add what you would change next time: parcel split, stronger protection, different route, clearer warehouse evidence, insurance decision or lower concentrated value. Superbuy offers optional services such as insurance and reinforcement during parcel submission, but an after-sales record is what lets you judge whether those choices would have addressed the actual failure. The best receiving process does more than support one claim. It converts the final parcel into better decisions for the next shipment.

Superbuy Delivery and After-Sales: 12 Practical Questions

What should I photograph before opening a Superbuy parcel?

Photograph the label, every side of the unopened carton, seals, corners and every visible puncture, crush, wet area or retaping mark. Include both full-carton and close views.

Should I make an unboxing video?

Yes when the parcel or contents have meaningful value. Record continuously from the sealed label and carton through removal and count of every item, and preserve the unedited original.

Why weigh the unopened parcel?

The reading can help investigate a missing-item claim when compared with dispatch records, but it is supporting evidence rather than proof because scales and recorded weights may differ.

Can I discard the carton after taking photos?

Keep the carton, label and internal packaging until the count is complete and any after-sales case is resolved or Superbuy confirms they are no longer required.

Where can I track a dispatched Superbuy parcel?

Superbuy's official workflow directs buyers to User Center and My Parcel for tracking after dispatch. Save the parcel record and final delivery scan before opening.

Where do I request after-sales help after delivery?

Select the relevant parcel in My Parcel and apply for after-sales service. Link every affected item and evidence file to that parcel and its order number.

What is the difference between transit damage and a seller defect?

Transit damage is associated with handling after packing; a seller defect existed in the product itself. Compare warehouse and arrival evidence without claiming more than the images prove.

What if an item appears to be missing?

Recheck every packaging layer and accessory compartment, then reconcile the paid order, warehouse record and parcel submission list before filing a missing-item case.

Should I repair a damaged item immediately?

Usually wait until the case is acknowledged and instructions are clear. Repairing, washing or discarding parts can alter the condition that needs to be assessed.

Does delivery tracking prove the parcel arrived intact?

No. A delivered scan records a logistics event, not the condition or completeness of the carton. Document the physical parcel at receipt.

Does Superbuy control every carrier or customs outcome?

No. Superbuy states that third-party logistics and customs can create delays, taxes, damage, loss and other events it cannot fully control, although it aims to assist buyers.

What makes an after-sales request easy to review?

Provide one factual summary, parcel and order numbers, a numbered evidence packet, the exact quantity affected and one clearly requested remedy.
