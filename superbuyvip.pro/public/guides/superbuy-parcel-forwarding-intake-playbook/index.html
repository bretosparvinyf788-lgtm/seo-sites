Know where the forwarding service begins

Parcel forwarding is not the same transaction as a shopping-agent order. With a shopping-agent order, Superbuy places the domestic purchase and can communicate with the seller during that process. With forwarding, you buy the item yourself, submit its information for review, and arrange delivery to the warehouse address shown in your account. That shift changes who controls the seller relationship. Superbuy can receive, weigh, photograph and store the item, but it did not choose the seller, confirm your variation or negotiate the original sale. Build your evidence before the domestic parcel moves.

Choose the warehouse before paying the seller

Superbuy’s forwarding guide distinguishes its Shenzhen and Hong Kong warehouse routes. The warehouse choice affects which origin parcels can be accepted and where the international journey begins. Do not copy an address from an old screenshot, another buyer or a previous shipment. Open the forwarding function in your own account and use the current warehouse address displayed there. The address contains a user-specific identification element that helps the warehouse match arrivals. Copy the recipient, telephone number, address and identifier exactly, then save the version you sent to the seller.

Submit an intake record that can survive a label problem

Create the forwarding record before asking the seller to dispatch. Give each item an internal reference, a plain product name, exact quantity, declared purchase value, category, seller order number and expected domestic tracking number. Add a neutral identifying description such as black cotton jacket, size large, rather than a vague label such as clothes. Superbuy says submitted item information is reviewed before the parcel is sent to the warehouse. Treat approval as an intake checkpoint, not as proof that the product itself is acceptable for international transport.

Respect the domestic dispatch window

The official forwarding workflow tells buyers to send the parcel to the selected warehouse within the stated submission period and complete the domestic logistics information online before it expires. A forwarding order without a valid carrier and tracking number is difficult to match when the physical label is damaged or the seller combines packages. Record the carrier name, tracking number, dispatch date, seller, number of cartons and the items expected inside each carton. If a seller changes the tracking number, update your record rather than leaving two conflicting numbers.

Separate arrival confirmation from product verification

Warehouse receipt proves that a parcel arrived; it does not prove that the seller sent the right variation or that an internal product works. Superbuy’s public guidance describes weighing, warehousing and photographs, while also warning that warehouse checks are not professional product inspection. Use the standard photographs to confirm visible identity, color, count where visible, exterior condition and obvious discrepancies. Do not treat them as evidence of battery health, electronic performance, material composition, authenticity, waterproofing or hidden construction.

Build a three-layer evidence chain

Your first layer is pre-dispatch evidence: seller order, paid amount, variation text, listing screenshot and seller promises. The second layer is transit evidence: domestic carrier, tracking events, carton count and delivery confirmation. The third layer is warehouse evidence: stored-item identifier, measured weight, photographs, issue message and your decision. Link all three layers in one row of your spreadsheet. When a parcel is lighter than expected or a variation looks wrong, this chain lets you ask a narrow question instead of sending an unsupported complaint.

Reconcile weight before consolidation

Compare the seller’s expected item weight, the domestic carrier’s parcel weight when available and the Superbuy warehouse measurement. Small differences can come from packaging or scale methods. A large unexplained difference deserves review before the item is mixed into an international parcel. Record whether the warehouse weight represents one product, a sealed carton or multiple combined items. If the item is unusually bulky, save its visible packaging dimensions or request evidence that will affect your international shipping decision.

Treat unmatched and combined arrivals as exceptions

Sellers sometimes split one order across several tracking numbers or combine several orders into one carton. Both patterns can confuse a warehouse intake record. Maintain a carton map: seller order, expected SKU, quantity and tracking number for every domestic parcel. When one carton contains several items, state that relationship in advance when the forwarding form allows it. When an arrival remains unmatched, provide the exact warehouse identifier and domestic delivery evidence. Avoid creating duplicate forwarding records unless support confirms that a new record is required.

Decide whether packaging should remain

Forwarding buyers control the purchase but may not control the seller’s packing quality. Retail boxes can protect rigid or collectible items, yet they can also increase international volumetric weight. Before consolidation, decide which packaging carries product value, which protects against a defined hazard and which is disposable shipping material. Write precise requests: retain retail box, remove external courier bag, protect corners, or keep factory seals intact. A broad instruction such as remove all packaging can destroy evidence or reduce protection before the international leg.

Screen transport restrictions before the item arrives

A successful domestic delivery does not guarantee an eligible international line. Batteries, liquids, powders, magnets, compressed products, food, cosmetics, medicines, oversized goods and branded items may narrow route availability. Use Superbuy’s shipping estimator and current restriction information as early signals, then verify the actual routes shown after warehousing. Forwarding transfers more product-selection responsibility to you, so do not rely on warehouse arrival to solve a restriction that was visible before purchase.

Keep storage and dispute clocks separate

Superbuy currently describes a ninety-day free-storage period, but that is a warehouse benefit, not an extension of the seller’s return policy. Your independent seller may impose a much shorter reporting or return window. Review photographs and weight as soon as the item is stored. If something is wrong, preserve the seller conversation, contact the seller under the marketplace rules and ask Superbuy what domestic return action is operationally possible. Waiting because free storage remains can eliminate the only practical remedy.

Compare consolidation plans with real constraints

Consolidation can reduce repeated first-weight and handling charges, but it can also create a larger dimensional parcel or mix items with incompatible route restrictions. Compare at least two parcel plans after all intended goods are stored: one consolidated parcel and a split plan based on sensitivity, value or size. Use the candidate route’s actual billing method, weight increments and restrictions. Do not choose one large parcel merely because consolidation is available. The best structure is the one that keeps cost, acceptance and loss exposure understandable.

Submit truthful parcel information

Superbuy’s forwarding and transport guidance asks buyers to provide truthful product and declared-value information. Build the declaration from your purchase records, not from an arbitrary target number copied from social media. Use recognizable item descriptions, defensible quantities and values consistent with the contents. Customs rules, taxes and inspection practices depend on the destination and can change. An agent can provide route options and operational assistance, but it cannot turn inaccurate information into a compliant declaration.

Close the forwarding record after delivery

After the international parcel is dispatched, save the final packed weight, dimensions, route, deposit, adjustment, tracking number and declared contents. After receipt, record transit time, packaging condition, product condition and any missing pieces. Compare the final result with your original assumptions. This turns forwarding from a sequence of screenshots into a repeatable control system. The next order should inherit proven warehouse instructions and route observations, while failed assumptions are retired instead of repeated.

Superbuy Parcel Forwarding: 12 Practical Questions

Is parcel forwarding the same as Superbuy’s shopping-agent service?

No. In forwarding, you purchase and pay the seller yourself, then use Superbuy for intake, storage and international delivery. Seller selection and the original transaction remain your responsibility.

Which Superbuy warehouse address should I use?

Use the current address displayed inside your own forwarding workflow. Select the appropriate Shenzhen or Hong Kong option for the parcel’s origin and destination, and copy the user-specific identifier exactly.

When should I create the forwarding record?

Create and submit it before the seller dispatches. Wait for the item-information review, then provide the domestic carrier and tracking number within the current submission window.

What evidence should I save before domestic shipment?

Save the paid order, exact variation, quantity, product images, seller promises, warehouse address version and expected carton count. These records help reconcile an incorrect or unmatched arrival.

Do warehouse photos prove that an item works?

No. Photos can support visible identity and condition checks, but they cannot establish electronic performance, authenticity, internal construction, material composition or long-term durability.

What if one seller order has several tracking numbers?

Map every tracking number to its expected items and carton count. Update the forwarding record or contact support with the exact mapping so each arrival can be matched correctly.

What if several orders arrive in one carton?

Record the combined relationship before arrival when possible. After storage, compare the warehouse item identifiers, photographs, count and weight with your carton map before consolidation.

Why compare the seller weight with the warehouse weight?

A material difference may indicate missing pieces, combined goods, unusual packaging or incorrect expectations. Resolve unexplained differences before the item enters an international parcel.

Does ninety-day free storage extend the seller’s return period?

No. Warehouse storage and seller after-sales are separate clocks. Review the item immediately because the seller’s dispute or return window may close much earlier.

Should I remove every retail box to lower shipping cost?

No. Remove packaging only after balancing dimensional savings against protection, identification, resale or collectible value. Keep instructions specific and measurable.

Can every item received by the warehouse be shipped internationally?

No. Route eligibility depends on product attributes, destination and current carrier rules. Check likely restrictions before purchase and verify the routes offered after storage.

What should I record after the parcel is delivered?

Record final weight, dimensions, route, shipping adjustment, declaration, transit time, packaging condition and item outcome. Use the result to improve future forwarding decisions.
