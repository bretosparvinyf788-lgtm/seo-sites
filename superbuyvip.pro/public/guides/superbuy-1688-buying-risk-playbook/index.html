Buying from 1688 Through Superbuy Without Guessing: An MOQ, Sampling, and After-Sales Risk Playbook

Treat 1688 as wholesale evidence, not a cheap catalogue

A 1688 listing can look familiar to anyone who has used a retail marketplace, but the buying logic is different. The lowest visible price may belong to a large quantity tier, a component, a sample, or one variation among several. Superbuy supports shopping-agent orders from 1688, yet the agent cannot turn an ambiguous wholesale offer into a clear retail promise after payment. The buyer must define the commercial unit first: what one price buys, how many units form the minimum order, whether colors may be mixed, and which specification is attached to each tier.

Build a specification fingerprint before paying

Create a compact fingerprint for every intended variation. Record the original Chinese option text, your translation, model, material claim, dimensions, color, quantity, packaging unit and one identifying image. Keep the original wording because two machine translations can make different factory terms look identical. If the listing combines finished products, accessories and replacement parts, state which one you require in the order remark. Superbuy's official shopping guidance says buyers select color, size and quantity or manually provide product information. Use that step to remove ambiguity. A warehouse photograph can later confirm a visible match, but it cannot reconstruct an option that was never clearly defined.

Audit the price ladder instead of copying the headline price

Wholesale pages frequently show several prices linked to quantity bands. Record each relevant tier and calculate the actual merchandise total at the quantity you intend to buy. Then add Chinese domestic delivery and any optional inspection or photography service. Superbuy's current fee explanation lists standard purchasing from mainstream platforms including 1688 without a purchasing service fee, but that does not make the displayed unit price the landed cost. Domestic freight, return freight, unusable extras, international shipping and corrective work remain real. Compare orders by usable delivered unit, not by the smallest advertised figure. A ten-piece minimum is not a bargain when only six pieces meet the required specification.

Confirm the MOQ as a rule, not a suggestion

Minimum order quantity can apply to one SKU, one color, one size, a mixed batch or the entire listing. These are not interchangeable. Write a simple equation in your notes: required quantity equals units per SKU multiplied by the number of SKUs, unless the seller explicitly allows mixing. If the answer is not visible, ask through the order enquiry before purchase. Superbuy's 1688 guidance warns that orders failing a supplier's minimum quantity may be judged invalid and need to be placed again. That creates delay and can also change price or stock. Never assume that meeting the total piece count satisfies a per-variation minimum.

Read seller service marks before choosing the product

Superbuy's public 1688 guidance separates sellers and products according to the service marks shown on the 1688 description or store page. The published guide describes stronger support for listings carrying specified guarantee or return-related marks and much narrower support for unmarked products. Save the marks visible at the time of purchase and the stated exchange period. Do not convert a mark into a universal guarantee: seller rules still govern the negotiation, and the marks can differ by product. The useful decision is comparative. When two factories offer similar goods, clearer after-sales terms and verifiable specifications can be worth more than a small unit-price saving.

Understand what sampling can and cannot prove

Sampling is a batch-control tool, not a promise that every unit is correct. Superbuy's current 1688 page describes sampling for qualifying products at five percent of a single SKU, with a minimum of five and a maximum of twenty sampled pieces. That can reveal repeated visible defects, wrong colors or a systematic mismatch, but it may miss a rare defect outside the sample. It also does not establish durability, chemical composition, electrical safety or authenticity. Before ordering, decide which defects sampling can actually expose, what defect rate you can tolerate and whether the value of the batch justifies a separate inspection request. Otherwise, the word inspection may create more confidence than evidence.

Do not confuse parcel count with item count

Quantity checking becomes difficult when goods arrive sealed, bundled or packed in opaque factory cartons. Superbuy's 1688 guidance says the warehouse may not unpack original packaging to count internal pieces when the quantity is not stated on the outside, and it also warns about limits on detailed quantity confirmation. Therefore, ask the seller to label cartons by SKU and quantity before domestic dispatch when the order matters. Request photographs of external labels, package count and any visible quantity information. If you need every piece opened and checked, determine whether that service is available before buying. A photograph of three closed cartons proves three cartons arrived, not that every promised unit is inside.

Design QC around the failure modes of a batch

A retail-style note such as check quality is especially weak for wholesale goods. Divide the inspection into identity, count, consistency and condition. Identity confirms the exact model and variation. Count confirms what can reasonably be counted. Consistency compares color, dimensions, print placement or construction across sampled units. Condition checks visible damage, contamination and packaging failure. For clothing, request one measured sample per relevant size and compare it with the seller chart. For hardware, confirm component count, finish and obvious deformation. For electronics, remember that visible inspection is not performance certification. Each requested image should answer a decision: accept, ask, replace, return or split the batch.

Calculate the cost of a failed batch before ordering

Wholesale savings magnify both success and error. Build a failure budget with four lines: merchandise at risk, Chinese return freight, non-refundable service cost and the international cost if the problem is accepted and shipped anyway. Add the cost of unusable units instead of quietly averaging them away. If twenty items cost less per piece but a ten-piece order limits the plausible loss, the smaller tier may be economically stronger. This is especially important when seller terms are limited or the product has a high variation risk. The correct order size is the one whose downside you can absorb while still learning enough to make the next order better.

Act while the seller's after-sales clock is open

Warehouse storage and seller after-sales are separate clocks. Superbuy currently publishes a ninety-day free-storage period, but that does not mean a 1688 seller will accept a return ninety days later. Review warehouse evidence as soon as it appears, record the discrepancy and send one precise enquiry with the order number, affected SKU, quantity and requested remedy. Superbuy's 1688 guidance states that shopping agents can help communicate with qualifying sellers, while the actual negotiation result follows the seller's policy and the buyer may bear return or exchange shipping. Delay weakens a case even when the goods can remain in the warehouse for weeks.

Plan international shipping at the SKU stage

Wholesale quantity changes parcel architecture. Ten soft shirts may consolidate efficiently, while ten rigid boxes or battery-powered items can create route limits, dimensional weight and concentrated value. Superbuy's shipping process asks the buyer to choose among recommended routes after warehouse arrival and warns that some items are restricted on certain lines. Classify every SKU before payment as ordinary, fragile, rigid, liquid, powder, battery-related or otherwise sensitive. Estimate whether cartons can be removed without losing identification or protection. A low factory price is not useful when one characteristic forces the entire batch onto a route whose cost or restrictions erase the margin.

Use a five-gate release decision

Release a 1688 order to international shipping only after five gates are closed. Gate one: the received variation matches the saved fingerprint. Gate two: the quantity evidence is adequate for the packaging format. Gate three: sampled condition meets the written acceptance rule. Gate four: seller disputes or replacements are resolved or consciously waived. Gate five: the batch fits a realistic shipping route and loss limit. Mark each gate pass, hold or fail. This prevents a common mistake: submitting the parcel because the products are already in storage. Warehouse arrival is not the finish line. It is the last practical decision point before domestic uncertainty becomes international cost.

Twelve practical questions about buying from 1688 through Superbuy

Does Superbuy charge a standard purchasing service fee for 1688 orders?

Superbuy's current fee page lists standard purchasing from 1688 without a purchasing service fee. Product cost, domestic delivery, optional services, returns and international shipping still apply.

Is the lowest price shown on a 1688 listing always available for one piece?

No. It may belong to a quantity tier, component or specific variation. Confirm the unit, tier and MOQ before submitting the order.

Can different colors be mixed to meet an MOQ?

Only when the seller's rule permits it. Do not assume a total quantity satisfies a minimum that applies separately to each SKU or color.

What should I save from the 1688 page?

Save the original option text, translated meaning, tier prices, MOQ, service marks, specification images, seller terms and the date checked.

Does Superbuy inspect every unit in a wholesale batch?

Not by default. Superbuy's public 1688 guidance describes limited sampling for qualifying products, and service scope depends on the product and seller marks.

What does a five-percent sample tell me?

It can reveal repeated visible problems within the sampled pieces. It cannot guarantee every unexamined unit or prove hidden performance, composition or durability.

Will the warehouse open sealed factory cartons to count everything?

Not necessarily. Superbuy warns that original packaging may not be opened for detailed quantity confirmation when internal counts are not shown.

Who pays domestic return or exchange shipping?

Superbuy's 1688 guidance says the buyer may bear the shipping cost generated by a return or exchange, while the seller's rules determine the negotiation outcome.

Is the ninety-day free-storage period also the seller's return window?

No. Warehouse storage and seller after-sales are separate. Review arrivals and raise discrepancies promptly.

Are products without guarantee or return marks automatically unusable?

No, but the available inspection and after-sales support may be narrower. Price the additional uncertainty before buying.

Should I order the largest quantity to get the best unit price?

Only when the usable yield, downside risk, shipping structure and demand justify it. A lower unit price can create a larger total loss.

What is the final decision before international shipping?

Confirm specification, count evidence, sampled condition, resolved after-sales issues, route eligibility and an acceptable loss limit before submitting the batch.
