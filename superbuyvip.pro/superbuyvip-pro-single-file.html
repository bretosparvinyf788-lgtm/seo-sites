SUPERBUY QC SYSTEM · 1,768 WORDS

The Warehouse Is a Decision Gate: A Superbuy QC System That Separates Visible Evidence from Unresolved Risk

A practical inspection method for turning Superbuy warehouse photos into evidence, not reassurance, before an international parcel becomes difficult to reverse.

Start before the parcel exists

Most Superbuy quality-control mistakes begin long before a warehouse employee takes a photograph. A buyer sees an attractive listing, chooses a size from a letter, pays, and assumes the warehouse will later decide whether the item is good. That expectation gives the inspection stage a job it cannot perform. The more useful approach is to treat warehouse arrival as a decision gate inside a longer evidence chain. Before payment, preserve the exact listing, selected variation, seller size chart, stated material, quantity, price and the few images that define the product. Add a short order remark for any detail that would make the purchase unacceptable. When the item arrives, the photographs can then be compared with a stable reference instead of a page that may already have changed.

Understand what Superbuy actually does

Superbuy's current user guidance describes a sequence in which a buyer submits an item, pays for the product and Chinese domestic delivery, and the shopping agent contacts the seller to make the purchase. After the product reaches the warehouse, Superbuy says it carries out quality inspection, stores the item and provides photographs for the buyer to view. That sequence is useful because it creates a pause between domestic acquisition and international shipment. It is not the same as independent laboratory testing, authentication or a durability guarantee. The warehouse can document visible condition and obvious inconsistencies. The buyer still owns the judgment about whether the photographed evidence is adequate for the money, shipping cost and remaining uncertainty.

Build an inspection brief, not a vague request

A strong inspection brief is short enough to execute and precise enough to answer. For a T-shirt, write the selected color and request chest width, garment length, collar shape and print position. For shoes, record the tagged size and ask for both shoes in one frame, outsole length, heel alignment and close views of problem-prone panels. For a bag, specify external dimensions, strap attachment, zipper operation and interior layout. Avoid requests such as 'check quality' because quality contains too many hidden meanings. A photograph cannot answer whether glue will fail after six months, but it can show a lifted edge today. Convert every concern into something visible, measurable, countable or comparable before submitting the order.

Use a fixed four-pass review

Review every warehouse arrival in the same order. Pass one is identity: confirm item type, color, marked size, quantity and selected variation. Pass two is geometry: compare left and right shoes, panel heights, print centering, pocket placement and garment proportions. Pass three is construction: inspect exposed stitching, hardware, surface damage, loose threads, stains, chipped finishes and crushed packaging. Pass four is logistics: identify rigid boxes, glass, batteries, liquids, magnets or other characteristics that may change route eligibility or packed volume. This sequence prevents an attractive close-up from distracting you from the wrong color, and it connects product approval with the later parcel decision rather than treating inspection and shipping as unrelated tasks.

Measure clothing as an object, not a label

Size labels are weak evidence because sellers, factories and regions do not share one dependable standard. A buyer who normally wears medium may receive a garment marked large that is still too narrow. Numeric measurements are more portable. Lay a similar garment you already own flat and record chest width, shoulder width, sleeve length, garment length, waist, rise and inseam as relevant. Compare those figures with the seller chart before payment and with warehouse measurements after arrival. Allow for measurement method and fabric behavior instead of demanding false precision. The useful question is not whether the label matches your usual label; it is whether the physical dimensions fall inside a range you know you can wear.

Inspect shoes as a pair

Single-shoe glamour photographs hide the defects that matter most. Ask for both shoes at the same angle and compare toe shape, panel height, eyelet placement, heel tabs and outsole length. Then inspect each shoe for adhesive marks, creasing, scratches and stitching interruptions. Confirm the marked size and request an outsole or insole measurement when fit risk is high. Remember that a photo cannot establish cushioning life, internal support or long-term bonding. Those remain unresolved risks. If a pair is inexpensive but expensive to ship, small asymmetries may still be unacceptable because international postage makes a disappointing product costlier to correct than it looked at checkout.

Separate visible defects from invisible claims

Create two columns in your notes: proven and unresolved. A clear image may prove color, quantity, gross dimensions, visible alignment and current surface condition. It does not prove exact fiber composition, waterproof performance, battery health, electrical safety, odor, authenticity or future durability. This distinction protects you from two opposite errors. The first is rejecting a good item because the camera cannot prove an invisible characteristic. The second is accepting an uncertain item because the photographs look clean. When a critical purchase depends on an invisible claim, use seller history, return conditions, price tolerance and the maximum loss you are willing to accept. Do not ask warehouse photography to carry evidence it cannot produce.

Account for marketplace differences

Superbuy's guidance specifically notes that products purchased from 1688 use a different set of quality-control standards. That warning matters because marketplace structure changes what information may be available and how variations are presented. A wholesale listing can combine several models, materials or minimum quantities on one page. A low displayed price may refer to the smallest component or a volume tier rather than the finished item you imagined. Preserve screenshots of the chosen variation, translate unit and quantity language, and state the required configuration in the order remark. If the listing remains ambiguous after translation, pause. Paying first and hoping the warehouse will interpret a wholesale page later is not a controlled buying method.

Make the 90-day window useful

Superbuy currently states that warehoused items receive 90 days of free storage. Treat that period as working time, not permission to forget an item. Record the warehouse arrival date, last free-storage date, inspection status and next action. Group items by route compatibility and urgency rather than waiting for an arbitrary number of purchases. A soft clothing order may wait for another garment, while a fragile or restricted item may need a separate plan. Review the warehouse queue weekly. Storage is valuable because it allows consolidation and evidence gathering, but delayed decisions can shrink seller return options even while the warehouse still holds the product.

Decide with four outcomes

Every inspected item should end with one of four outcomes: pass, ask, exchange or return. Pass means visible evidence meets the prewritten acceptance criteria. Ask means one answer or photograph could resolve the uncertainty. Exchange means the item is wrong or defective but the seller relationship and timing make replacement reasonable. Return means the mismatch, risk or likely landed cost no longer supports the purchase. Add one sentence explaining the decision. This small discipline prevents sunk-cost thinking. The product price has already been paid, but international shipping has not. Warehouse arrival is therefore the last strong point at which a buyer can compare the item with the original intention before adding another layer of irreversible cost.

Write questions that can be answered

A useful enquiry includes the order number, the exact discrepancy and the evidence requested. Instead of writing 'this looks bad,' write 'the listing shows a centered chest print; the warehouse image appears shifted left; please provide one front photo with the garment laid flat and confirm chest width.' Superbuy's guidance says buyers can use order enquiry during purchasing and that agents may contact them by email or message when information is incomplete. Use that channel as a record, not as a conversation full of assumptions. One precise message is easier for an agent to forward to a seller and easier for you to evaluate later than five emotional messages describing the same uncertainty.

Connect QC to parcel architecture

Product approval should include a shipping consequence check. A shoe box may protect the item but create volumetric weight. A heavy metal accessory may make a clothing parcel inefficient. A liquid, battery or fragile component can reduce the available routes. Decide whether original packaging is essential, removable or replaceable before parcel submission. Superbuy's published process lists package removal, reinforcement and insurance among optional parcel services that can be requested. The correct choice depends on the item, not on a universal packing hack. Removing a decorative box may save volume; removing structural protection from a fragile object may create a larger loss than the freight saving.

Keep a post-delivery feedback loop

The warehouse decision is not the end of the system. After delivery, record whether the sizing estimate worked, whether the material matched the listing impression, whether the visible construction survived use, and whether the packaging choice was sensible. Compare those outcomes with the warehouse evidence. If a defect was visible but missed, improve the inspection checklist. If a problem was invisible, label it correctly rather than blaming the photographs. Over time, your spreadsheet becomes a calibrated record of sellers, product types and risk patterns. That is more valuable than a large collection of links because it converts purchases into knowledge that can change future decisions.

Warehouse QC questions buyers actually need answered

01What can Superbuy warehouse photos reliably verify?+

Visible identity, color, quantity, obvious damage, approximate measurements and surface alignment.

02What can warehouse photos not prove?+

Authenticity, composition, odor, internal construction, electrical safety, waterproofing or durability.

03When is an extra photo worth requesting?+

When one angle or measurement can change the accept, exchange or return decision.

04How should clothing measurements be checked?+

Compare the same flat measurement with a garment you already wear comfortably.

05Why should shoes be photographed as a pair?+

Paired views expose mismatched toes, panels, heels, eyelets and sole lengths.

06Does 90-day free storage define the return deadline?+

No. Storage time and the seller's return window are separate deadlines.

07How should a buyer handle an unclear 1688 variant?+

Preserve original option text, quantity, unit and configuration; pause unresolved orders.

08Should every small cosmetic flaw cause a return?+

No. Compare it with acceptance limits, replacement feasibility and total correction cost.

09Why does packaging belong in the QC decision?+

Boxes and restricted components can change routes, volumetric weight and protection needs.

10What is the most useful final QC record?+

A dated variant, evidence, unresolved risk, decision, reason and next deadline.

LANDED COST METHOD · 1,758 WORDS

Why the Cheapest Listing Can Produce the Most Expensive Parcel: A Landed-Cost Framework for Superbuy Orders

A cost-control method that connects product price, Chinese domestic delivery, packed dimensions, route eligibility and the final verified international charge.

The listing price is only the first number

A low marketplace price can be real and still produce a poor purchase. International buyers often compare two listings by product price while ignoring the shape of the item, domestic delivery, packaging, route restrictions and correction cost. Superbuy's published buying flow separates payment into stages: the buyer first pays for the product and Chinese local delivery, then later pays an international shipping deposit when submitting a parcel. That structure should change how you keep records. Do not call the first payment the total. Create a landed-cost worksheet before purchase, update it at warehouse arrival, and close it only after the final parcel charge is known. The goal is not perfect prediction; it is avoiding decisions made with an incomplete denominator.

Use three cost states

Maintain three columns for every item: known, estimated and contingent. Known costs include the paid product price, domestic delivery and any selected service already charged. Estimated costs include the item's share of international freight and packaging based on current weight and dimensions. Contingent costs cover risks such as a return to the seller, exchange delivery, separate parcel, reinforcement or customs-related payment. This structure is better than one optimistic total because uncertainty stays visible. When comparing two products, compare their estimated landed ranges and the amount at risk, not only the number displayed on the marketplace. A cheaper rigid item may consume more parcel volume than a slightly more expensive soft item and therefore create a higher final cost.

Understand the deposit and final charge

Superbuy's current user guidance says the international shipping deposit is calculated from the product's estimated weight, selected shipment method and destination. It also says the final shipping fee is calculated from the package size and weight verified by the shipping company, with any difference from the deposit returned to the user's Superbuy account after shipment. The practical lesson is that the deposit is not a promise of final cost. Treat it as a temporary calculation made before the carrier verifies the parcel. Save the submitted weight, dimensions, route and deposit; then compare them with the final verified figures. That difference teaches you more about future estimates than a generic claim that one route is always cheap.

Learn dimensional weight without worshipping one formula

Superbuy's shipping calculator explains that most air cargo may use dimensional weight calculated as length in centimeters multiplied by width and height, divided by 6,000. The formula reveals why a light shoe box can cost more than a dense clothing bundle. A forty-centimeter cube has a dimensional reference above ten kilograms even if its scale weight is much lower. Use the formula as an early warning, not an official quotation. Routes can apply different divisors, billing increments, limits and category rules. The parcel's final packed measurements matter, and the selected route decides how those measurements are charged. Estimate both actual and dimensional weight, then plan around the larger exposure until the live route shows otherwise.

Measure the package you expect, not the product alone

A product photograph rarely shows the shipping geometry. Shoes may arrive inside retail boxes and domestic cartons. Bags can contain stuffing. Jackets may be folded loosely. Fragile goods may need reinforcement that increases all three dimensions. Estimate a product's packed form at the buying stage: soft and compressible, rigid and stackable, irregular, fragile, or restricted. At warehouse arrival, update the estimate using visible packaging and available measurements. If a box is not essential, decide whether package removal is appropriate. If it protects shape or resale condition, keep it and price the volume honestly. Shipping optimization is not automatically the smallest possible parcel; it is the lowest sensible cost that preserves the value you actually care about.

Respect first-weight units and limits

The Superbuy calculator explains that the first weight unit is the minimum weight charged for a parcel, with additional weight charged after that unit. This means several tiny parcels may repeatedly pay base increments, while a consolidated parcel can use those increments more efficiently. Consolidation is not unlimited, however. The same official calculator notes that routes have their own weight limits and an overweight parcel may need items removed and resubmitted. The right parcel is therefore a compatibility cluster, not simply everything in the warehouse. Group items whose category, urgency, fragility and size work well together. Split when one item forces a poor route, exceeds a limit or exposes the rest of the parcel to unnecessary risk.

Route eligibility is part of product cost

An item that cannot use ordinary routes has a hidden price premium. Superbuy's calculator asks buyers to identify categories such as textiles and leather, liquids and powders, digital or electrical goods, food or medicine, batteries and other special items. It also publishes a prohibited-items notice naming examples such as perfume, simulation guns, banknotes, antiques, weapons, knives, gunpowder, drugs, lighters and high-risk chemicals. Do not treat the category selector as paperwork after purchase. Check route eligibility before paying. A product that requires a narrow or expensive route may be unattractive even when the listing price is excellent. If eligibility is uncertain, use the live calculator or enquiry channel before the item reaches the warehouse.

Avoid universal consolidation advice

Consolidation can reduce repeated base charges, but combining everything can create a route-quality penalty. Imagine six shirts, one power bank and a glass accessory. The shirts may qualify for several textile-friendly routes, while the battery and glass introduce different restrictions and packing needs. A single parcel may lose the best clothing route or require heavier reinforcement. Compare at least two architectures: one mixed parcel and two compatibility-based parcels. Record total deposit, expected billing weight, route characteristics and value concentration. Sometimes one parcel wins; sometimes separation is cheaper or safer. The point is to test the structure with current route data rather than repeating the slogan that consolidation always saves money.

Treat value-added packing as an engineering choice

Superbuy's user guidance mentions services such as package removal, reinforcement and insurance during parcel submission. Each changes the risk-cost balance. Package removal may reduce dimensional weight but can expose shoes or collectibles to deformation. Reinforcement adds material and may increase weight, yet it can be rational for fragile or high-value contents. Insurance has value only when its coverage, exclusions and claim requirements fit the route and item. Write the purpose of each service in your parcel plan. If you cannot explain which failure it prevents, reconsider it. Good packing is not the maximum number of options; it is a design matched to the contents, route handling and loss you could tolerate.

Build a landed-cost allocation rule

After shipment, allocate shared freight back to individual items so future product decisions improve. A simple weight-only split is easy but unfair to bulky light goods. A dimensional-only split can overcharge dense items. Use the larger of actual and estimated dimensional contribution when data is available, or assign products to bands such as soft-small, rigid-medium and oversized. Add direct services to the item that caused them. The allocation need not reproduce the carrier invoice perfectly; it needs to explain which purchases consumed parcel capacity. Without allocation, a ten-dollar item can look like a bargain forever while quietly absorbing twenty dollars of freight in every mixed parcel.

Include failure cost

Landed cost should include the expected cost of being wrong. A garment with uncertain measurements may require an exchange and another domestic delivery. A fragile item may arrive damaged despite added packing. A restricted product may need a separate route or return. Do not invent a dramatic worst-case number for every order. Instead, multiply plausible loss by the likelihood you assign after reviewing the seller, product type and evidence. Even a rough risk allowance improves comparisons. If two listings appear equal but one has clear measurements, simple packaging and broad route eligibility, its lower uncertainty can justify a higher product price. Rational buying values information because information reduces expensive corrections.

Use a parcel submission checklist

Before paying the international deposit, confirm destination, address format, selected items, declared category, route restrictions, packed-weight estimate, dimensional exposure, packaging services, insurance decision and customs responsibility. Save the route name and the conditions shown at that moment because route availability can change. Verify that every item has a deliberate reason to be in that parcel. Then record the deposit separately from the final carrier-verified charge. After dispatch, track the parcel through the account. Superbuy's guidance also warns that international shipments can face delay, tax, damage, loss, confiscation and other events outside the platform's direct control. A complete cost plan must therefore include time and risk, not just the cheapest visible freight line.

Review after arrival

When the parcel arrives, close the worksheet with final shipping, any refund difference, customs-related cost, damage and delivery time. Compare expected and actual cost by item and parcel. Ask why the estimate missed: inaccurate warehouse weight, packaging retained, dimensional billing, route change, minimum increment or a service added later. Preserve that explanation. Three or four completed parcels will produce a personal model based on the countries, product types and packing choices you actually use. That model is more valuable than public screenshots of somebody else's freight because their destination, categories, timing and route conditions may be different.

Landed-cost and parcel-planning questions

01Is the international shipping deposit the final charge?+

No. Carrier-confirmed weight and dimensions determine the final charge and adjustment.

02Does the 6,000 volumetric divisor apply to every route?+

No. Live routes may use another divisor, rounding rule or minimum increment.

03Should buyers always remove shoe boxes?+

Only when savings outweigh lost structure, protection, presentation or collector value.

04Is one consolidated parcel always cheaper than two?+

No. Mixed parcels can lose routes, exceed limits or concentrate excessive value.

05What is the first-weight charge?+

The route's minimum starting block, repeated across several very small parcels.

06How should shared shipping cost be allocated?+

Use actual or dimensional contribution, then assign item-specific services directly.

07When should restricted-goods eligibility be checked?+

Before payment, because restricted categories can sharply reduce available routes.

08What makes a packaging service financially sensible?+

It should reduce a named failure risk at a proportionate cost.

09Why include customs and delay in the cost model?+

Landed cost includes taxes, time loss, damage exposure and correction costs.

10Which number should close the parcel record?+

Final confirmed freight plus customs, direct services, damage and delivery time.

SPREADSHEET OPERATING SYSTEM · 1,774 WORDS

How to Build a Superbuy Spreadsheet That Survives Listing Changes, Warehouse Delays and Route Restrictions

A durable research system for preserving seller evidence, controlling warehouse decisions and learning from the final delivered parcel instead of collecting fragile links.

Stop treating a spreadsheet as a link dump

A long list of product URLs can look impressive while providing almost no buying control. Marketplace pages change, variants disappear, prices move and sellers reuse listings. A durable Superbuy spreadsheet should preserve the state of a buying decision, not merely the address where that decision once began. Each row needs enough evidence to answer five questions later: what was selected, what was promised, what was paid, what arrived, and what happened after international delivery. The spreadsheet becomes an operating system that connects research, order remarks, warehouse inspection, parcel architecture and post-delivery learning. Ten thoroughly documented rows can outperform ten thousand unverified links because each row remains useful when the original page no longer tells the same story.

Design one row around decisions

Start with stable fields: internal item ID, source marketplace, seller name, captured product title, selected variation, quantity, product price, Chinese domestic delivery, order date and source URL. Add evidence fields for material claim, numeric measurements, saved image reference and the exact order remark. Then add operational fields: order status, warehouse arrival date, free-storage deadline, QC outcome, actual or estimated weight, packed-dimension risk, route category and parcel ID. Finish with result fields: final allocated freight, customs-related cost, delivery date, sizing result, condition result and seller reliability note. The row should tell a complete story without requiring your memory or a still-unchanged listing.

Capture before payment

Superbuy's current guidance says buyers can paste a product link from supported marketplaces or search for a product, and that its system can capture information for most products on Taobao, Tmall and JD.com. It also describes a manual shopping-agent order when information cannot be captured normally. Automated capture is convenient, but convenience does not freeze the seller page. Before paying, save the chosen color, size, quantity, price, size chart, material statement and decisive listing images. Record the destination country and any requirement that belongs in the order remark. A screenshot without the selected variation is weak evidence; a precise record of the variation with the relevant chart is much stronger.

Give every row a confidence state

Use a visible status such as researched, ordered, purchased, in domestic transit, warehoused, question pending, approved, parcel submitted, dispatched, delivered or closed. Add a confidence label: verified, partially verified, seller-dependent or outdated. These labels prevent an old spreadsheet row from masquerading as a current recommendation. The status should change only when new evidence arrives. A live URL does not automatically make a row verified, and a warehouse photograph does not verify every material claim. This explicit uncertainty is a feature, not an embarrassment. Buyers make better decisions when the spreadsheet shows what is unknown instead of converting uncertainty into confident marketing language.

Create a variation fingerprint

Marketplace listings often combine many products under one title. Build a variation fingerprint from the attributes that uniquely identify your intended item: model name, color, size, material, version, quantity, seller option text and one identifying image. Paste that fingerprint into the order note in a concise form. If the option uses a translated nickname or number, preserve the original characters alongside your English interpretation. When the warehouse item appears, compare it with the fingerprint before looking at cosmetic quality. This prevents a common failure in which a buyer carefully inspects a well-made product that is nevertheless the wrong version. Identity comes before quality because a perfect wrong item is still wrong.

Record communication as evidence

Superbuy's guidance says buyers can use order enquiry during purchasing and that agents may contact them by email or message when more information is required. Add a communication log to the row or link it to a note. Record the date, question, agent or seller answer and resulting decision. Keep questions singular and verifiable. If an agent confirms that a seller cannot supply a measurement, label that field unknown rather than copying a speculative value from another listing. If the seller changes the price or domestic delivery, update the known-cost column and preserve the earlier quote. A clean communication trail reduces repeated enquiries and makes after-sales discussion more concrete.

Use warehouse arrival as a workflow trigger

The arrival date should trigger a checklist, not a passive status change. Superbuy states that warehoused items undergo inspection and photographs are made available, with 90 days of free storage. On arrival, verify identity, inspect visible construction, compare numeric measurements, assess packaging geometry and assign pass, ask, exchange or return. Enter the free-storage deadline, but do not confuse it with a seller's return window. Set a next-action date within days, not months. If a product needs another photograph, measurement or seller response, the spreadsheet should surface that task until resolved. A warehouse queue without deadlines quietly converts reversible problems into expensive international deliveries.

Separate item readiness from parcel readiness

An item can pass QC and still be unready for a particular parcel. Add two independent flags. Item-ready means the product itself meets the acceptance criteria. Parcel-ready means its category, urgency, packaging, dimensions and risk fit a planned shipment. A battery product may be item-ready but incompatible with the best route for clothing. A shoe box may be approved for condition but still awaiting a decision about package removal. This separation stops the warehouse page from becoming the only planning tool. The spreadsheet can group compatible items and compare parcel scenarios before you reach the submission screen, when time pressure and sunk cost make poor choices more tempting.

Model route restrictions early

Superbuy's official calculator asks for an item category and warns that prohibited goods cannot be shipped. It distinguishes ordinary goods from textiles or leather, liquids and powders, digital or electrical items, foods or medicine and other special categories. Add a route-class field during product research, not after arrival. Use broad labels such as ordinary textile, built-in battery, liquid, fragile, branded, food or prohibited-check-required. The purpose is not to replace the live calculator; it is to expose items that need confirmation before payment. A spreadsheet row without route information can recommend a cheap product that later forces an expensive or unavailable shipping solution.

Add parcel scenario sheets

Create a parcel table separate from the product table. Each scenario should list included item IDs, total actual weight, estimated dimensions, dimensional reference, selected route, first-weight structure, weight limit, packing services, deposit and risk notes. Compare at least one alternative when a parcel contains mixed categories or rigid packaging. Superbuy's calculator publishes the common dimensional reference of length times width times height divided by 6,000 for most air cargo, but the live route remains authoritative. Scenario planning gives you a reasoned starting point; it should never be presented as an official quote. Save the submitted scenario so the difference between estimate and final carrier verification can improve the next one.

Protect the sheet from stale data

Add last-checked dates to prices, source pages, shipping assumptions and policy notes. Use conditional formatting to flag products not rechecked within your chosen interval. Do not silently overwrite historical prices; store a current value and a short change log when the difference matters. If a listing redirects, changes category or replaces its images, mark it changed and require new review. Remove rows only when you are sure their history has no learning value. An archived failed purchase can be more instructive than a successful link because it reveals which evidence, seller behavior or route assumption caused the problem.

Measure seller and product outcomes separately

After delivery, score the seller on description accuracy, variation accuracy, dispatch behavior, response quality and willingness to resolve problems. Score the product on fit, material impression, construction, packaging condition and value at the final landed cost. Do not collapse those into one star rating. A reliable seller can supply a product whose design does not suit you, while an acceptable product can arrive despite poor seller communication. Separate scores make future filters more useful. Add a note about what the warehouse photos correctly predicted and what remained invisible. That distinction improves both seller selection and QC requests.

Publish responsibly

If the spreadsheet is public, distinguish current research from personal outcomes and unverified submissions. Show the last review date, explain that prices and route rules change, and avoid claiming that photographs prove authenticity or long-term quality. Do not present a product as safe to ship merely because it once used a particular route. The live destination, item category and current carrier conditions still matter. A public spreadsheet earns trust through transparent limits: exact product source, clear date, practical QC note and honest uncertainty. Search traffic may arrive through broad phrases such as Superbuy spreadsheet or Superbuy finds, but usefulness depends on whether the page helps a buyer make a better decision today.

Run a weekly operating review

Once a week, filter the spreadsheet for unanswered enquiries, warehouse arrivals without decisions, approaching storage deadlines, item-ready products without parcel plans, parcels in transit and delivered items without outcome notes. Close completed rows and write one lesson from every finished parcel. This routine keeps the sheet alive without requiring constant attention. It also creates natural editorial material: sizing errors, packaging tradeoffs, route compatibility and QC evidence can become original buyer guides based on observed decisions rather than generic summaries. The spreadsheet and the articles then reinforce each other—one stores structured evidence, while the other explains the reasoning patterns behind it.
