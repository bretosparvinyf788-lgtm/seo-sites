Kakobuy QC Photos: Standard Inspection and ¥2 Extra Photos

How to distinguish included item inspection, paid personalized photos and free parcel photographs—and how to ask for evidence that actually supports a decision.

September 22, 2026Quality inspectionAbout 1,550 words

QC photos are most useful when you know exactly what question each image is supposed to answer. A broad front view may confirm color and overall shape. It may not show a label, a measurement, a small defect or whether the ordered variation matches the seller’s description. The difference between “photos exist” and “the photos answer my decision” is the difference between a decorative gallery and a useful inspection record.

Kakobuy’s public help pages describe several photo-related services. They are easy to confuse because buyers often call every warehouse image “QC.” In practice, standard product photos, personalized photo requests and parcel photographs have different purposes and different fee rules. This guide separates them and explains what a careful buyer can reasonably infer from each.

Three types of photos, three different jobs

Standard item photos

Kakobuy states that a Standard Photo Service is included when the applicable Quality Inspection option is selected. The public rule describes inspection around style, quantity, color, size, model and obvious visible problems. It does not publicly promise one fixed number of free item photos. That last detail matters because third-party pages sometimes claim a universal number without showing a current policy.

A standard set is designed to record the item as received at the warehouse. It is the first check for a wrong color, missing component, obvious stain, severe construction issue or visibly incorrect variation. The exact usefulness depends on framing, resolution, lighting and whether the area you care about is visible.

Personalized photographs

When the standard views do not show a decision-critical detail, Kakobuy offers a personalized photograph service. The published price is ¥2 per image, and the help page says the result is generally provided through the site message system within 24 hours after purchase. The request needs the relevant goods order number and a precise description of the photograph.

This is the service to use for a specific measurement, a close view of stitching, a label, a sole, hardware, an included accessory or a defect reported by the warehouse. The small fee is most valuable when the instruction is unambiguous. “More photos please” transfers the choice back to the photographer. “Place a measuring tape flat across the chest from armpit seam to armpit seam” defines the view and the evidence needed.

Parcel photographs

Parcel photographs document the completed package rather than the product. Kakobuy’s public information says three parcel photos are free if the buyer actively selects “Package Photograph” during parcel submission. When the option is not selected, the images are not provided by default.

These pictures can record the external condition, label area and overall shape of the packed parcel. They do not replace product QC. A sealed box photo cannot confirm the garment color inside, and an item photo cannot show how the final parcel was packed.

What standard inspection can reasonably catch

Visual inspection is well suited to count, broad color, model, size label, visible surface damage and clear variation mismatch. For clothing, it can reveal a wrong color, an obvious print issue, a missing drawcord or a visible stain. For shoes, it can show the overall pair, size marking, surface condition and large asymmetry. For a bag, it can show external hardware, shape and obvious marks.

The word “obvious” is important. A small flaw may be outside the camera angle or below the resolution needed for a decision. Lighting can shift perceived color. A size label can be correct while the physical measurement differs from the buyer’s expectation. A product can look fine from the outside and still have a hidden construction issue.

What QC photos do not prove

Kakobuy’s rules place limits on professional or functional inspection. QC is not a laboratory test, authentication certificate or long-term use test. It does not establish material composition, electronic performance, water resistance, structural durability or authenticity. Sealed packaging may not be opened. Electronics may not be powered on. Specialist goods may require knowledge and tools beyond a warehouse visual check.

A QC photo shows what the camera captured. It does not prove what the camera could not capture.

This boundary should appear on every independent QC page. Labels such as “verified,” “perfect” or “authentic” overstate what warehouse photography can establish. A better description names the checks completed: “color and exterior reviewed from six images” is more useful than “quality verified.”

How to write a good personalized photo request

A strong request has one subject, one angle and one purpose. If a measurement is needed, identify the two endpoints and ask for the tape to remain visible. If a label matters, state which label and request a close image that keeps the entire label readable. If you are checking a reported defect, reference its location relative to a seam, edge or recognizable feature.

Avoid subjective instructions such as “check if quality is good.” The photographer cannot apply your personal standard, and the result will be difficult to audit. Replace the subjective request with visible criteria. Ask whether both shoes have the same panel alignment, whether the zipper closes, whether an accessory is present, or whether a mark remains under different lighting.

When two views are necessary, purchase two photos and describe them separately. Combining several requirements into one image often produces a frame that answers none of them clearly.

A category-by-category checklist

Shoes

Review the pair together, then each side where possible. Check overall symmetry, toe shape, visible glue, panel alignment, stitching, sole edges, size markings and accessories. If fit is uncertain, request the insole length or another relevant measurement. A photo of the printed size alone does not confirm physical fit.

Clothing

Confirm color, size tag, front and back design, print or embroidery placement, seams and obvious marks. For sizing, request physical measurements according to a consistent method. Chest width, total length, shoulder width and sleeve length can be more useful than a familiar size letter because seller sizing varies.

Bags and accessories

Check shape, hardware color, closures, straps, included pieces and surface condition. Structured bags can deform during storage or transport, so decide whether internal support matters. Jewelry and small accessories may require a close image with a scale reference because a standard wide photo can hide detail.

Electronics and functional goods

Treat visual QC as limited. An exterior image may show the model, plug, visible damage and included accessories, but it cannot establish internal condition or performance. Do not assume the inspection includes a power-on or specialist function test unless the exact service has been confirmed.

Making the keep, exchange or return decision

Begin with the order itself. Did the warehouse receive the correct color, size, quantity and variation? Next, compare the visible condition with the seller’s description and the tolerance you established before ordering. Finally, decide whether the evidence is sufficient. If one missing view could change the decision, a personalized photo may be cheaper than accepting uncertainty.

Time matters because return or exchange options can depend on the seller and processing stage. Review photos promptly. Keep the request factual and reference the goods order number. If the issue is visible, describe its location and use the relevant image. A clear record reduces back-and-forth.

How to Judge Whether a QC Page Is Trustworthy

An independent QC page should connect the image set to one product variation and one date. It should show the source item, color, labeled size, marketplace, number of views and inspection scope. If the images belong to a different batch or older order, that context should remain visible. A random gallery without product identity can mislead a later buyer.

The page should also distinguish user-submitted material from warehouse records and avoid inventing scores. If there is no reliable basis for a rating, the site should present a checklist rather than a number. Visible evidence, explanatory captions and a last-checked date create more trust than a row of unexplained five-star reviews.

Common QC mistakes

The first mistake is approving immediately because the overall image looks attractive. The second is looking for a universal “good batch” label instead of checking the actual received item. The third is treating color under warehouse lighting as perfectly accurate. The fourth is forgetting measurements when seller sizing is uncertain. The fifth is assuming product photos and parcel photos are the same service.

Another mistake is expecting QC to answer an authenticity question. Even detailed photos do not create a professional authentication process. If authenticity is essential, visual warehouse inspection is not a substitute for appropriate independent verification.

A practical QC workflow

  1. Before ordering, identify the two or three product details that could change your decision.
  2. When the item arrives, confirm variation, quantity and obvious condition using standard photos.
  3. List any unanswered question in precise visual terms.
  4. Purchase a personalized photo only for a decision-relevant missing view.
  5. Review promptly while return or exchange options may still be available.
  6. Record whether the final decision was based on visible evidence, measurement or accepted uncertainty.
  7. Select parcel photographs separately if you want a record of the completed package.

The best QC process is not the one with the most images. It is the one that uses the fewest clear images needed to answer the real decision. By separating standard inspection, paid detail requests and package records, buyers can spend less time guessing and avoid claims the photographs were never designed to prove.
