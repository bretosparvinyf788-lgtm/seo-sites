LitBuy Delivery Inspection Guide: Build a Seven-Day Claim File for Lost or Damaged Parcels

A receiving-desk method for freezing the delivery record, inspecting every packaging layer, and presenting one evidence-led shipping case before the reporting clock expires.

Delivery Evidence Guide12 min read1,682 wordsLast researched: September 2, 2026
Editorial scope: This independent guide was checked against LitBuy’s current Shipping Policy, Returns & Exchanges terms, Refund Terms, and Disclaimer on September 2, 2026. It does not promise that a carrier investigation, refund, replacement, or compensation claim will succeed.

Delivery is a control point, not the end

A parcel can travel thousands of miles with a detailed digital trail and still create uncertainty at the doorstep. A “Delivered” scan proves a carrier event, but it does not prove that the correct carton reached the correct person, that every item is inside, or that the contents survived transit. The first minutes after arrival are therefore an evidence opportunity, not merely an unboxing moment.

LitBuy’s current Shipping Policy asks customers to contact the company within seven days when a package is lost or arrives damaged. The period is tied to the delivery date or expected delivery date. That makes timing part of the claim file. Waiting until you have a perfect theory can be worse than reporting a well-documented problem promptly and refining the facts during review.

Use a receiving log with six fields: parcel number, international tracking number, carrier, displayed delivery or expected-delivery date, observed condition, and action deadline. Add the record before opening. Your goal is to preserve the state that existed at handoff.

Prepare a receiving station before arrival

Choose a clear floor or table with strong, even light. Keep a phone charged, storage space available, and a simple scale nearby if you own one. Ask anyone who may accept the package not to discard labels, cut through damaged areas, or open it off camera. The setup is deliberately plain: one continuous view is easier to understand than a collection of dramatic close-ups with missing transitions.

Save the LitBuy parcel summary and current tracking history before delivery. Record the selected route, parcel identity, items expected, final packed weight if shown, and any warehouse parcel photos available to the account. These records form the “before transport” side of the comparison. They do not prove carrier liability by themselves, but they prevent the receiving evidence from floating without an origin record.

Receiving rule: preserve first, interpret second. Photograph the parcel exactly as received before straightening tape, pushing in a dent, removing a label, or cleaning moisture.

Classify the problem before opening a case

Do not submit every delivery problem under the word “missing.” Use one primary class. Non-delivery means the parcel has not been received and the case concerns its location or confirmed failure. Misdelivery means tracking says delivered but the intended recipient cannot find or identify the parcel. External damage means the carton, mailer, tape, seal, or label shows a physical abnormality. Internal damage means the product is broken, crushed, wet, or otherwise harmed even if the outer parcel looks normal. Shortage means expected contents are absent.

A case can contain more than one class, but each fact needs its own evidence. A torn carton can support an external-damage observation; it does not automatically prove which item is missing. An unopened-looking carton can contain a packing shortage; it does not automatically prove theft. Separate observations from conclusions so LitBuy and the carrier can investigate the correct handoff.

Record the outer parcel in one sequence

Start wide enough to show the entire unopened package and its surroundings. Then record all six sides, corners, tape lines, seams, punctures, compression, wet areas, and labels. Keep the tracking label readable in at least one still image, but do not publish it publicly because it can contain personal information. If the delivery agent provides a damage note or the carrier app shows a handoff photo, preserve that record too.

Film the transition from sealed parcel to opened parcel without stopping when practical. Cut along an undamaged seam rather than through the suspected impact point. Show the first view under the lid, every cushioning layer, the position of each item, and the empty carton after removal. A continuous sequence makes it harder for a reviewer to wonder what happened between “closed” and “broken.”

Do not test a visibly damaged electrical, sharp, leaking, or swollen item merely to produce more evidence. Safety comes first. Isolate it, show the condition without unnecessary handling, and follow applicable local disposal or emergency guidance.

Audit three layers of condition

Review the outer container, internal protection, and product separately. At the outer layer, note where force or moisture appears to have entered. At the protection layer, record crushed void fill, displaced dividers, missing wrap, or an item touching the carton wall. At the product layer, show the specific defect from context, medium distance, and close range. Include an undamaged comparison surface when it clarifies scale.

Keep packaging in the arrangement first observed until the record is complete. Do not throw away a dented box because the product seems usable; hidden damage may appear during a careful inspection. Do not discard tags, accessories, manuals, or product packaging if a return review may follow. LitBuy’s return terms state that original packaging, labels, accessories, and documents can matter to eligibility and inspection.

For a shortage, place all received items together and compare them against the LitBuy parcel list. Count units, not just product names. A pair, multipack, accessory set, and seller bonus can create different expectations. State exactly which line and quantity are absent instead of saying “things are missing.”

Build a non-delivery timeline

A quiet tracking page is not immediate proof of loss. LitBuy’s Shipping Policy says tracking information can take up to forty-eight hours to become available and describes delivery times as estimates rather than guarantees. Customs clearance, weather, and carrier disruption can also cause delay. Preserve the last scan, the displayed expected date, and the time you checked rather than inventing a loss date.

If tracking shows Delivered but the parcel is absent, verify the shipping address, delivery location, household or building reception, carrier handoff photo, and any safe-place message. Ask the carrier for a trace or delivery details using the same tracking identity. Record each check. This is not about shifting responsibility; it removes easy location errors before the formal investigation begins.

If the expected date passes with no delivery, calculate the current LitBuy contact deadline from the expected-delivery record you preserved. Report within the seven-day window even if the carrier trace remains open. Describe the parcel as unreceived and under investigation unless a competent party has confirmed loss.

Write a claim brief that can be read once

Lead with identity: LitBuy parcel number, order references if relevant, international tracking number, carrier, recipient country, and delivery or expected-delivery date. Follow with one sentence defining the primary problem. Then provide a numbered timeline from dispatch through discovery and your first contact. Keep timestamps in one timezone or label them clearly.

Attach evidence in a deliberate order: tracking and parcel summary, unopened outer-package views, opening sequence, internal packaging, product condition or shortage count, and carrier communication. Name files by event rather than camera filename. “03-unopened-corner-impact” is more useful than a random string.

State a preferred resolution without presenting it as guaranteed. LitBuy’s return guidance asks users to include the order number, item name, reason, photos or videos when applicable, and the preferred resolution—return, replacement, or refund review. For a shipping investigation, use the same discipline: say what you want reviewed and remain available for carrier evidence requests.

Know which statuses are not yet refund events

LitBuy’s current Shipping Policy says parcels that are in transit, delayed, or undergoing customs clearance are not eligible for refunds. It distinguishes those states from confirmed delivery failure and from a returned item that has been received and inspected. Do not weaken a legitimate case by calling an ordinary delay a completed loss.

Likewise, opening a support ticket does not mean a refund has been approved. Track four milestones separately: case acknowledged, carrier investigation opened, factual result recorded, and resolution completed. If a return is authorized, use the address supplied by LitBuy support rather than the sender address on the parcel, and preserve return tracking. The published return terms warn that an unauthorized return may not be accepted or refunded.

Account for fragile and inspection limits

LitBuy’s disclaimer identifies ceramics, glass, and irregularly shaped goods as vulnerable to transit wear or breakage and says losses categorized as fragile by the logistics provider may remain with the buyer. It also explains that warehouse inspection is not a professional test of electronics, authenticity, or internal completeness; some items can receive only an external-condition check.

Those limits change how you buy and document, not whether you should record damage. Before shipping, understand the item’s risk and available protection. At delivery, still preserve the complete condition chain. Do not promise yourself compensation simply because damage is visible, and do not assume a warehouse photo certified a function the inspection could not test.

Questions that close common evidence gaps

How quickly should I report a lost or damaged package? LitBuy’s current Shipping Policy asks for contact within seven days of the delivery date or expected delivery date. Preserve the relevant date and report promptly.

Does a late scan prove loss? No. Tracking can take time to appear, and delay or customs processing is not the same as confirmed delivery failure.

What should I record before opening? Capture the entire parcel, all sides, labels, seals, damage points, delivery context, and a continuous transition into the opening sequence.

Can I discard the packaging after photographing it? Keep it while the case is active. The outer carton, internal protection, labels, and seals can connect the product damage to a transport event.

Does every broken fragile item qualify for compensation? Do not assume so. The outcome depends on the applicable terms, product classification, route, carrier findings, and evidence.

Can I demand a refund while tracking says customs clearance? LitBuy’s current policy says in-transit, delayed, and customs-clearance parcels are not refund-eligible states.

Close the file only after the record matches

A useful delivery claim is a compact reconstruction, not a pile of photographs. It connects one LitBuy parcel to one tracking identity, one handoff date, one clearly classified problem, and one uninterrupted condition record. It also respects the difference between a delay, an investigation, a confirmed failure, and a completed resolution.

Keep the physical packaging and digital file until LitBuy and the carrier have finished reviewing the case. Record every new status as a dated event. When the factual result and resolution agree across the support record, carrier record, and payment or replacement record, the file can close. Until then, preserve the chain.
