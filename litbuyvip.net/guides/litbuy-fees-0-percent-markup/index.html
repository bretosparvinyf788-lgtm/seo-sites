LitBuy Fees Explained: What “0% Markup” Covers—and Which Costs Still Belong in Your Budget

A transparent cost map separating base purchasing, seller delivery, warehouse choices, international freight, returns, and destination charges.

Cost Transparency10 min read1,796 wordsLast researched: August 3, 2026
Editorial scope: This independent guide is based on LitBuy’s public service pages and help resources checked on August 3, 2026. Variable prices, promotions, route rules, and optional-service charges must be confirmed in the live LitBuy account.

“No markup” is not the same as “no cost”

When a shopping agent uses “0% markup” language, the narrow meaning is that the service does not add a percentage markup to the listed product price for the standard purchasing step. It does not mean the complete cross-border transaction is free. A product still has to travel from the Chinese seller to a warehouse, be stored and checked, be packed into an international parcel, move through a carrier network, and possibly pass through tax or customs processes at destination.

The distinction matters because buyers often compare an agent’s headline service-fee claim with the final amount paid and assume any difference is hidden markup. In reality, the total can contain several legitimate third-party or optional costs. The right audit question is not “Why is the final total larger than the item price?” It is “Can every additional amount be assigned to a clear stage of the workflow?”

LitBuy’s public site describes professional purchasing, forwarding, real-time after-sales progress, storage, free QC and inspection, packing and reinforcement, a Freight Estimator, and multiple payment methods. That service map shows why the purchase price is only the first line of the budget. This guide separates the cost layers so a buyer can evaluate value without treating every charge as either free or suspicious.

Layer one: the seller’s product price

The product price is the amount displayed by the source seller for the selected variant and quantity. Verify that the chosen colour, size, bundle, and minimum order quantity match the apparent price. On 1688 especially, a low headline figure can correspond to a higher-volume tier, a component rather than the complete product, or a different specification.

Save the source-page evidence at purchase time. Record the title, selected variant, quantity, displayed price, and seller promotion. If the seller changes the listing later, the order record remains understandable. For products subject to restrictions or supplier review, LitBuy may show a risk or purchase notice. That notice is not a fee, but it can change whether the purchase can proceed or which shipping options remain available.

A transparent agent should pass the selected purchase amount through without disguising it. A transparent buyer should also recognise that a seller price can change before the agent successfully places the order. If the account requests confirmation of a difference, compare the new seller evidence rather than assuming the platform added a percentage.

Layer two: Chinese domestic delivery

Domestic delivery is the seller’s shipment to the LitBuy warehouse. It may be free, charged per order, charged by quantity, or adjusted for remote origin and heavy goods. This amount belongs to the seller-side logistics stage, not international freight. Combining several products from one seller can sometimes reduce repeated domestic charges, but only if the listing and seller support combined dispatch.

Track domestic delivery separately in your ledger. A low-cost item with high domestic freight may be less attractive than a slightly more expensive alternative from a nearby seller. For bulky or fragile products, domestic packaging quality also affects the warehouse outcome. If an item arrives damaged, the evidence and after-sales process should identify whether the problem existed before international packing.

Do not compare two agents only by their base service-fee slogan while ignoring domestic delivery. The same source order can produce different totals if one comparison accidentally excludes seller freight.

Layer three: payment and currency effects

LitBuy’s public footer lists payment support including major cards, PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Klarna, and Afterpay. Availability can vary by region and account. Each payment rail can have its own processing economics, exchange rate, refund path, or issuer fee. These effects are not necessarily an agent markup, but they influence the amount that leaves the buyer’s bank account.

Compare payment methods using the effective CNY received or balance credited, not only the visible fee line. A low displayed fee can be offset by a weaker exchange rate or an issuer’s foreign-transaction charge. Refund conversion can also produce a small difference even when the same CNY amount is returned.

Because payment terms can change, confirm the live checkout screen. Avoid publishing a permanent percentage on an SEO page unless the official account interface displays it and the page carries a recent verification date. The useful content is the comparison method, not an old fee copied from a forum.

Layer four: warehouse services and optional decisions

LitBuy publicly promotes free basic QC and inspection, inspection photos, storage, and packing or reinforcement. Free basic QC does not make every possible warehouse request free. Additional photos, special measurements, detailed testing, repacking, reinforcement, removal of packaging, or other optional work may have current account-specific charges or conditions. Confirm them before ordering the service.

Optional does not mean unnecessary. Paying for one measurement photo can be cheaper than importing an unusable garment. Reinforcement can be cheaper than damage. Conversely, purchasing every add-on by habit can erode the value of a low-cost order. Tie each warehouse request to a risk: fit, fragility, completeness, restricted shipping, or dimensional weight.

Storage is also a time-dependent resource. LitBuy states that items can be stored for up to 120 days and that the first 90 days are free. This supports consolidation, but it is not a reason to leave items indefinitely. Plan a shipment deadline and understand what happens after the free period from the live policy page.

Layer five: returns and after-sales

A return can involve seller acceptance, domestic return freight, packaging condition, timing, and agent handling. LitBuy publishes returns and exchange resources and describes full after-sales progress tracking. The exact outcome depends on the item and seller. “No markup” does not mean the seller will pay every cost created by a change-of-mind return.

Separate three cases. A seller error or materially damaged arrival may support an after-sales claim. A buyer-selected wrong size may be a preference return. A restricted or non-returnable category may offer limited options. Preserve the order selection, warehouse photos, communication, and deadline. The evidence determines whether a cost should sit with the seller, buyer, carrier, or another party.

Make the decision before international shipping. Once the item leaves China, a low-value return is often uneconomic. This is why QC and cost control are connected: spending a small amount to resolve uncertainty can prevent a much larger sunk cost.

Layer six: international parcel charges

International freight is usually the largest variable after the merchandise. It depends on destination, eligible route, packed weight, dimensions, product type, and current carrier pricing. LitBuy provides a Freight Estimator rather than one permanent global rate. That is appropriate because the final package is a new object with its own weight and geometry.

Do not attribute every freight difference to a service fee. Reconcile the estimate with the final parcel: which items were included, what packaging remained, what the measured dimensions were, which route was selected, and whether the line billed actual or dimensional weight. If the parcel includes batteries, liquids, magnets, branded products, or unusual dimensions, route availability can be more important than the headline rate.

LitBuy currently advertises new-user shipping savings of up to 30% on public pages. Treat this as a campaign claim, not a guaranteed permanent reduction. Check eligible lines, minimum spend, discount cap, expiry, and whether the coupon is actually present in the account.

Layer seven: destination taxes and delivery-side costs

The destination country may impose VAT, sales tax, customs duty, brokerage, handling, or remote-area delivery charges. The applicable rules depend on destination, declared value, product classification, carrier, and local law. These are not erased by a zero-markup purchasing model.

Research the destination before building the haul. A product that is inexpensive in China can have a different landed-cost profile after tax or import restrictions. Avoid relying on declarations designed to evade law. A sustainable cost plan assumes lawful customs processing and keeps product descriptions and supporting records available.

The final budget should therefore include a destination contingency rather than treating customs as an impossible surprise. The correct amount is country-specific, so a general LitBuy guide should explain where to verify it instead of inventing a universal tax percentage.

A worked cost map

Imagine a hypothetical order containing three clothing items. The merchandise totals CNY 520. Seller domestic delivery is CNY 18. The buyer uses free standard QC but requests one additional measurement service whose live price must be confirmed. The items are consolidated, packed, and quoted on an eligible international line. A coupon reduces the live parcel charge, while the buyer’s card issuer applies its own conversion rate. At destination, local tax treatment depends on the country and declaration.

The accounting should show each stage separately: CNY 520 merchandise; CNY 18 seller delivery; optional warehouse service at the live account price; payment-method effect; international freight after the actual coupon; and destination tax or carrier collection. Nothing in that model contradicts a 0% product markup claim. It simply refuses to mislabel the rest of the supply chain as free.

This format also makes agent comparisons fair. Compare the same product, seller freight, parcel configuration, route class, insurance level, payment method, and tax assumption. Otherwise the comparison is not measuring service fees; it is mixing different transactions.

How to audit a LitBuy order without paranoia

Before payment, capture the product and variant. After purchase, record seller domestic freight. At warehouse arrival, separate free standard inspection from any optional request. Before parcel submission, record packed weight, dimensions, selected route, and coupon conditions. After payment, compare the balance change with the quote. At delivery, add destination collections. This creates a complete landed-cost record.

If an amount is unclear, ask which stage produced it and request the relevant order or parcel record. Clear questions receive clearer answers: “Is this seller domestic freight?” “Is this an optional warehouse service?” “Which chargeable weight did this route use?” “Was this refund returned to balance or the original payment method?”

The objective is not to prove that every order should cost only the seller price. It is to make every cost legible. A buyer who understands the layers can use LitBuy’s purchasing, QC, storage, packing, and forwarding services deliberately instead of reacting to the final total after the parcel is already committed.

Frequently misunderstood points

Does 0% markup mean there is no service fee anywhere? It generally describes the base purchasing price model, not seller freight, payment effects, optional work, international shipping, returns, or destination charges. Confirm the current account terms.

Is free QC the same as free custom photography? No. The public site promises free QC and inspection photos. Additional or specialised requests should be checked in the live interface.

Are coupons guaranteed? No. Public wording says savings of up to a stated percentage. Eligibility, caps, routes, and expiry determine the actual benefit.

Why can my card charge differ from the CNY amount? Exchange rates, issuer fees, and payment processing can change the effective total even without a product markup.

What is the most useful fee comparison metric? Landed cost for the same basket and shipping configuration, with every layer labelled. A headline service-fee percentage by itself is not enough.

Cost transparency means knowing where each cost occurs, deciding which services create value, and refusing to treat a marketing phrase as a complete invoice.
