LitBuy Forwarding Guide: How to Send Self-Purchased Parcels to the Warehouse Without Losing Track

A parcel-identity workflow for copying the live warehouse address, submitting domestic tracking, reconciling receipt, and shipping only after every record matches.

Forwarding Field Guide12 min read1,794 wordsLast researched: August 17, 2026
Editorial scope: This independent guide was checked against LitBuy’s public forwarding page, Help Center topics, and current warehouse workflow on August 17, 2026. Warehouse addresses, available controls, handling options, route eligibility, and charges must be confirmed inside the buyer’s live account.

Forwarding begins before the seller ships

A forwarding order looks simple on paper: buy an item yourself, send it to an agent’s warehouse, then ship it internationally. The difficult part is not the distance. It is identity. The seller knows its own order number, the Chinese carrier knows a domestic tracking number, LitBuy receives a physical carton, and you know the item you intended to buy. Unless those four records connect cleanly, the warehouse may receive a parcel that is real but difficult to match.

LitBuy currently presents forwarding as a separate workflow and asks users to log in or register to obtain the warehouse address. Its public guidance also refers to submitting the domestic tracking number, waiting for the item to show “Stored,” and then selecting stored goods in Warehouse for packing and shipping. Those are the visible handoff points. A reliable buyer creates the missing bridge between them.

This guide treats forwarding as a parcel-identity chain. You will prepare the address, preserve the seller record, monitor the domestic carrier, reconcile the arrival, and only then build an international parcel. A corrected record is cheap; an unidentified carton is not.

Decide whether forwarding is the right order path

Use forwarding when you control the purchase on the Chinese seller or marketplace and need LitBuy to receive the resulting domestic parcel. That may suit an account-specific price, a seller conversation, a private order, or an item already purchased elsewhere. It also means you—not the agent’s purchasing workflow—own the accuracy of the seller order, payment, variation, quantity, and domestic delivery instruction.

Do not choose forwarding merely because the product link parser is inconvenient. If you need an agent to clarify the variation, communicate a pre-purchase condition, or manage the seller transaction, a purchasing or DIY-order workflow may create a clearer responsibility chain. Forwarding is strongest when the commercial order is already unambiguous and the remaining task is controlled warehouse receipt.

Run an eligibility screen before payment. Ask whether the product can be accepted, stored, and shipped to your destination through an available line. Batteries, liquids, powders, food, medical items, pressurized goods, magnets, oversized products, and controlled brands may create restrictions. A seller’s willingness to send domestically does not prove that an international route will accept the item.

Path rule: if you need help defining what to buy, use a purchasing workflow. If you already own a precise order and need warehouse receipt, forwarding may be the cleaner path.

Copy the live warehouse address exactly

Obtain the warehouse address only after signing in to the current LitBuy forwarding interface. Do not reuse an address from an old order, a tutorial screenshot, a community post, or another person’s account. Warehouses, recipient fields, identification codes, phone numbers, and formatting requirements can change. The live account is the operational source.

Copy each field into the seller checkout separately: province, city, district, street, building or unit, recipient, phone number, postal code if required, and any account-specific identification. Chinese marketplace forms sometimes split an address differently from a Western form, so compare the final formatted address with the LitBuy version before paying.

If the interface supplies a customer code or requires information in the recipient or address line, preserve its spacing and characters. Do not “simplify” a warehouse instruction because it looks repetitive. That identifier may be how the receiving team connects an otherwise anonymous domestic parcel to your account.

Create a forwarding record before dispatch

Open a one-row record for every seller parcel. Include seller or shop name, marketplace order number, item description, variation, quantity, amount paid to the seller, expected parcel count, LitBuy forwarding reference, and domestic tracking number. If one order will ship in two cartons, create two parcel rows. If three orders share one domestic parcel, list all included items under that one tracking number.

Add evidence that survives a changed or deleted listing: the paid order screen, exact selected options, seller messages that define bundle contents, and a product image. This is not material for decorating a claim. It is a reconciliation file. When the warehouse photo shows a black jacket, you should be able to confirm which order, size, quantity, and seller promise it represents without reopening ten apps.

Use a short internal name such as “navy coat / size L / one unit,” not a vague label such as “clothes.” It should be recognizable without inventing facts the receiver cannot observe.

Submit the domestic tracking number with context

LitBuy’s public forwarding page refers to submitting the tracking number. Enter it when the seller has produced a genuine domestic shipment, then verify the carrier and digits against the seller order. A copied number with one transposed digit can create a perfect-looking record for the wrong parcel.

Do not treat “tracking number created” as proof that the carrier has the goods. Look for the first acceptance or collection scan, then follow movement toward the warehouse city. Record the seller’s dispatch date and the carrier’s first physical scan. That distinction becomes useful if the number remains dormant or the seller replaces it.

If a tracking number changes, update the forwarding record before delivery and keep the original number in your notes. If the seller splits the shipment, submit and document each number rather than expecting one declaration to cover an unknown second carton. If two sellers coincidentally use similar descriptions, the tracking number—not the product nickname—should remain the primary matching key.

Monitor the last domestic mile

Domestic tracking usually becomes most important near delivery. Watch for delivery attempts, abnormal routing, address questions, refusal, return-to-sender movement, or a “delivered” scan. A carrier scan confirms what the carrier recorded; it does not by itself confirm that LitBuy has identified and stored the parcel in your account.

When the carrier shows delivered, capture the timestamp and any proof or recipient information available. Then allow the warehouse receiving process to create the account record. Avoid submitting repeated generic support messages during a normal intake gap. If the gap becomes unusual, send one compact evidence set: forwarding reference, carrier, tracking number, delivery timestamp, seller parcel count, and a screenshot of the final scan.

LitBuy’s Help Center currently includes forwarding-order guidance, receipt notes, shipment tracking, customs topics, and after-sales categories. Use the current topic that matches the actual stage. A domestic delivery question and an international parcel question are different cases even when they concern the same product.

Reconcile the Stored item before you consolidate

LitBuy’s public forwarding instructions say that once the status shows “Stored,” the buyer can go to Warehouse, select items to be packed and shipped, provide the shipping address, and pay the related fees. Treat Stored as a new checkpoint, not permission to ship without review.

Compare the warehouse entry with your forwarding record. Confirm the domestic tracking number, received parcel count, visible item identity, quantity, variation markers, and condition evidence available in the account. Check whether the seller sent all units together, left an accessory in a separate box, or substituted packaging that changes the appearance.

Do not assume that every forwarding parcel receives the same inspection evidence or handling options as an agent-purchased order. Review what the live interface actually provides. If one missing photo or measurement will determine acceptance, request or select the appropriate available service before consolidation. If the received identity is wrong, keep the item out of an international package while the record can still be investigated.

Use an exception ladder instead of guessing

For a delivered-but-not-stored parcel, first verify the tracking number, address, parcel count, and final carrier scan. Then check whether the item has appeared under a different forwarding entry. Escalate with the evidence bundle only after removing those basic mismatches.

For an unknown warehouse item, compare carrier number, seller name, weight if shown, outer-package clues, and expected contents. Do not claim an item solely because its photo resembles your purchase. For a wrong or incomplete item, preserve seller-order evidence and identify whether the problem began with seller fulfillment, domestic transport, or warehouse matching. The correct remedy depends on that stage.

For a route restriction discovered after storage, pause the parcel plan. Check the currently available routes and destination, then decide whether the item can travel separately, requires a permitted handling option, should be returned domestically, or needs another compliant outcome. Never describe a restricted item inaccurately to force a route.

Close the chain before international submission

Before selecting items in Warehouse, perform a final cross-check. Every forwarded tracking number should point to a stored item or a documented exception. Every stored item should point back to a seller order. Every accepted item should have a destination-compatible route visible in the account. Every unresolved mismatch should remain outside the package.

Then review packaging as a deliberate instruction. Seller cartons may add volume; removing them may reduce protection, identification, presentation value, or return options. Soft goods, fragile goods, electronics, collectibles, and shaped products should not receive one blanket instruction. Use only the packaging and reinforcement choices currently offered for the package.

Finally, verify recipient name, address, phone number, postal code, included-item list, available route, declared information, and payable amount. LitBuy’s public page describes address entry and payment after the stored items are selected, but it does not justify a permanent price or timing promise. The live submission screen is the decision document.

Forwarding questions that prevent real errors

Where should I get the LitBuy warehouse address? Log in or register and copy the address shown in the current forwarding interface. Do not reuse an address from another account or an old guide.

When should I submit the domestic tracking number? Submit it when the seller has created the actual shipment, verify the carrier and every digit, and then look for a physical acceptance scan rather than relying only on label creation.

What if one seller order ships in two cartons? Record and submit both tracking numbers as separate parcel identities while linking them to the same seller order and expected contents.

Does a carrier’s Delivered scan mean the parcel is Stored? No. Delivered describes the carrier event. Stored is the warehouse-account status used by LitBuy’s forwarding instructions for the next packing and shipping step.

Can all forwarded goods be consolidated? Do not assume so. Product restrictions, destination, available routes, handling needs, or unresolved warehouse states may prevent a combination. Test the actual selection in Warehouse.

Should I submit an international parcel while a forwarding mismatch is open? No. Keep the affected item outside the package until tracking, identity, quantity, condition, and route questions are resolved.
