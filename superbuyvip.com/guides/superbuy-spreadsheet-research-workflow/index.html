How to Use a Superbuy Spreadsheet Without Buying Blind

A practical workflow for checking seller history, product details, QC evidence and estimated shipping before an item enters your haul.

Field manualUpdated August 20261,780 words

A Superbuy spreadsheet can shorten a long search, but it cannot make the final decision for you. The row that looks neat on a screen is only a pointer to a live marketplace listing. Stock changes, sellers replace photographs, variants disappear, domestic delivery charges move, and the item that reaches the warehouse may not match the image that first caught your attention. The useful skill is therefore not finding more links. It is turning each link into a compact evidence file before money and international shipping are committed.

This guide treats the spreadsheet as an intake desk. Its job is to organize candidates, expose unanswered questions and preserve the facts you will need when warehouse photos arrive. Superbuy then handles the purchasing, warehouse and parcel stages. Keeping those roles separate prevents a common mistake: assuming that inclusion in a curated sheet is the same thing as a product guarantee.

1. Replace the wish list with a decision record

Start with five columns that actually influence a purchase: source status, exact variant, evidence quality, expected parcel impact and decision state. Source status tells you whether the listing still opens and can be ordered. Exact variant records colour, size, model and quantity in the seller's own terminology. Evidence quality describes whether you have measurements, recent buyer photographs or only promotional images. Parcel impact flags bulk, fragility, batteries, liquids or special packaging. Decision state should be one of research, ready, ordered, inspect, accept, exchange or return.

This structure is more useful than a long sheet of names and prices because it preserves context. When ten items arrive over several days, you will not have to reconstruct why a particular size was chosen or which flaw mattered. The sheet becomes a chain of custody for decisions rather than a catalogue you scroll through impulsively.

2. Audit the source page before copying anything

Open the original product page, not a screenshot or social post. Confirm that the listing is active, the selected variant is available and the seller has not quietly reused the page for a different product. Read the variant labels beside the actual selection controls. A photograph may show three colours while the order menu names them with codes. Save the code you selected, because that is what the shopping agent can act on.

Look for a measurement table, material statement, package contents and recent transaction evidence. None of these proves quality, but together they reduce ambiguity. Be cautious when every photograph is a polished campaign image, the size chart has no units, or several visually different versions share one vague option. Those are not automatic reasons to reject the item; they are reasons to move it from ready back to research.

3. Lock the variant with a specification sentence

Before submitting an order, write one sentence that another person could follow without seeing your browser: item name, colour, size, version, quantity and one critical condition. For example, your condition might be “include the detachable strap” or “confirm the insole marking is EU 42.” This sentence is more reliable than remembering a thumbnail. It also gives you a direct comparison point when the warehouse record appears.

For apparel, keep both the seller size and the garment measurements that matter to you. Letter sizes are not universal. For footwear, record the seller's sizing system and the foot or insole measurement used to choose it. For sets, specify the expected number of pieces. The aim is not to write an essay in the order remark; it is to eliminate the single ambiguity most likely to create a wrong purchase.

4. Separate seller risk from agent service

Superbuy can purchase, receive, photograph, store, consolidate and ship an item, but it does not turn a weak seller listing into a reliable product. Seller risk concerns authenticity, description accuracy, production variation and after-sales cooperation. Agent service concerns order communication, warehouse handling and parcel execution. Judge them separately. If the source evidence is poor, do not use the presence of warehouse inspection as an excuse to skip research.

The official Superbuy process includes warehouse quality inspection and photographs, yet visible inspection has limits. A photograph cannot prove long-term durability, internal electronics, scent, exact fabric composition or comfort. Your pre-purchase record should therefore identify which uncertainties can be checked visually and which remain commercial risk even after QC.

5. Build a landed-cost range, not a fake exact total

The marketplace price is only the first layer. Add Chinese domestic shipping, any optional service you already know you will request, a realistic share of international freight and a safety margin for packaging or route changes. Superbuy currently states that standard purchasing from mainstream platforms such as Taobao, Tmall, JD.com and 1688 carries no purchasing service fee. That does not mean every possible order is fee-free: second-hand, unlisted or specialist purchasing services have different rules, and optional warehouse services are charged when selected.

Do not divide an old parcel bill by item count and call that the freight estimate. A lightweight T-shirt and a shoe box affect a parcel differently. Use a range: compact case, expected case and bulky case. The compact case assumes removable retail packaging and a compatible line. The expected case reflects likely packing. The bulky case protects you if volumetric weight or reinforcement dominates. If the item is no longer attractive in the expected case, it should not be marked ready.

Official-policy checkpointSuperbuy's published fee guide separates the domestic purchasing stage from the international shipping stage. International cost is generated from the destination, selected line and final parcel data; free consolidation can reduce repeated base charges, but it cannot guarantee a lower bill when the finished parcel becomes bulky.

6. Give each uncertainty an evidence grade

Use three grades. Grade A is directly checkable: the warehouse photograph can show colour, count, visible damage or a size label. Grade B needs a targeted request: an exact measurement, close view of a mark, accessory check or packaging detail. Grade C cannot be resolved confidently at the warehouse: comfort, long-term performance, hidden construction or subjective material feel. This simple grading stops you from buying a detail photo for a question a photo cannot answer.

7. Submit the order as a controlled handoff

Paste the source link into Superbuy and verify that the interpreted title, price and variants still correspond to the seller page. Select colour, size and quantity deliberately; do not assume the first default is correct. Superbuy's official guide notes that some products may trigger international-delivery risk reminders and that 1688 listings can have minimum quantities or distinct purchasing conditions. Read those notices before payment rather than treating them as generic pop-ups.

Your order note should be short and operational. Use it for a critical choice the variant menu cannot express, not for a long quality manifesto. After payment, monitor order enquiries and messages. If the shopping agent asks for clarification, answer while the seller can still process the order. A delayed response can be more costly than a slightly higher product price.

8. Convert warehouse arrival into a decision gate

When the item enters My Warehouse, compare it with the specification sentence and evidence grades from your sheet. Work in order: identity, quantity, visible size, major colour, overall shape, obvious damage, then category-specific details. Superbuy currently advertises three free QC photos after warehouse arrival. Treat them as a starting view, not as proof that every seam, surface and function has been tested.

Record one outcome immediately: accept, ask, exchange or return. “Ask” must include the exact missing evidence and the deadline by which you want to decide. Do not let several items sit in an undefined state until parcel day. Domestic after-sales possibilities are generally easier to explore before international shipment, and your spreadsheet should show which item is blocking the parcel.

9. Test parcel compatibility before adding one more find

A haul is not simply a pile of accepted products. It is a group that can travel under compatible rules. A battery, liquid, magnet, unusually long object or sensitive branded item can narrow the available routes. A fragile collectible may need rigid protection that makes an otherwise compact clothing parcel larger. Mark these traits early so a cheap discovery does not unexpectedly reshape the whole shipment.

Ask three questions before adding another item: does it share likely route eligibility, can it share the same protection standard, and does it improve or damage the parcel's size efficiency? If the answer is uncertain, keep it in a separate parcel scenario until Superbuy displays the actual eligible options.

10. Use the storage window as a schedule

Superbuy publishes a 90-day free storage period from warehouse arrival. After that, its current fee guide describes daily storage charges and a maximum standard storage horizon unless an extension is purchased. Do not interpret 90 days as permission to ignore the item for three months. Use arrival dates to create a parcel clock: first arrivals set the planning horizon, later items must justify the time they add.

11. A repeatable 15-minute research routine

  1. Open the live source and verify the exact variant.
  2. Capture measurements, contents and the critical condition.
  3. Grade remaining uncertainty as A, B or C.
  4. Estimate compact, expected and bulky landed-cost cases.
  5. Flag route or packaging constraints.
  6. Submit only when the expected case still makes sense.
  7. At warehouse arrival, compare the product with the saved record and decide promptly.

The point of this routine is not perfection. It is consistency. A ten-minute check repeated for every item prevents the expensive pattern of impulsive ordering, vague QC and last-minute parcel decisions.

Rebuilt questions buyers should ask

Does a place in the spreadsheet mean the seller is verified forever?

No. A curated entry is a research lead captured at a point in time. Recheck the live listing, variant and seller evidence before every order because marketplace conditions change.

Should I order first and solve sizing during QC?

No. Warehouse photographs may show a label, but they cannot make a poorly chosen size suitable. Use seller measurements before ordering and request a targeted measurement only when it can resolve a specific remaining doubt.

Is standard purchasing always free?

Superbuy currently lists no purchasing service fee for mainstream platforms including Taobao, Tmall, JD.com and 1688. Different fees can apply to second-hand, unlisted or specialist services, and optional warehouse services remain separate.

What makes a spreadsheet genuinely useful?

It preserves decisions. The best sheet records why a variant was selected, what remains uncertain, how the item affects shipping and what warehouse evidence will trigger acceptance, exchange or return.
