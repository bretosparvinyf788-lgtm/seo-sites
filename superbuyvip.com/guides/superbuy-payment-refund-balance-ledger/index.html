Superbuy Payment and Refund Ledger: Reconcile Every Charge

Separate product payments, shipping deposits, balance credits and seller refunds so every movement has an order, reason and next action.

Payment-control playbookPublished August 28, 20261,558 words

A Superbuy purchase rarely produces one neat charge. A buyer may pay for the item and Chinese domestic delivery first, approve a later adjustment, add an optional service, submit a parcel deposit and then receive a balance credit after the package is measured. If every movement is remembered as “the order cost,” it becomes difficult to tell whether a refund is missing or a shipping estimate was simply reconciled.

Superbuy’s current public guidance describes two main payment stages. Stage one covers the product and domestic shipping in China. Stage two begins after warehouse storage, when the buyer submits a parcel and pays the international shipping amount. Its forwarding guide explains that the parcel payment is initially based on estimated weight, shipping method and destination; the final fee is calculated after the carrier verifies the packed dimensions and weight, with a difference returned to the Superbuy account when appropriate.

1. Build a ledger around events, not card statements

Your bank or card statement proves that money left a payment source, but it usually does not explain the entire order. Create one ledger row for each event: initial product payment, domestic freight adjustment, optional service, seller refund, parcel deposit, final shipping reconciliation and balance use.

Each row needs a date, Superbuy order or parcel number, event type, amount shown by Superbuy, payment source, account-balance movement and status. A final note should say what happens next. “Waiting for seller refund” is actionable. “Paid about sixty dollars” is not.

2. Keep the two payment stages separate

Superbuy’s shopping guidance says the first payment covers the item and Chinese local delivery. This creates the purchasing order and allows the agent to buy from the seller. It does not prepay the final international journey. The item must first reach the warehouse, pass receiving and become available for parcel submission.

The second payment belongs to the outbound parcel. It depends on the selected destination, eligible line and estimated parcel data. Store stage-one costs under the item record and stage-two costs under the parcel record. Link them, but do not combine them until you calculate the final landed total.

3. Record the displayed currency and funding currency

Superbuy’s official guidance says multiple currencies and several payment methods may be available. Your account display, payment channel and bank statement may not use the same currency. Record both the amount shown at checkout and the amount that ultimately appears on your payment statement.

Do not label the difference automatically as a Superbuy fee. Exchange conversion, card processing or intermediary-bank deductions may belong to the payment channel. For a bank remittance, Superbuy’s current remit page says the account is topped up according to the amount actually received. Keep any bank receipt until the matching balance entry appears.

4. Treat supplementary payments as change orders

A marketplace listing can change before purchase. Domestic freight may differ, a seller may confirm another price, or the selected variant may require an adjustment. When an agent requests additional payment, record the reason and compare it with the original order rather than paying from habit.

Ask whether the change affects product price, China shipping, quantity or an optional service. Save the message that explains it. If the new total breaks your purchase ceiling, decide whether to approve, choose another variant or cancel. A small unexplained supplement repeated across many orders can quietly damage a haul budget.

The one-reference ruleNever record a credit or charge without an order number, parcel number or transaction reference. Money without a reference becomes a memory problem; money with a reference becomes a case you can verify.

5. Separate three kinds of money returning

Not every incoming balance movement has the same cause. A seller refund follows a cancelled or adjusted shopping order. A parcel reconciliation follows the difference between an international shipping deposit and the final measured fee. A manual top-up correction relates to the funding process. Give each its own event type.

This distinction prevents a common mistake: seeing a balance increase and assuming the expected seller refund has arrived. Match the amount and reference to the correct order or parcel. If the source is unclear, use the transaction details in the balance area before closing any open refund case.

6. Understand the international shipping deposit

Superbuy’s forwarding guide says the international shipping deposit is calculated from estimated weight, the selected method and destination. At that point, packaging may not yet have produced the carrier-verified figures. The deposit is therefore a working amount, not automatically the final shipping cost.

After consolidation and packing, the parcel is weighed and measured. The guide states that the final fee uses the verified package data and that a difference can be returned to the Superbuy account after shipment. Keep the original deposit, final fee and balance adjustment as three separate ledger fields. That makes reconciliation immediate.

7. Do not spend a new balance credit too quickly

A balance credit is easy to reuse on the next order, which can make the original case harder to audit. When money returns, first match it to its source and mark the related row complete. Only then classify the remaining account balance as available for another purchase or parcel.

This is especially useful when several refunds and shipping adjustments arrive near one another. A short holding rule—reconcile before reuse—prevents one credit from being mentally counted twice.

8. Reconcile by parcel, not only by haul

A large haul may produce more than one international parcel because of route restrictions, fragility, size or timing. Give every parcel its own subtotal: included item cost, allocated domestic freight, value-added services, shipping deposit, final shipping charge and any returned difference.

Allocation does not change what Superbuy charged; it helps you understand which parcel consumed the budget. Use a consistent method. You might assign item-level costs directly and keep parcel services at parcel level. Do not spread a fragile-item reinforcement fee across unrelated clothing merely to make the numbers look even.

9. Use a five-status refund workflow

  1. Requested: the cancellation, return or adjustment was submitted.
  2. Accepted: the responsible party confirmed the remedy.
  3. Processing: the refund is underway but no matching credit is visible.
  4. Credited: a balance or payment movement appears with a reference.
  5. Reconciled: the amount and source match, and the case is closed.

Dates matter as much as labels. Record when the status last changed and when you will review it again. Do not invent a promised completion time; use the live case response and contact support with the relevant order number when the recorded expectation passes.

10. Run a ten-minute weekly money audit

  1. Export or review recent Superbuy transaction details.
  2. Match every debit to an order, parcel or service.
  3. Match every credit to a seller refund, correction or parcel reconciliation.
  4. Check supplementary payments against their explanations.
  5. Compare parcel deposits with final shipping figures.
  6. List refunds still in requested, accepted or processing status.
  7. Record payment-channel amounts separately from Superbuy amounts.
  8. Confirm the remaining account balance is not already assigned.
  9. Save evidence for any unresolved discrepancy.
  10. Set one dated next action for each open case.

The purpose is not complicated bookkeeping. It is to know what the haul has actually cost, what money is still in motion and which question must be asked next.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Superbuy use two payment stages?

The first stage covers the item and Chinese domestic delivery. The second covers international shipment after the item reaches the warehouse and a parcel is submitted.

Is the parcel payment always the final shipping fee?

Not necessarily. Official guidance describes an estimated deposit followed by a final calculation based on verified packed size and weight.

Where does a shipping difference go?

Superbuy’s forwarding guide says an applicable difference is returned to the Superbuy account after shipment. Match it to the relevant parcel record.

How can I check a refund or top-up?

Use the account balance and transaction-detail area, which the current Help Center identifies for balance, refunds and top-ups.

Does a balance credit prove my seller refund arrived?

No. It may come from a parcel reconciliation or another correction. Match the amount and transaction reference to the original case.

What should I record for a card payment?

Record the Superbuy checkout amount and currency, the card-statement amount and currency, the order reference and the transaction date.

Why might a bank remittance top-up be lower than sent?

Superbuy says remittance credit is based on the amount actually received. Intermediary deductions or conversion may affect that amount, so keep the bank receipt.

Should I reuse a refund balance immediately?

First reconcile it to the correct order or parcel. Reusing an unidentified credit can make the original refund appear missing later.

What is a supplementary payment?

It is an additional amount requested after the original order, often because a confirmed price, domestic shipping cost, quantity or service differs. Verify the reason before approval.

How do I calculate final landed cost?

Add the reconciled item, domestic shipping, optional-service and allocated international parcel costs. Use final figures rather than the original parcel estimate.

What evidence should I keep for an open refund?

Keep the order number, request date, agent or seller response, expected amount, transaction screenshot and next review date.

When should I contact support about money movement?

Contact support when a documented expectation has passed or a transaction cannot be matched. Include the relevant order, parcel or transaction number.
