Category-by-category research checklist

01

Shoes / Slippers

Footwear research starts with fit evidence, not the printed size alone. Compare the seller chart with a shoe that already fits you.

  • Ask for insole length and check the measurement method.
  • Use QC photos to inspect both sides, toe shape, outsole and pair symmetry.
  • Decide whether the retail box is worth the extra parcel volume and protection.

02

T-Shirts

Labels such as small or large are unreliable across sellers. Garment measurements and print placement provide better evidence.

  • Compare chest width, shoulder width and length with a shirt you own.
  • Inspect print or embroidery alignment, seams, collar and visible marks.
  • Confirm color and fabric description on the live listing.
Open T-shirt collection ↗
03

Fashion Clothing

Jackets and layered pieces combine fit, construction and hardware questions, so a single front photo is rarely enough.

  • Check garment dimensions and whether the seller recommends layering room.
  • Review lining, closures, pockets, embroidery and seam finish in QC images.
  • Consider item weight and bulky construction during parcel planning.

04

Hoodies

Hoodies can look similar in listing photos while differing in cut, fabric weight, printing and zipper construction.

  • Compare chest, length and sleeve measurements rather than size names.
  • Inspect graphics, embroidery, drawstrings, ribbing and zipper alignment.
  • Allow for fabric thickness when estimating consolidated parcel volume.
Open hoodie collection ↗
05

Pants / Trousers

Waist labels do not explain rise, thigh room, inseam or intended fit. Build the decision from a complete measurement set.

  • Compare waist, rise, thigh, inseam and leg opening with your own trousers.
  • Check pocket placement, stitching, closures and fabric condition at QC.
  • Confirm whether measurements are flat, stretched or circumference values.
Open trouser collection ↗
06

Leather Belts

Belt length can mean total length or the distance to a specific hole. Resolve that ambiguity before choosing a variant.

  • Confirm the seller's measuring method and usable hole range.
  • Inspect buckle finish, hole spacing, edge paint and visible surface damage.
  • Choose whether boxes and presentation packaging should remain.
Open belt collection ↗
07

Fashion Bags

Bag research should cover capacity, strap geometry and structure as well as appearance. Packaging choices can affect shape.

  • Confirm external dimensions, strap drop and the items you expect to carry.
  • Review corners, handles, closures, hardware and panel alignment at QC.
  • Balance shape protection against volumetric shipping cost.
Open bag collection ↗
08

Perfume

Fragrance and other liquids may have limited or unavailable shipping routes. Route feasibility must come before product comparison.

  • Check current destination-specific liquid eligibility with the shipping agent.
  • Review volume, bottle protection and leakage risk before purchase.
  • Do not treat a general route estimate as approval for the actual item.
Open perfume collection ↗
09

Electronics

Electronics add compatibility, battery and restricted-goods questions that ordinary apparel research does not cover.

  • Confirm plug type, voltage, language, connectivity and included accessories.
  • Ask whether the item or battery changes available shipping lines.
  • Understand what warehouse inspection can and cannot test functionally.
Open electronics collection ↗
10

Other Stuff

Mixed-category finds need classification before comparison. Material and function determine the useful QC and shipping questions.

  • Identify dimensions, material, weight and any liquid, magnetic or battery content.
  • Write an item-specific QC checklist instead of relying on generic photos.
  • Verify route restrictions and protective packaging for the actual object.
