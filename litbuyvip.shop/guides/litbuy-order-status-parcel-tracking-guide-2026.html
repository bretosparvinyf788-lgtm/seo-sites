LitBuy Order Status and Tracking: From Purchase to Delivery

Understand LitBuy order, warehouse, packing and parcel tracking stages. Learn which number to check and what evidence to send when progress stalls.

Updated: 2026-10-08 · English

An order number and a tracking number serve different stages

A shopping-agent order usually describes the product purchase. Domestic tracking describes seller-to-warehouse delivery. A warehouse record confirms what was received. An international parcel number groups selected stored items, and the carrier tracking number follows the shipped box. These identifiers are related but should not be treated as interchangeable.

Exact status wording varies with the account interface and route. The stage descriptions below are a reading guide, not a verified list of LitBuy interface labels. Match the meaning and accompanying timestamps in your own record.

Purchase submitted and seller dispatch

Check the selected SKU, payment record and any agent message first. An order submitted for purchase may still need review, seller stock confirmation or additional information. A payment confirmation does not prove seller dispatch.

When domestic tracking is added, note the carrier and latest scan. A domestic delivery marked complete means the seller’s parcel reached its recorded destination; it does not itself prove warehouse QC completion or that the correct variant was received. For a stock or dispatch question, use the purchase order ID rather than an international tracking number that does not exist yet.

Warehouse receiving, QC and your decision

Receiving can include parcel identification, item checking, photographs, weighing and recording. Compare the warehouse product record with your saved listing and variant. If the order shows delivery but no warehouse record, check whether the domestic tracking number was registered correctly, particularly for self-purchased forwarding items.

Once evidence appears, decide whether to accept, request clarification or pursue eligible after-sales. Keep uncertain or wrong goods out of the international parcel. The seller’s return window and warehouse storage allowance are separate; waiting for other purchases can use up the seller’s time limit.

Packing or rehearsal is not carrier transit

Selecting items and submitting a parcel starts a preparation stage. Instructions may require packing, reinforcement, removal of disposable packaging or measurement. A rehearsal result supplies information for a shipping decision; it does not mean the parcel has left the warehouse.

Read whether the account is waiting for your approval, an extra payment or a route selection. Check final weight, dimensions and total payable amount before confirming. If preparation exceeds the service’s displayed processing range, send the parcel number and submission timestamp to the platform’s support channel.

Label created and carrier accepted

A tracking number can be allocated before the carrier physically receives the box. “Label created” or equivalent electronic information is different from a pickup or acceptance scan. Cross-check the agent handoff record with the named carrier’s tracking page. Some routes also allocate a final-mile number later.

For example, an agent parcel number might identify your grouped warehouse items while a separate tracking number identifies the carrier’s shipment. Record both in your notes. Do not enter the purchase order ID into a carrier form and conclude that the shipment is missing when it returns no match.

International movement and delivery

Read scans in order, keeping their displayed time zones in mind. Export handling, transit and final-mile events may come from different logistics partners. A gap between scans is not enough by itself to identify loss. Compare with the current route’s expected processing range and the messages in your account.

When a delivery scan appears but the parcel is not found, check the delivery location information and the carrier’s available evidence. Contact the platform or carrier through its stated enquiry process, providing the shipment reference and the relevant scan. If packaging or contents are damaged, preserve the box and the evidence requested under the applicable claim terms.

Send a support request that can be investigated

Include the relevant order or parcel ID, carrier and tracking number, destination country, last scan with date, the stage that appears stalled and your specific question. Attach the account status and tracking screenshots while obscuring unnecessary personal or payment information. Keep messages in the official support channel.

An example: “Parcel [ID] was submitted on [date]. The account shows [status], and carrier tracking [number] last shows [event/date]. Please confirm whether the box has been handed to the carrier and whether another tracking number applies.” This is clearer than asking only why an order is slow.

Tracking questions answered

Does a label mean the parcel is moving? No; look for handoff or acceptance evidence. Does domestic delivery mean international delivery? No; it is an earlier stage. Is every unchanged status a lost parcel? No; investigate against the expected range and actual scans.

Keep a timeline: purchase submitted, seller dispatched, domestic delivered, warehouse received, QC approved, parcel submitted, packed, paid, handed over and delivered. That record shows which stage needs attention and which reference number support needs.
