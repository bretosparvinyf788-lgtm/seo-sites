LitBuy Size Guide: Measure Shoes, Hoodies and Pants Before Buying

Choose LitBuy product sizes using seller charts, garment measurements and focused QC requests. Worked examples for shoes, hoodies and pants.

Updated: 2026-10-08 · English

Start with measurements, not a universal size conversion

There is no single LitBuy size chart that covers every seller and product. A label such as M, EU 42 or W32 is a reference; it does not establish how a specific variant fits. Use the selected listing’s chart and compare like-for-like measurements. This guide describes a measurement workflow, not a promise that any catalog item runs true to size.

You need three pieces of evidence: the seller’s chart for the chosen SKU, measurements from something that fits you, and warehouse measurements when uncertainty matters. Save them together before the seller’s return window ends.

Shoes: separate foot length from insole length

Measure each foot standing on a flat surface, ideally wearing the socks you plan to use. Mark heel and longest toe, measure in centimeters, and use the longer foot. Check whether the seller’s chart is based on foot length, inner length or a size label; those are different quantities.

For illustration, a 26.0 cm foot does not mean you should automatically choose a 26.0 cm insole. Toe space, shoe shape and the chart’s measuring convention matter. Do not apply a fixed universal allowance. If fit is uncertain, ask for the selected size’s insole measurement or the seller’s recommendation and compare with a similar shoe you own. Some shoes have non-removable insoles, so ask whether the measurement is feasible before paying for it.

Hoodies: compare flat width, length and sleeve method

Lay a well-fitting hoodie flat without stretching it. Measure chest width from armpit to armpit, body length from the specified shoulder point, shoulder width where the garment has a defined seam, and sleeve length using the same start point as the seller. A raglan sleeve and a shoulder-seam sleeve need different comparisons.

Suppose your hoodie is 58 cm wide and 70 cm long, while a seller chart gives 56 cm and 68 cm for one size. The candidate is 2 cm narrower when laid flat and 2 cm shorter. Doubling the flat chest widths gives an illustrative 116 cm versus 112 cm circumference; this is not the same as body chest circumference. The chart’s tolerance and construction still affect fit.

Pants: check waist method, rise and inseam

Measure trousers laid flat: waistband width, front rise, thigh width at a defined distance below the crotch, inseam and leg opening. If the waistband stretches, ask whether the chart lists relaxed or stretched width. Check whether a seller’s waist value is full circumference or flat width.

A 40 cm flat waistband corresponds roughly to an 80 cm circumference if measured without stretching. It should not be compared directly with a chart value of 40 cm circumference. For a practical comparison, pants with a 74 cm inseam will be 3 cm shorter along the inner leg than a reference pair measured at 77 cm. The rise and intended fit may change where the hem sits.

Write a focused warehouse measurement request

Specify the item and variant, the required measurement, the unit, the start and end points, and whether the garment should lie flat without stretching. A useful request is: “For this hoodie SKU, please photograph chest width armpit to armpit and body length using a visible centimeter tape.” A vague request to “check size” is harder to act on.

Check the fee and feasibility before requesting extra photos. Keep the full photo so both tape endpoints are visible. Camera perspective and a folded garment can distort the result. If the measurement looks inconsistent, ask for clarification rather than treating a blurred number as proof.

Decide before international shipping

Compare the evidence with your acceptable fit range. If the variant is wrong or the dimensions are clearly unsuitable, check seller return or exchange eligibility promptly. Do not ship an uncertain garment internationally hoping that its label will solve the fit issue.

A small difference may be acceptable for a loose hoodie and unacceptable for fitted trousers. Choose according to your reference garment and intended use. Record your decision with the measurement photo, seller chart and selected SKU so you can reuse the comparison next time.

Size questions answered

Does EU 42 always mean the same length? No; check the product chart and its measuring basis. Is flat chest width the same as chest circumference? No; flat width is roughly half the garment circumference when measured consistently. Can QC photos guarantee fit? No; they provide evidence to compare, not a fit guarantee.

A useful size record includes units, measurement method, chosen variant, chart tolerance, reference-item dimensions and warehouse result. That is more reliable than a universal recommendation to size up or size down.
