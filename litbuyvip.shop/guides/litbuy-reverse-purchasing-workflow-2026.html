How to Build a Low-Risk Reverse Purchasing Workflow with LitBuy

A fact-checked process for moving from a Chinese marketplace link to warehouse inspection, consolidation and an international parcel—without letting low product prices hide avoidable risk.

Published July 28, 2026Approx. 1,700 wordsEnglish

Reverse purchasing is not a single checkout. It is a chain of decisions: find a product on a Chinese domestic marketplace, authorize a China-based service to buy or receive it, inspect it at a warehouse, combine it with other items, and choose an international route. Each stage can either reduce uncertainty or quietly add cost. The safest LitBuy workflow therefore begins before payment and ends only after the parcel and evidence are archived.

What “reverse purchasing” means in this guide

Here, reverse purchasing describes an overseas buyer using a China-based purchasing or forwarding service to access products from domestic marketplaces. LitBuy presents two relevant service paths. Its purchasing service supports orders from sources including Taobao, 1688 and WeChat stores, while its forwarding service lets buyers send goods purchased elsewhere to the LitBuy warehouse for consolidation and international shipment.

The distinction matters. In a purchasing order, the agent handles the domestic transaction and can often help communicate with the seller. In a forwarding order, you control the original purchase, so you also carry more responsibility for the seller, address details, product declaration and domestic delivery record.

1. Start with landed cost, not the product price

A cheap listing can become an expensive parcel when domestic delivery, warehouse options, international freight, payment charges, taxes and dimensional weight are added. Before ordering, create a simple landed-cost range rather than one optimistic number.

  • Product layer: item price, domestic freight and possible seller-return freight.
  • Warehouse layer: optional photographs, measurements, repacking or reinforcement.
  • International layer: chargeable weight, route surcharge, insurance and destination taxes.

Use three estimates: best case, expected case and conservative case. The conservative version should allow for a larger carton, an exchange, a route restriction or the loss of a temporary coupon. This makes it easier to reject a marginal item before sunk costs influence the decision.

2. Choose purchasing or forwarding deliberately

Use LitBuy’s purchasing route when you want the platform to place the domestic order, track it into the warehouse and provide a more unified after-sales trail. It is especially useful when the seller accepts only Chinese payment methods or when the listing requires communication.

Use forwarding when you already have a reliable domestic purchasing method, need to receive several Chinese deliveries at one warehouse, or are ordering from a source that is easier to handle directly. Before forwarding, copy the warehouse address exactly, preserve the domestic tracking number and make sure the recipient identifier required by your LitBuy account is present.

3. Audit the product link before paying

Do not treat a product link as permanent evidence. Chinese marketplace listings can change options, replace images or disappear. Save the listing title, seller name, selected SKU, color, size, quantity, price and key description screenshots at checkout. For size-sensitive goods, save the size chart and note how measurements are taken.

Then screen the seller and listing for practical warning signs: vague material claims, inconsistent photos, unusually low prices, missing dimensions, mixed products under one URL and no visible return terms. For electronics, verify model number, plug type, voltage and included accessories.

This record becomes the comparison standard when QC photos arrive.

4. Run a restriction check before the domestic order

LitBuy’s shipping estimator asks for destination, weight, product type and package dimensions because route availability depends on more than kilograms. Its published guidance also distinguishes prohibited and restricted goods. Examples include tobacco and alcohol, many knives and sharp objects, certain foods, liquids, pastes, powders, medicines and products that may infringe intellectual-property rights.

Do this check before payment, not after the item reaches the warehouse. Search the estimator using the correct product category and destination. If the item contains a battery, magnet, liquid, powder, aerosol, food ingredient or branded design, assume it needs additional confirmation.

LitBuy product pages may also show risk notices when an item or keyword is unsupported because of logistics limits, regional brand controls or supplier qualification review. Treat such a notice as a stop signal, not a challenge to work around.

5. Use warehouse QC as a decision gate

LitBuy states that warehouse arrival includes inspection and QC pictures. That is valuable, but a warehouse photograph is visual evidence, not authentication, material testing or a durability guarantee. Use it to answer specific questions:

  • Did the correct color, size, model and quantity arrive?
  • Are obvious stains, tears, cracks, deformation or missing parts visible?
  • Do labels and measurements match the saved order record?
  • Does the product need a closer angle, ruler photo or functional check?

Review full views before zooming into details. Compare both shoes, both sleeves, both sides of a bag and all components of a set. Warehouse lighting can alter color and reflections, so request a second angle when a mark is ambiguous. For fit-critical clothing or footwear, a measurement is more useful than a size label alone.

6. Decide quickly: accept, exchange or return

The cheapest time to solve a product problem is usually before international shipment. Make the decision using severity and reversibility.

Accept minor cosmetic issues that do not affect use and are consistent with the price. Exchange an item when the seller appears able to supply the correct size or an undamaged unit. Return when the wrong product arrived, the listing materially misrepresented it, or an exchange would consume too much time and domestic freight.

LitBuy’s Help Center includes guidance for cancellation, return or exchange and after-sales situations such as non-delivery, missing goods and damage. Follow the live account deadline because seller return windows can be shorter than warehouse storage periods. Describe a problem factually—“ordered XL, received M; left seam is open”—and attach the clearest image.

7. Consolidate around compatibility, not just volume

Consolidation can reduce repeated first-weight charges, but a larger parcel is not always cheaper. Group items that can use the same route and protection level. Separate batteries, liquids, fragile goods or oversized products when their restrictions would eliminate better routes for ordinary clothing.

LitBuy’s homepage describes storage of up to 120 days with the first 90 days free, while the Help Center emphasizes a 90-day free-storage period and notes fees after the overdue window. Use 90 days as the planning ceiling, verify the current account rules, and avoid treating the absolute maximum as free time.

8. Estimate shipping with finished-parcel logic

LitBuy’s estimator uses destination, product type, weight in grams and dimensions in centimeters. Many routes use a first-weight and additional-weight structure, and bulky parcels may be affected by dimensional calculations.

Before submitting a parcel, compare at least two realistic routes using the same inputs. Record:

  • actual packed weight and outer dimensions;
  • first-weight and additional-weight increments;
  • dimensional or volumetric rule, if shown;
  • product restrictions, tracking and delivery range;
  • insurance, packaging and other optional charges;
  • the final payable amount after any coupon.

A route with a lower headline rate can be more expensive when it rounds aggressively or handles volume poorly. Compare final checkout totals, not promotional percentages.

9. Give packaging instructions that balance size and protection

LitBuy says packing and reinforcement are selected according to item size. Improve the result by identifying what can be removed and what must stay. Shoe boxes, hangers and presentation packaging add weight and volume, but they can also protect shape. Soft clothing may tolerate compression; structured bags, glasses, electronics and collectibles may not.

A useful instruction is specific: remove seller shipping bags, fold non-collectible boxes, keep the shoe trees, waterproof the clothing and reinforce the corners. “Make it as small as possible” can produce a cheap but poorly protected parcel. Photograph or save the final parcel measurements so later estimates are based on your own history rather than generic social-media rates.

10. Keep a parcel evidence file

Before payment, save the final item list, warehouse weights, carton dimensions, selected route, declared description, insurance choice, coupon terms and checkout breakdown. After dispatch, add the parcel number and tracking events. When the parcel arrives, record any external damage before opening and film the unpacking of high-value or fragile goods.

This evidence supports after-sales questions about missing items, damage or delivery. It also improves the next order: you learn which categories created volume, which packaging requests worked and what the effective shipping cost was per chargeable kilogram.

A practical LitBuy reverse-purchasing checklist

  1. Save the listing, selected SKU and seller details.
  2. Estimate landed cost with a conservative range.
  3. Choose purchasing or forwarding based on who should control the domestic order.
  4. Check destination and product restrictions before paying.
  5. Match every warehouse arrival to the saved order record.
  6. Review QC images and request only decision-critical extras.
  7. Resolve exchanges and returns before parcel submission.
  8. Consolidate products with compatible route and protection needs.
  9. Compare shipping lines using final weight, dimensions and total payable cost.
  10. Archive parcel evidence through delivery and inspection.

Frequently asked questions

Does LitBuy buy from Taobao and 1688?

LitBuy’s official site presents purchasing support for sources including Taobao, 1688 and WeChat stores. Availability for a particular link can still depend on the seller, product risk and current platform rules.

Can I send goods I bought myself to the LitBuy warehouse?

LitBuy describes a forwarding service for goods purchased elsewhere. Use the exact warehouse address and account identifier, and retain domestic tracking so the parcel can be matched.

Are LitBuy QC photos proof that an item is authentic?

No. QC photos help confirm visible condition, option and quantity. They do not replace authentication, material analysis or functional testing.

How long is free storage?

The official homepage and Help Center use slightly different framing, but both point to the first 90 days as the practical free-storage period. Verify the live account terms and do not delay consolidation unnecessarily.

Why can the final shipping price differ from the estimate?

The final carton can have different weight and dimensions from preliminary item data. Route rounding, first-weight rules, dimensional weight, optional services and restrictions can also change the total.

What is the safest way to use a shipping coupon?

Apply it only after the final parcel data is known. Check the cap, eligible route, expiration and excluded fees, then compare the discounted total with other available routes.
