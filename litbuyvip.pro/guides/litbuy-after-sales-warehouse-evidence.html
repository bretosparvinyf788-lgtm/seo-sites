LitBuy After-Sales Evidence: Build a Warehouse Decision File

A factual system for comparing what was ordered with what arrived, requesting purposeful evidence and deciding what happens before parcel submission.

Published August 22, 2026 · 12 minute read · 1,772 words

The most expensive warehouse mistake is an undocumented one

A disappointing warehouse photo does not automatically create a useful after-sales case. Buyers often react with “this looks wrong,” but the seller, purchasing agent and warehouse need a more precise answer: what was ordered, what arrived, what evidence proves the difference, and what outcome is being requested? If those facts are scattered across screenshots and chat messages, the problem becomes harder to evaluate while the order continues moving through its timeline.

LitBuy publicly presents free QC and inspection photos, real-time tracking of storage, inspection and shipping progress, and an after-sales path for issues such as non-delivery, missing items or damage. Its Help Center also lists cancellation and return or exchange guidance for purchasing orders. These features are most useful when the buyer turns them into a disciplined decision file before the item enters an international parcel.

Separate disappointment from a verifiable mismatch

Begin by naming the issue without exaggeration. A verifiable mismatch is a difference between the saved order specification and the received evidence: wrong quantity, visibly different color, incorrect size label, missing listed component, obvious exterior damage or another concrete inconsistency. Disappointment is broader. A fabric may look less impressive than expected, or a color may appear different under warehouse lighting, without the available evidence proving that the seller sent the wrong item.

This distinction is not about accepting poor quality. It is about asking the correct next question. A verifiable mismatch can support a targeted after-sales request. An unclear impression calls for better evidence. If the problem cannot be described in one factual sentence, do not rush to select a remedy. First identify what the existing photos can and cannot establish.

Create a four-part order record before requesting anything

A clean record contains the order baseline, arrival evidence, discrepancy statement and desired outcome. The baseline is the exact variant, quantity, seller description and any pre-order instruction saved when the request was submitted. Arrival evidence includes the warehouse status, received quantity and relevant inspection images. The discrepancy statement compares one baseline fact with one observed fact. The desired outcome states whether you need clarification, another photograph, replacement, return review or a different supported action.

Keep each issue attached to one order line. Combining three products into one complaint makes it difficult to see which evidence belongs to which seller and which remedy is realistic. Give the case a simple internal label, such as the order number plus “size-label” or “missing-accessory.” That label keeps screenshots, notes and later replies together without changing any identifier used inside the live LitBuy account.

Use warehouse photos as evidence, not decoration

Review overview images first. Confirm the number of visible units, outer condition, selected color and recognizable design. Then move to the exact proof point required by the order. For apparel, that may be the size tag or a measurement. For shoes, it may be the box label and outsole length. For an accessory set, it may be a laid-out view showing every included component.

Do not collect more images simply because additional evidence feels safer. Every request should answer a decision question. “Please photograph the care label clearly” is useful when material or size information is relevant. “Please take more pictures” is vague and may still leave the important area hidden. A smaller set of purposeful images creates a stronger record than a large gallery without an identified comparison.

Account for lighting, angle and packaging before alleging a defect

Warehouse photography can reveal visible condition, but it can also create false alarms. Cool lighting may shift color. Folded fabric can resemble uneven construction. Protective film can look like scratching. A compressed parcel can temporarily change an item’s shape. When the concern could be caused by presentation, ask for a specific confirmation rather than treating the first image as final proof.

Use a three-level classification. Confirmed means the evidence clearly shows a mismatch. Unclear means the relevant detail is hidden, distorted or missing. Acceptable means the evidence matches the saved specification within what a photograph can reasonably establish. Only confirmed and unresolved cases need further action. This classification prevents both careless acceptance and unsupported accusations.

Write a discrepancy statement that another person can audit

A strong statement follows a simple pattern: ordered fact, received fact, evidence reference. For example: “The order record shows two units; the warehouse overview shows one unit in the received set.” Another might be: “The selected variant is size L; the visible tag reads M.” Avoid emotional descriptions, assumptions about intent or claims that the photos cannot prove.

Then add the requested next step. If the tag is unreadable, request a clear tag image. If the quantity is visibly short, ask for verification against the inbound package record and the available after-sales options. If damage is visible, identify its location and severity. Clear writing helps support staff evaluate the issue without reconstructing your entire purchasing history.

Choose the remedy only after checking the live order state

LitBuy’s Help Center distinguishes cancellation guidance from after-sales requests, and describes return or exchange rules for purchasing orders. That does not mean every order, seller, product or status has the same available outcome. The practical choice depends on what has happened: the seller may not have shipped, the item may be in domestic transit, the warehouse may have received it, or it may already be included in an international parcel.

Check the live status before requesting a remedy. Do not write “cancel” when the real need is a warehouse discrepancy review, and do not assume a return is available simply because the item remains stored. Read the current instructions shown for the order and submit the request through the supported path. The evidence file prepares the facts; it does not override seller terms or platform rules.

Do not let consolidation erase the decision point

Once an item is approved and submitted inside an international parcel, reversing the upstream purchase decision becomes more difficult. Keep disputed or unclear items outside the active parcel plan. Mark them as “hold for decision” in your own record until the supported after-sales path is complete or you deliberately accept the item.

LitBuy publicly describes items being stored and combined for shipment, with up to 120 days of storage and the first 90 days free. Treat storage as organizational space, not proof that an after-sales opportunity remains open for the same length of time. Seller and order-resolution conditions may follow different timelines. Review a new arrival promptly and make the warehouse decision while the transaction history is still easy to trace.

Preserve an acceptance note for items you decide to keep

Not every imperfect item should become a return request. Sometimes the evidence shows a minor difference that the buyer accepts after considering cost, time, intended use and the uncertainty of another order. Record that decision too. Write what was observed, why it was accepted and whether the item needs special packing.

An acceptance note prevents the same concern from resurfacing when you later review the parcel. It also improves future buying decisions. If a seller’s sizing repeatedly differs from the chart, that is useful research. If warehouse lighting caused the concern, you can design a better photo request next time. A closed case should leave behind a lesson, not just a forgotten screenshot.

Build a reusable warehouse decision checklist

For every arrival, verify the order line, selected variant and expected quantity. Match the domestic tracking record where applicable. Review overview photos, then inspect one or two product-specific proof points. Classify the result as acceptable, unclear or confirmed mismatch. If action is needed, create the four-part record and keep the item out of parcel submission.

After a response arrives, update the case with the date, explanation and supported outcome. Save replacement or refund status only when it is actually reflected in the account. If the item is kept, record acceptance. If it leaves the warehouse through a supported return process, close the item in the parcel plan. This small routine creates a reliable bridge between product discovery and international shipping.

Frequently asked questions

Do QC photos guarantee that an item has no defects?

No. Photos can show visible condition and labels, but they cannot prove hidden construction, long-term durability, internal electronics or every material claim.

What should I do when the color looks wrong?

Compare the listing and saved variant, then consider lighting and camera exposure. If the difference remains important, request a targeted confirmation under clearer conditions.

Is one overview photo enough for a missing-item claim?

It may show a quantity issue, but the best record also identifies the expected quantity and asks for verification against the inbound package or order record.

Should I submit the rest of my parcel while one item is disputed?

Keep the disputed item outside the parcel. Whether other approved items should move depends on your plan, but do not accidentally include the unresolved product.

Can I assume a return because the item is still in storage?

No. Storage status and return eligibility are different questions. Check the live order state and current supported after-sales options.

How many extra photos should I request?

Request only the views needed to answer the decision question. One precise image can be more useful than several general photographs.

What if the seller sent a different size?

Save the selected size, capture the visible received label and write a direct discrepancy statement before reviewing the available remedy.

Does damaged outer packaging prove the product is damaged?

No. Document the packaging, then inspect the product itself. The two conditions should be recorded separately.

Why record an item I decided to accept?

An acceptance note closes the case, preserves the reason and helps improve seller selection, sizing choices or photo requests on the next order.

What is the most important after-sales evidence?

The strongest evidence is a clear comparison between a saved order fact and a visible received fact, tied to the correct order line and status.

A good case file turns uncertainty into a decision

The warehouse is not merely a waiting room between purchase and shipping. It is the last practical checkpoint where the buyer can compare the promised item with the received item before committing it to an international parcel. Use that checkpoint deliberately.

Save the order baseline, review targeted evidence, describe discrepancies factually and choose a remedy only after checking the live status and current rules. Whether the result is clarification, after-sales action or informed acceptance, the process should end with a documented decision. That is how a spreadsheet becomes more than a link collection: it becomes a record of why each item earned a place in the final parcel.
