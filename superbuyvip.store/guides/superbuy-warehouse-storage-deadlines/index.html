SUPERBUY WAREHOUSE STORAGE DEADLINES.

A practical aging-control system for tracking every stocked item before storage fees, extensions and maximum deadlines narrow your choices.

SuperbuyVIP EditorialPublished September 6, 2026Independent guideApprox. 1,627 words

A Superbuy warehouse balance can look harmless because nothing is moving and no international parcel has been submitted. That calm screen hides a running clock. Each stocked item has an arrival date, a free-storage window, a paid-storage phase and a final decision deadline. When a cart is built from several sellers, those clocks rarely start together. The oldest item can become urgent while the newest purchase is still traveling inside China.

The practical mistake is treating storage as one account-level allowance. Superbuy’s current fee guide describes storage at item level: 90 days are free after arrival, storage beyond that period is charged daily, and the normal maximum storage period is 180 days. An optional extension can move the maximum deadline for a selected product, but it does not restore free storage. A buyer therefore needs an item-by-item exit plan, not a vague reminder to ship someday.

Start the clock from warehouse arrival, not purchase day

The marketplace payment date is useful for seller follow-up, but it is not the date to use for warehouse aging. One seller may dispatch the next morning while another takes two weeks. A pre-sale can take longer. The storage calendar becomes meaningful when the item is stocked into the warehouse and appears in the account with its warehouse record.

Create one row for every warehouse item. Record the order number, short product name, stock-in date, free-storage end date, normal maximum date and current decision status. Do not merge five units into one spreadsheet row merely because they came from one seller. Superbuy’s published overdue charge is calculated per item, so quantity affects the exposure.

Use three dates for every item: warehouse arrival, day 90 and day 180. The first records evidence; the second changes the cost; the third changes whether the item can remain under the normal policy.

Understand what changes after day 90

Superbuy’s current fee structure states that items receive 90 days of free storage after arrival. Once that free period ends, the listed storage charge is CN¥0.1 per item per day. This is a small daily number, which is exactly why buyers underestimate it. The relevant calculation is not the charge on one T-shirt for one day. It is the number of overdue items multiplied by the number of overdue days.

Suppose a warehouse holds 18 items for 24 days beyond their individual free periods. At the published rate, the storage total would be CN¥43.2: 18 × 24 × CN¥0.1. That example assumes every item is overdue for all 24 days. In a real account, arrivals differ, so calculate each row separately and then total the results. A newly stocked item should not be charged the same aging days as an older one.

Do not wait until day 90 to begin parcel planning. Use day 60 as the first review point. By then, the buyer should know which items passed inspection, which are awaiting seller action and which are restricted from the intended line. Day 75 is a decision point: either prepare the parcel, arrange a domestic resolution or approve a deliberate extension. Day 85 is for execution, not more browsing.

Treat day 180 as a control deadline

The same official guide gives 180 days as the normal maximum storage period. It also warns that an item can be treated as abandoned and disposed of when no parcel-shipping order is submitted and no feedback is provided after that point. This is not simply a higher storage-fee tier. It is an operational deadline that can end the buyer’s ability to keep postponing a decision.

Build a red-alert column that calculates the days remaining until the normal maximum. Sort it from the smallest number upward at least once a week. An account containing one urgent item and twenty comfortable items is still urgent. A total-item count or total-value figure will not reveal that risk unless the earliest deadline is visible.

Do not assume a customer-service message automatically pauses the clock. If an item has a defect case, return request or route restriction, record the case number, latest reply and next action. Ask for a clear outcome while enough time remains to execute it. A conversation that has not produced a warehouse action is not an exit plan.

Use storage extension for a defined reason

Superbuy currently lists an “Extend Storage Period” value-added service at CN¥10 per DI per month, with a maximum extension of six months for an individual product. The description makes an important distinction: this service extends the product’s maximum storage period. It does not extend the 90-day free period. Daily storage fees continue after free storage ends and are payable when the parcel is submitted.

That makes extension a deadline tool, not a cost reset. It can be rational when a matching pre-sale item has a known release date, when a documented after-sales case needs more time, or when a planned parcel is waiting for one specific purchase. It is weak planning when the reason is merely “I may buy more later.” Open-ended shopping can consume the entire extension without producing a shippable parcel.

Before purchasing an extension, write four fields: reason, requested months, target exit date and expected parcel group. If the target date is not earlier than the new maximum, the plan is already too loose. Compare the extension fee and continued daily storage with the cost of shipping the ready items now. Sometimes a smaller first parcel is cheaper than holding many completed items for one uncertain arrival.

Separate readiness from compatibility

An item can be approved after QC and still be unsuitable for the same parcel as the rest of the warehouse. Batteries, liquids, powders, fragile products and unusually bulky goods can narrow international routes. Storage pressure should not push incompatible goods into one submission. First mark each item as ready, then assign a compatible route family.

Use four decision states: ready to ship, waiting for evidence, domestic action required and not suitable for the planned route. “In warehouse” is a location, not a decision state. A stocked item without a decision should appear on an exception list until the buyer approves it, requests additional evidence, returns it or assigns another exit.

Consolidation is useful only after compatibility is established. Superbuy offers free consolidation of multiple warehouse items, but the international fee still depends on final parcel data, selected line and destination. Keeping an old item for consolidation is not automatically a saving. The additional storage, possible extension and loss of route flexibility must be considered beside any reduction in repeated first-weight charges.

Make returns part of the storage calendar

A visible defect, wrong variation or quantity problem should be handled while the domestic seller’s after-sales route is still workable. The warehouse clock and the seller’s return clock are separate. Waiting until an item nears its Superbuy storage limit can make a domestic return much harder even though the item still appears in the warehouse.

For every unresolved item, record the evidence required, the date the issue was reported, the seller or agent response and the last acceptable domestic action date. If additional photos or re-inspection would change the decision, request them early. The objective is not to collect more pictures; it is to reach a yes-or-no outcome before the remaining options narrow.

Some items may need to be separated from a larger warehouse order. Superbuy’s current value-added service table includes a split-order or discard-item service, displayed at CN¥2 per DI. Its purpose includes separating gifts, batteries or liquids, or discarding selected contents. Use such a service only after identifying exactly which unit must move and why. A vague discard instruction can destroy the wrong evidence or item.

Run a weekly warehouse aging review

A useful review takes fifteen minutes and follows the same order. First, sort by days remaining to the end of free storage. Second, isolate every item without a completed inspection decision. Third, group approved items by compatible route. Fourth, compare shipping now with the cost and purpose of holding. Finally, assign one next action and one date to every exception.

Add three simple dashboard totals: items that will leave free storage within 14 days, items already accruing daily storage and items within 30 days of their normal maximum. These numbers are more useful than total items in storage because they describe approaching decisions. Also show the oldest stock-in date at the top of the sheet.

Keep costs in separate columns. Record estimated international freight, overdue storage already accumulated, future storage through the target ship date and any extension service. Do not bury storage inside a general “fees” cell. A visible cost line makes it easier to stop waiting when the expected consolidation saving has already been consumed.

Build the exit plan before adding another order

Before paying for another spreadsheet find, check the oldest warehouse item. Estimate the new seller’s domestic dispatch time, warehouse arrival and inspection decision date. If that schedule pushes existing goods beyond day 90 or close to day 180, the new purchase needs a stronger reason than a low product price.

The safest rule is that every purchase enters a named parcel group before payment. The group can change after stock-in, but it must have an intended destination, route family and target submission week. This prevents the warehouse from becoming an unplanned collection of bargains with unrelated deadlines.

Superbuy’s storage policy is generous when it is treated as planning space. Ninety free days allow different domestic orders to arrive and be checked; the later paid period provides additional time to resolve exceptions. Neither period should replace ownership. Track each item from arrival, price the delay, use extensions only for defined cases and give every warehouse record a dated exit. The result is a smaller storage risk, a cleaner parcel decision and fewer purchases lost to procrastination.
