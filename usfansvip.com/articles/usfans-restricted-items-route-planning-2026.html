USFans Restricted Items Guide 2026: Screen Liquids, Batteries and Fragile Goods Before Parcel Submission

Published September 8, 202612 min read1,500–1,800 word practical guide
RESTRICTION SCREEN

Identify risky product characteristics before payment, protect ordinary route choices and submit only documented parcel combinations.

The cheapest time to solve a shipping restriction is before the product is purchased. Once a questionable item reaches the USFans warehouse, the buyer has already paid the seller, waited for Chinese domestic delivery and started the return clock. At that point, discovering that a liquid, battery, glass panel or food product cannot use the intended international route is not a small shipping surprise. It can change the entire parcel plan.

A reliable USFans restricted-items workflow therefore begins with classification, not checkout. The goal is not to memorize a permanent blacklist. Airline rules, customs policies, safety requirements, destinations and carrier acceptance can interact differently. The practical goal is to identify the product characteristic that creates risk, ask the right question early, and keep uncertain goods away from ordinary items until route eligibility is clear.

Separate prohibited, restricted and route-limited goods

Buyers often use “restricted” to describe three different outcomes. A prohibited product is not accepted. USFans product guidance currently states that tobacco, alcohol and contraband goods are not accepted. A restricted product may still move only under special arrangements, packaging or selected lines. A route-limited product may be acceptable to one destination through one carrier but unavailable through another.

This distinction matters because each outcome requires a different decision. A prohibited item should be removed from the buying plan. A restricted item needs confirmation before purchase or shipment. A route-limited item needs a compatible parcel design. Treating all three as ordinary merchandise and hoping the warehouse will sort them out transfers every avoidable cost to the end of the process.

Build an item-risk record before payment

Create one row for each product and record more than its name. Add the destination country, material, form, battery specification, liquid volume, food or supplement status, fragility, dimensions and whether the seller provides safe retail packaging. The description “beauty device” is not enough: a corded appliance, a rechargeable device with an installed lithium battery and a device bundled with liquid serum may produce three different route results.

Keep the live listing description and variation selected at payment. For electronics, capture the battery chemistry, capacity and whether the cell is installed, detachable or shipped loose. For cosmetics, record the container size and whether the product is liquid, cream, paste, gel, aerosol or powder. For fragile goods, record glass, ceramic and screen components instead of relying on the seller’s general product category.

Handle liquids, powders, pastes and gels as specifications

USFans identifies liquids, powders, pastes and gels among commonly restricted goods. Its purchasing guidance also says medicines, liquids, creams, powders and cosmetics face Chinese export limitations and advises checking with support before purchase. The useful question is therefore not “Can cosmetics ship?” It is “Can this exact product, in this quantity and form, ship to my destination through an available route?”

Do not hide a restricted characteristic behind a broad label. A face cream remains a cream; a drink concentrate remains a liquid; loose pigment remains powder. Record the actual form and volume. If support confirms eligibility, retain the answer with the item record and do not assume it covers a different size, formulation or destination. If eligibility is unclear before checkout, choose an ordinary alternative rather than converting a small product saving into return fees and lost time.

Treat batteries as a route-planning variable

USFans lists battery-containing products, especially loose or high-capacity batteries, among common restricted categories. Buyers should separate four questions: Does the item contain a battery? Is it installed in equipment or loose? What type and capacity is it? Which current routes accept that configuration to the destination? A seller photo showing a charging cable does not answer any of these questions.

Ask the seller or purchasing agent for an objective specification when the listing is incomplete. Avoid combining an uncertain power bank, spare cell or rechargeable accessory with an otherwise simple clothing parcel. One battery item can reduce available lines for every other item in the package. Confirm it separately, then decide whether a split parcel preserves better choices for the rest of the order.

Plan fragile goods around both acceptance and protection

USFans identifies glass and ceramic products, screens, monitors and large glass panels as restricted categories. Physical protection and transport eligibility are related but not identical. Reinforcement can reduce movement or impact exposure, but it does not make an item acceptable to a carrier that excludes large glass panels. First confirm that a suitable route exists; only then design the packaging.

Use warehouse evidence to check the actual construction and seller packaging. A decorative item described as resin may include a glass cover. A lamp may combine a fragile shade and electrical components. A framed print may use glass rather than acrylic. When requesting packaging services, describe the risk specifically: protect corners, immobilize the inner product, retain or remove seller packaging as appropriate, and reinforce the outer carton. General requests such as “pack well” leave too much interpretation.

Screen food, supplements and perishables before purchase

Food, supplements and perishable goods appear in USFans’ common restriction guidance. These products add more than leakage or spoilage risk; destination customs rules can depend on ingredients, origin, labeling and quantity. A route that carries ordinary packaged snacks does not automatically prove that seeds, meat products, fresh goods or supplements are acceptable.

Use a strict rule: if the product is consumed, applied for a health purpose, biologically active or time-sensitive, verify it before payment. Do not buy a large quantity merely because the unit price is low. Start with confirmation of the exact product and destination. If documentation, ingredients or shelf life cannot be established, the item is a poor candidate for an international-agent parcel.

Protect the ordinary items in a mixed order

A mixed parcel should not be built around its most difficult product by accident. Group items into three buckets: ordinary goods with broad route availability, confirmed restricted goods with a documented compatible route, and unresolved goods that remain outside parcel submission. This prevents a single cosmetic, battery or fragile component from holding clothing, shoes and accessories that could otherwise ship normally.

Compare the route and packaging consequences of combining versus splitting. Consolidation may reduce repeated base charges, but a special route can carry a different price, delivery expectation or weight rule. A split parcel is justified when it protects route choice, isolates leakage or breakage risk, or prevents one unresolved item from delaying the entire order. Make that decision from the available routes, not from a blanket belief that one parcel is always cheaper.

Use the warehouse and parcel-submission stages deliberately

USFans’ beginner process tells buyers to select warehouse items, submit for delivery, fill in the delivery details and choose from recommended delivery types. It notes that some items are restricted with certain delivery methods and that suitable options are presented from the available list. This makes the route list an item-and-destination result, not a general promise that every product can use every line.

The same official guidance mentions optional package removal, reinforcement and insurance services that can be requested in the parcel remark. Use those services for defined purposes. Package removal may reduce useless volume but can remove protective retail packing. Reinforcement may help a fragile shipment but add size or weight. Insurance may manage covered transport risks, yet USFans warns that parcels containing unconfirmed prohibited or restricted goods may be delayed, returned, seized or destroyed and may not be covered.

A twelve-point restricted-items check

  1. Identify the final destination before selecting the product.
  2. Record the exact variation, material, form and quantity.
  3. Flag liquids, powders, pastes, gels, batteries, glass, screens, food and supplements.
  4. Reject tobacco, alcohol, contraband and other clearly prohibited goods.
  5. Ask about medicine, cosmetics or unclear restricted characteristics before payment.
  6. Save the product specification and any support confirmation.
  7. Inspect the warehouse record for unexpected risky components.
  8. Keep unresolved items outside a mixed parcel.
  9. Review only the routes currently offered for the actual item combination.
  10. Compare consolidation with a protective split-parcel plan.
  11. Request precise removal, reinforcement or insurance services when appropriate.
  12. Keep the route, declaration, packing request and final parcel record together.

Frequently asked questions about USFans restricted items

Does restricted mean the item is completely prohibited?

Not always. A restricted item may require a particular route, quantity, packing method or prior approval, while a prohibited item is not accepted. Treat the route shown for your destination and item combination as the operational answer.

Which products does USFans identify as commonly restricted?

USFans currently lists liquids, powders, pastes, gels, fragile glass or ceramic goods, screens, large glass panels, battery-containing items, food, supplements, perishables and goods without safe packaging among common restricted categories.

Are tobacco and alcohol accepted?

USFans product guidance states that tobacco, alcohol and contraband items are not accepted. Do not purchase them expecting warehouse arrival to create a shipping solution.

Should I ask support before buying medicine or cosmetics?

Yes. USFans says medicine, liquids, creams, powders and cosmetics are limited under Chinese export rules and advises contacting support before purchase.

Can an item with a battery ship through every route?

No. Battery type, capacity, whether it is installed or loose, the destination and the carrier can all affect route eligibility. Confirm the exact product specification before purchase and select only a route offered for that item.

Why should restricted goods be kept out of a mixed parcel until verified?

One uncertain item can remove otherwise suitable routes or hold the entire parcel. Verification before consolidation protects the shipping options of ordinary clothing, shoes and accessories.

Does reinforcement make a restricted product unrestricted?

No. Reinforcement may improve physical protection, but it cannot override airline, customs or safety restrictions. Eligibility and packaging are separate decisions.

What can happen if a restricted item is submitted without confirmation?

USFans warns that the parcel may be delayed, returned, seized or destroyed and that the loss may not be covered by shipping insurance.

Should I rely on an old parcel as proof that a product is still eligible?

No. Routes and acceptance rules can change by destination, carrier and current regulation. Recheck the present route options for every new parcel.

What evidence should I keep before submitting the parcel?

Keep the live product description, ingredient or battery specification, warehouse item record, support answer when needed, selected route, requested packing service and final parcel declaration.

Make eligibility a purchase decision

Restricted-item control is not a last-minute customs form. It starts when the buyer reads the listing and identifies what the product actually is. Classify risky characteristics, confirm uncertain goods before paying, preserve ordinary route choices by separating unresolved items, and use packaging services only after transport eligibility is established. That sequence turns a vague restriction warning into a documented decision that can be checked before money, time and an entire parcel are committed.
