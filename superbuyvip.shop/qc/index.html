Warehouse evidence

Superbuy QC Photos Guide

Understand standard warehouse photos, decide when a detailed image is worth buying and inspect products before the seller after-sales window narrows.

A QC photo is warehouse evidence, not a beauty shot and not a certificate of authenticity. Its value comes from answering a question that changes your next action.

What the standard QC service covers

Current Superbuy public guidance states that three standard photos are taken after warehouse inspection. They can confirm the broad product type, color, quantity and obvious visible condition. They are not guaranteed to show a measurement, hidden component, size label, close-up stitch or custom angle.

Use an evidence ladder

  1. Compare the listing with the variation you ordered.
  2. Use the standard photos to confirm broad identity and visible condition.
  3. Request one detailed image only when an unresolved fact could change the decision.
  4. Approve, exchange or return before the relevant after-sales deadline.

Ask one question per paid photo

A vague request such as “take more pictures” often produces another angle that still does not answer the concern. A stronger instruction identifies the subject, viewpoint and success condition: “Place the removable insole beside a ruler, photograph it directly from above, and keep both ends and the centimeter marks visible.”

ProductHigh-value request
ShoesInsole length beside a ruler; size label; toe-box symmetry.
HoodiesChest width; embroidery close-up; wash tag.
BagsHardware, zipper, lining and strap attachment.
ElectronicsModel label, plug type and supported power-on proof.

Separate objective defects from preference

A wrong size, missing part, broken component, stain or model mismatch is objective evidence. A color that appears different under warehouse lighting may be a preference question. For uncertain color, request a neutral-light image with a white or gray reference rather than relying on a single processed seller photo.

Set the return threshold in advance

Define what would make you return the item before the photos arrive. Examples include the wrong labeled size, a measurement outside a stated range, missing hardware or visible damage larger than a chosen threshold. This reduces endless photo requests for details that would not actually change the outcome.

Approve, investigate or return

Approve when the evidence meets the threshold. Investigate when one unresolved detail matters. Return when the mismatch is already clear. The investigate category should be temporary; it is not an invitation to collect decorative photographs.

One-photo rule: one image should answer one decisive question. Combining label, stitching, measurement, packaging and color into one request usually produces weak evidence for all five.
