International parcel workflow

Superbuy Tracking Guide 2026

Read tracking as a chain of custody from warehouse dispatch through export, customs, carrier handoff and final delivery.

September 11, 202612 min readIndependent guide

Superbuy tracking becomes easier when you stop treating every quiet period as the same problem. A parcel can be waiting for packing, export, transport, customs, a carrier handoff or final delivery. The useful question is not simply “Where is it?” but “Which stage was last confirmed, and who controls the next scan?”

Separate order tracking from parcel tracking

An order and an international parcel are different records. The order covers the buying stage: payment, agent purchase, seller dispatch, Chinese domestic delivery, inspection and warehouse storage. The parcel begins after stored items are selected, packed and submitted for international delivery. A seller delay belongs to the order timeline; a quiet export scan belongs to the parcel timeline.

This distinction prevents support requests from reaching the wrong workflow. Before the warehouse receives an item, record the order number and domestic tracking. After international dispatch, record the Superbuy parcel number, the international carrier number and any later local-carrier number.

Build a tracking identity card

Keep one short record for each parcel. Include the Superbuy parcel number, submission date, dispatch date, selected route, destination, item count, declared contents summary and every tracking identifier. Add the receiver name and postcode exactly as submitted, but do not place sensitive address details in public posts.

Read the eight international tracking stages

1. Parcel submitted

The warehouse has received the delivery request, but the carton may not yet have been packed or released. A price paid at this point can be a shipping deposit based on estimated parcel information. It is too early to expect an external carrier scan.

2. Packed and dispatched

Superbuy consolidates the selected items, applies the approved packing instructions, verifies the carton and sends it to the logistics channel. The carrier number may appear before the carrier posts a public event. Label creation is not the same as physical acceptance.

3. Export processing

The parcel can move through a warehouse transfer, route consolidator, security screening or export facility. Batch processing may create a gap between physical movement and the next public scan. Use the route’s current handling information, not a fixed assumption copied from another line.

4. International transport

Air or other line-haul movement may not create a visible scan at every step. “Departed,” “airline received” or similar wording should be read as a stage marker, not a promised flight or arrival time. Transport schedules, capacity and operational changes can affect the next event.

5. Import and customs

Arrival in the destination country does not mean the parcel is ready for delivery. Import processing and customs review may occur before a domestic carrier accepts it. Public tracking can remain quiet while documents or physical goods are being processed.

6. Carrier handoff

The international logistics provider may transfer the parcel to a postal service or local courier. This is the point where a second tracking number sometimes becomes important. Check the latest event for a partner name or reference rather than repeatedly searching only the original number.

7. Last-mile delivery

The local carrier controls the address validation, delivery attempt, collection notice and final scan. Confirm the postcode, apartment or unit and receiver access. If the status reports an address exception or failed attempt, contact the named last-mile carrier promptly.

8. Delivered and after-sales window

Inspect the parcel condition before discarding packaging. If the carton is crushed, wet, opened or resealed, photograph it before moving the contents. Match the items against the parcel manifest and preserve evidence needed by the current after-sales or insurance process.

Why tracking can stop updating

A scan gap can mean that the parcel is between systems rather than stationary. Common causes include pre-advice created before carrier acceptance, consolidated export batches, transport without public intermediate scans, customs processing, a weekend or operational closure, and a handoff to a local network that uses another identifier.

The wording matters. “Information received” suggests that shipment data exists but physical acceptance may not be confirmed. “Departure” indicates progress from a facility, not continuous live location. “Customs processing” is different from seizure or rejection. “Delivery attempted” is a last-mile event that usually requires receiver action rather than Superbuy warehouse action.

A quiet page becomes concerning when it exceeds the current route’s normal stage range, contradicts another carrier record, or shows a specific exception. Measure from the last confirmed physical event, not from the moment you refreshed the page.

Use a stage-based escalation clock

A universal “contact support after three days” rule is too blunt for international delivery. Build the clock around the stage. Before carrier acceptance, compare the parcel submission and dispatch information. During export, compare the route’s current handling notes. During customs, watch for a request for documents or payment. After handoff, use the local carrier’s status and contact method.

Compare multiple tracking sources carefully

Superbuy’s public parcel tracker accepts a tracking number or parcel number, and the account’s My Parcel area is the primary record for the submitted shipment. The named carrier may provide more detailed movement after it accepts the parcel. A destination carrier may become the best source after handoff.

When two trackers disagree, compare timestamps, locations and identifier types. One system may have a delayed feed while another shows the newest physical event. Do not treat a generic third-party tracker’s prediction as stronger than an official carrier scan. Save the conflicting entries if you need support to clarify the handoff.

Prepare a useful support message

A message that says only “tracking not updating” forces the support team to reconstruct the case. Provide the Superbuy parcel number, carrier tracking number, route, destination country, dispatch date, last physical event, number of quiet days and the exact question you need answered.

A useful structure is: “Parcel [number] was dispatched by [route]. Tracking [number] last showed [event] at [location] on [date]. No newer physical scan appears in My Parcel or the carrier record. Please confirm whether the parcel is awaiting export, transport, customs or a handoff, and whether a new local number or receiver action exists.”

Superbuy’s Help Center asks users to provide a relevant order number or username when seeking help. For a parcel case, supplying the parcel number and dated tracking evidence makes the request more precise.

Handle customs and delivery exceptions differently

A customs stage is not automatically a loss. Watch for an official request tied to the parcel and verify who issued it. A receiver may need to provide accurate information or pay an amount that the selected route did not include. Keep the product records and parcel manifest consistent.

An address exception belongs to the last-mile workflow. Contact the named carrier, confirm the submitted address and ask whether redelivery or collection is available. A damage event requires physical evidence: outer carton, shipping label, internal packing and affected item. A missing-item claim requires the manifest, unpacking evidence when available and the original QC record.

Tracking quality starts before shipment

When comparing routes, record whether end-to-end tracking is included, which carrier stages are visible and whether a local handoff is expected. A cheaper route can be rational, but the lower price should not be mistaken for identical visibility. If the parcel has a deadline or contains difficult-to-replace goods, tracking and compensation terms deserve explicit weight in the route decision.

Save the route description on the payment date. Superbuy states that international delivery is operated by designated third parties and that processing time, restrictions, customs tax and other delivery events cannot be fully controlled. That makes current route evidence more useful than a permanent promise.

Delivery-day evidence checklist

  1. Capture the final tracking status and delivery time.
  2. Photograph visible outer-carton damage before opening.
  3. Keep the shipping label readable in at least one image.
  4. Record any broken seal, moisture or resealing.
  5. Compare all items and quantities with the parcel manifest.
  6. Photograph internal protection before removing it.
  7. Test affected items only when safe.
  8. Preserve packaging until the case is resolved.
  9. Open the current after-sales request within the applicable period.
  10. Submit one chronological evidence set rather than scattered messages.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I track a Superbuy parcel?

After the parcel is sent, Superbuy directs users to User Center and My Parcel. Its public tracking tool also accepts a tracking number or parcel number.

Why is my Superbuy tracking not updating?

The parcel may be between carrier scans, awaiting export transport, in customs, or moving through a handoff that uses another tracking number. Identify the last confirmed stage before escalating.

Is the Superbuy parcel number the same as the carrier tracking number?

Not always. Keep the Superbuy parcel number, the international carrier number and any later local-carrier number as separate identifiers.

When should I contact Superbuy about tracking?

Contact support when the route’s current handling or delivery range has clearly been exceeded, a scan reports an exception, or the carrier needs information you cannot supply. Include the parcel number and a dated timeline.

What evidence should I keep for a lost or damaged parcel?

Keep the manifest, QC images, packing information, route terms, payment record, tracking history and delivery-condition photos. Follow the live after-sales and insurance requirements.

Final tracking rule

Treat tracking as a chain of custody. Preserve every identifier, identify the last confirmed physical stage, find the party responsible for the next event and escalate with a dated question. That method separates an ordinary scan gap from a customs request, handoff problem or genuine delivery exception.
