Superbuy Restricted Items & Parcel Risk Checklist 2026: Avoid Route Dead Ends, Return Problems & Surprise Fees

Use a decision-first workflow to manage restricted products, warehouse evidence, returns, packaging and international shipping before small problems become expensive parcel mistakes.

Published August 3, 202613 min read1,598 wordsIndependent buyer guide

A cheap product can become an expensive mistake before the parcel leaves China. The risk usually appears as a chain: the wrong purchasing method, a route-sensitive item, a missed warehouse message, a vague QC request, unsuitable packaging, and a parcel submitted before the buyer understands the available lines. This guide turns Superbuy’s current public rules into a practical 2026 risk checklist.

Start by classifying the purchase, not the product

Most buyers begin with the item itself: shoes, clothing, electronics, cosmetics, or collectibles. A better first question is where the item is being purchased. Superbuy’s current fee structure separates standard purchasing from more complicated sources.

Purchases from mainstream platforms such as Taobao, Tmall, JD.com, and 1688 are currently listed without a basic purchasing service fee. That does not mean the order is free. The buyer still pays the item price, Chinese domestic delivery when charged by the seller, payment-related costs, optional services, international freight, and any destination taxes.

Second-hand and less standardized marketplaces require more caution. Superbuy currently lists a flat CN¥20 service fee per order for several second-hand platforms, while some other second-hand or unlisted sources use percentage-based fees within published minimum and maximum ranges. Superbuy also states that second-hand products may not support return or exchange. The practical lesson is simple: a low listing price on a second-hand platform should never be compared directly with a mainstream-platform price until the service fee, return limitation, seller uncertainty, and shipping risk are included.

Before paying, label every order as standard, second-hand, unlisted, or special. That label tells you how much evidence you need.

Build a “route risk” list before the warehouse receives anything

The most expensive time to discover a mailing restriction is after several incompatible items have been consolidated. Superbuy’s current public shopping pages show that cigarettes, alcohol, and contraband are not accepted. They also show that liquids, creams, cosmetics, medicines, battery-powered products, power banks, branded goods, and other sensitive categories can have quantity or route limitations.

A restriction does not always mean every line rejects the item. It often means fewer routes, different packaging, or separation from ordinary goods. Availability changes by destination, carrier, warehouse, product attributes, and current conditions.

Create three groups while shopping:

  1. ordinary goods, such as compact clothing and non-sensitive accessories;
  2. route-sensitive goods, such as liquids, batteries, magnets, electronics, medicines, or products flagged by the system;
  3. bulky or fragile goods, such as boxed shoes, structured bags, ceramics, models, and large retail packaging.

Do not assume these groups should travel together. One small sensitive item can remove several low-cost routes from an otherwise ordinary clothing parcel. The item may be inexpensive, but its effect on the parcel can be large.

Treat QC as a decision tool, not a photo collection

Superbuy currently promotes routine warehouse inspection and three free QC photos after an item is stored. Those standard images are useful for confirming the broad identity, color, quantity, visible condition, and packaging state. They are not a full authentication service, a laboratory test, or a guarantee that hidden defects have been found.

The correct QC question is: “Do the images prove the facts that matter to my keep-or-return decision?”

For clothing, check the size tag, color, stains, missing parts, and severe construction problems. For footwear, check labeled size, pair consistency, shape, sole damage, and accessories. For electronics, standard photos may confirm model and exterior condition, but not operation.

Superbuy’s current value-added service list includes detailed photos, re-inspection, and a power-on inspection for electronics and appliances. The public listing currently shows detailed photos at CN¥2 per image, re-inspection at CN¥6 with three inspection photos, and power-on inspection at CN¥12 with evidence of the powered-on state. Live checkout should remain the final price reference because service displays and examples can change.

Buy extra evidence only when it can change the outcome. “Take more pictures” is weak. “Photograph the size label and chest measurement in one frame” is useful. One request should answer one unresolved question.

Decide return thresholds before the item arrives

A buyer who invents standards after seeing warehouse photos usually makes inconsistent decisions. Write the return threshold before ordering.

For example, clothing may be acceptable when color and size match despite a wrinkled bag. Shoes may be unacceptable when the pair has different labeled sizes but acceptable with a small box dent. An electronic accessory may require the correct connector and model number.

Superbuy’s public terms describe a limited window for no-reason return or exchange on eligible purchases and note that some orders cannot be returned. Second-hand items are specifically presented as not eligible for return and exchange. Seller rules, platform rules, customized goods, hygiene-related products, pre-sales, and removed tags or packaging can also affect eligibility.

This is why warehouse photos should be reviewed immediately. Waiting does not create better evidence. It only reduces the time available for communication with the seller and the purchasing agent.

Do not remove packaging until the product decision is finished

Packaging removal can reduce weight and volume, but timing matters. Superbuy currently lists package removal and simple packaging as free options. It also lists paid services such as vacuum packaging, folding shoe boxes, switching packaging, moisture protection, bubble protection, and other reinforcement choices.

The cheapest-looking option is not always the cheapest final decision. Removing a retail box too early can weaken return options or protection. Vacuum compression can help soft clothing but may deform structured products. Folding a shoe box saves space but creates creases. Simple packing suits some non-fragile goods, not every parcel.

Use a two-step rule:

  1. Finish QC and after-sales decisions while the product remains returnable.
  2. Choose packaging according to the shipping problem you are solving: excess volume, moisture, impact, deformation, or presentation.

A useful instruction says what may be removed, what must remain, and which item needs protection.

Understand how the freight estimate can change

Superbuy’s current fee guide explains that international freight is calculated from the destination, selected line, and final parcel data. The fee may include a first-weight charge, additional-weight charge, customs-related charges, fuel surcharges, and operational charges, depending on the line.

The submitted shipping amount is estimated. The final carrier bill can produce a refund or a shortfall, so calculator results are planning numbers, not guaranteed invoices.

The current shopping page also shows a dimensional-weight rule used for major-country routes: where a parcel reaches certain size conditions, chargeable weight may be the greater of actual weight and volume weight, with volume weight calculated as length multiplied by width multiplied by height divided by 6,000. It also displays side-length limits. These rules can vary by route, so the shipping calculator and parcel submission screen should be checked with the actual item attributes.

A light oversized box can cost more than a dense parcel. Estimate shipping consequences before buying bulky packaging.

Use consolidation selectively

Superbuy currently allows multiple stored orders to be consolidated into one international parcel without a separate consolidation fee. Consolidation can reduce duplicated outer packaging and repeated first-weight charges, but it is not automatically best to combine everything.

Combine goods when they share routes, packaging needs, declaration logic, and risk tolerance. Split fragile, valuable, sensitive, oversized, urgent, or route-blocking items.

A useful parcel plan might place ordinary clothing together, ship batteries or liquids through a compatible line separately, and keep a fragile collectible away from compressed apparel. The goal is not the fewest tracking numbers. The goal is the lowest total risk-adjusted cost.

Handle coupons and promotions conservatively

Superbuy’s public pages and logged-in account areas may display new-user benefits, rebates, freight offers, line-specific adjustments, and temporary promotions. These offers can have eligibility rules, route exclusions, minimum spending requirements, expiry dates, or account restrictions. Public announcements also show that route prices and tax conditions can change.

Do not budget around a coupon until it applies at checkout. Record the normal amount first, then treat a confirmed offer as a reduction.

Superbuy’s rebate guidance also explains that estimated rebates can change and that returns or after-sales outcomes may reduce or remove the rebate. A rebate is therefore not the same as an immediate guaranteed discount.

Run the final pre-shipment check

Before submitting the parcel, stop shopping for ten minutes and run one final review:

  1. Confirm that every item passed the keep-or-return threshold.
  2. Check whether any item is restricted, sensitive, battery-powered, liquid, magnetic, fragile, or oversized.
  3. Compare routes with and without the sensitive item.
  4. Decide which retail packaging is worth keeping.
  5. Write exact packaging instructions.
  6. Check actual and estimated dimensions, not weight alone.
  7. Confirm the destination address, phone number, postcode, and recipient name.
  8. Review the declaration guidance shown by the system.
  9. Apply only coupons that are visibly eligible.
  10. Save screenshots of the parcel selection, services, declaration, and payment breakdown.

For complex parcels, Superbuy provides a Shipping Expert workflow covering timing, packaging, split-shipment preferences, and declaration information. Its current page says the service fee excludes freight and packaging charges, is non-refundable after the request, and is generally handled within one to three working days. Accurate buyer information is still required.

The practical rule: solve risk before paying freight

The strongest Superbuy strategy is moving each decision to the earliest stage where it can still be corrected.

Classify the marketplace before purchase. Identify sensitive goods before consolidation. Review QC photos immediately. Purchase extra evidence only when it changes the decision. Finish returns before altering packaging. Compare routes using weight, dimensions, restrictions, and current line availability. Confirm promotions at checkout rather than assuming they will apply.

That process prevents the avoidable errors that turn a low-cost product into an expensive parcel.
