Superbuy QC Photos Guide 2026: Free Images, Detailed Requests & Return Decisions

Use warehouse images as evidence instead of treating them as decorative product photography.

Published July 24, 2026Updated July 30, 2026Independent guide

A Superbuy QC photo is not a beauty shot and it is not a certificate of authenticity. It is warehouse evidence captured at a specific moment before an item enters an international parcel. Buyers make better decisions when they stop asking whether a photo looks good and start asking whether it answers the question that would change their next action.

Think of QC as an evidence system

Current public guidance states that three standard QC photos are taken after warehouse inspection. Those images are designed to confirm the received item at a general level: product type, color, quantity, obvious damage or a visible mismatch. They are valuable, but they are not commissioned around your personal concerns.

The useful model is an evidence ladder. First compare the listing with the order selection. Second use the standard photos to confirm broad identity and visible condition. Third request a detailed image only when one unresolved fact could change a keep-or-return decision. Finally act before the seller after-sales window becomes difficult.

Separate objective defects from preference disagreements

A wrong color, wrong size label, missing component, broken part, visible stain or model mismatch is objective evidence. A shape that appears slightly different under warehouse lighting may be a preference question. Separating the categories improves communication with the purchasing agent and seller.

Evidence typeExampleResponse
Order mismatchSelected size 42; label shows size 40Save the order variation and request correction or return.
Visible damageTorn seam, cracked housing or deep stainAsk for one confirming close-up and use after-sales promptly.

Design one question for each detailed photo

The public value-added service table lists a detailed photo at CN¥2 per image, although live checkout should remain the final reference. A small price does not make a vague request efficient. “Please take more photos” transfers the thinking problem to the photographer and often produces another angle that still does not answer the concern.

A decision-grade instruction has a subject, viewpoint and success condition. For footwear: place the removable insole beside a ruler, photograph directly from above, and keep both ends and centimeter markings visible. For clothing: photograph the left-chest embroidery straight-on so the edge stitching and letter spacing are readable.

One-photo rule: one image should answer one decisive question. Combining label, stitching, color, measurement and packaging into a single request usually creates weak evidence for all five.

Category-specific inspection protocols

Shoes: symmetry, sizing and construction

Confirm that both shoes are the same model, color and labeled size. Compare toe shape, heel height, panel alignment, sole attachment and obvious glue marks. For fit uncertainty, request the removable insole length or an interior measurement with a ruler. If box condition matters, state that separately because damaged packaging is different from damaged footwear.

Clothing: geometry before branding details

Measurements often matter more than a close-up logo. Request chest width, garment length, waist, rise or inseam according to the product. Ask that the garment be laid flat without stretching. Then inspect seams, print placement, embroidery, zipper operation, buttons, wash labels and visible stains.

Bags and leather goods: stress points

Look at handle attachment, strap hardware, zipper tracks, corner coating, edge paint, lining, closures and interior pockets. A polished front view can miss the areas that fail first. Request the base corners or strap anchor points when durability matters.

Electronics: identity and limited functional evidence

Confirm model number, plug type, voltage information, port layout and included accessories. A photograph cannot prove long-term function. Where a supported power-on service exists, use it for a narrow question such as whether the display activates, not as a complete technical test.

Collectibles and fragile goods

Inspect corners, transparent parts, printed faces, seals, paint transfer and cracks. Decide whether opening sealed packaging would reduce value before requesting an inspection that requires opening.

Control color and scale errors

Warehouse lighting, camera processing, screen settings and reflective materials can shift color. When shade is decisive, request a neutral-light image with a white or gray reference. A hand in the image is not a standardized measurement tool; use a ruler when scale matters.

Perspective also matters. A camera held close can exaggerate toe shape or make one side appear longer. Straight-on and overhead views reduce distortion. When comparing a pair, ask that both items be aligned on the same plane.

Set a return threshold before viewing

Before warehouse arrival, define what would make you return the item. Examples include a wrong labeled size, stain larger than a coin, missing hardware or a measurement outside a stated range. Also define what you will accept, such as minor packaging damage or removable thread. A threshold written in advance is more reliable than a standard invented after seeing the photos.

Classify the result as approve, investigate or return. Approve when evidence meets the threshold. Investigate when one unresolved fact matters. Return when the mismatch is clear. The investigate category should be temporary, not an invitation to collect decorative photographs.

Create a compact QC record

For a multi-item haul, record the product link, ordered variation, warehouse arrival date, photo status, unresolved concern and final decision. This becomes useful when listings disappear or sellers change product images. It also prevents parcel approval while one item still has an open after-sales request.

  1. Save the order variation before the seller edits the listing.
  2. Review all standard photos at full size.
  3. Compare objective details with the order, not with memory.
  4. Write one precise detailed-photo request if needed.
  5. Set a deadline for the decision.
  6. Document approval, return, exchange or disposal.

Frequently asked questions

Can QC photos prove authenticity?

No. Warehouse images can document visible details, but they do not create an independent authentication certificate.

What is the best extra photo for shoes?

Choose the image that resolves your main risk. For fit, request insole length beside a ruler. For construction, request the specific seam, heel, toe, label or sole area that concerns you.

Should many detailed photos be ordered at once?

Only when each image has a distinct purpose. Begin with the question most likely to change the decision.

What do GL and RL mean?

GL commonly means green light or accept. RL commonly means red light or reject. They are community shortcuts, not official warehouse grades.

Final perspective: QC is not about collecting the maximum number of images. It is about obtaining the minimum evidence required to make a confident, timely decision.
