Superbuy Customs Declaration Guide 2026: Taxes, Restricted Goods & Parcel Splitting

Plan declaration before parcel submission: verify what arrived, separate restricted goods when needed, compare routes, and budget freight and tax as different costs.

Published August 17, 202615 min read1,756 wordsFacts reviewed August 17, 2026

Most buyers do not think about customs until the parcel page asks for a declared value. That is too late. A useful Superbuy shipping plan starts while the items are still in the warehouse, because product type, route eligibility, packing choices and declaration interact. A parcel that looks efficient by weight can become expensive or difficult to clear if it mixes restricted categories, uses an unsuitable route or carries a declaration that does not match what is actually inside.

This guide treats customs declaration as a parcel-design task rather than a box to fill in at the end. The objective is not to guess a “safe” number. It is to build a parcel whose contents, value, route and paperwork make sense together.

Research note: Superbuy’s public fee page, shopping and forwarding guidance, shipping calculator, Shipping Expert form, new-user promotion page and current logistics notices were reviewed on August 17, 2026. Route names, taxes, carrier rules and promotional terms can change, so the live parcel-submission page remains the final reference for a shipment.

Start with the two-stage cost model

Superbuy currently separates the buying process into two broad stages. First, the buyer pays for the product and China domestic delivery, plus any purchasing fee or optional service that applies. Mainstream platforms such as Taobao, Tmall, JD.com and 1688 are currently listed with no basic purchasing service fee. Some second-hand and unlisted platforms use different fee rules.

Second, the buyer submits stored items as an international parcel and pays an international shipping deposit. Superbuy says the deposit is estimated from parcel weight, route and destination, then reconciled after the logistics provider confirms final parcel size and weight. Customs duties or VAT imposed by the destination are borne by the recipient. International freight, local tax and optional warehouse services are therefore separate cost lines.

Do not use a coupon to justify a lower declaration

Superbuy’s public new-user material currently advertises a CN¥560 new-user gift in the form of coupons under campaign rules. A shipping coupon changes what you pay to Superbuy for an eligible parcel. It does not change the real value of the goods inside the box.

Keep those ledgers separate. Account credit, points or a freight promotion can reduce checkout cost, but declaration still needs to describe the shipment accurately.

Use warehouse time to build a clean item record

Superbuy currently states that warehouse arrivals receive three standard QC photos and 90 days of free storage. Those services are useful for customs planning.

The QC stage lets you confirm that the product you are about to declare is actually the product that arrived. If the ordered item was a normal jacket but the seller sent a battery-powered heated jacket, that difference can affect route eligibility and description.

Keep a simple parcel worksheet with item name, quantity, purchase value, obvious material or function, battery or liquid status, and whether original packaging will remain. It is easier to build this while the order and QC evidence are fresh.

Use paid inspection only when classification is uncertain

The current Superbuy fee page lists Detailed Photo at CN¥2 per photo, Re-inspection at CN¥6 per order item, Detailed Inspection at CN¥5.50 per item and Power-on Inspection for supported electronics at CN¥12 per item. These are not customs services, but they can resolve information gaps before shipping.

A detailed photo can confirm whether an accessory is included. Re-inspection can verify model, size, color and quantity. A power-on test can confirm what an electronic item does. Pay only when the answer can change the parcel plan, return decision or product description.

Separate “restricted” from “prohibited”

Superbuy’s shipping calculator distinguishes normal goods from categories such as liquids, powders, electronics, built-in batteries, power banks, food and medicine. It also states that prohibited goods are not supported and should be returned to the seller if they have already reached the warehouse.

A restricted item is different from a prohibited item. Restricted goods may still have eligible routes, quantity limits or special handling rules. Prohibited goods should not be solved by changing the wording of the declaration.

This matters when consolidating. Adding one power bank to an otherwise ordinary clothing parcel can remove general-goods routes from the available list. The cheapest parcel is not always the parcel with the most items inside it.

Build parcels around route compatibility first

Before comparing prices, group the warehouse by transport profile: ordinary textiles and shoes, battery products, liquids and powders, and unusually large or heavy items.

Then test those groups in the current shipping calculator. Superbuy asks for destination, weight, dimensions and goods attributes because those variables affect available lines. Its public guidance also explains that oversized parcels and volumetric weight can change chargeable weight.

Only after you know which items can travel together should you optimize consolidation. If combining two categories removes a useful route, splitting may be cheaper overall even though it creates two parcels.

Truthful declaration beats “magic number” advice

Superbuy’s forwarding guidance tells users to provide truthful product and declared-value information to reduce the risk of returns or customs inspection caused by inaccurate declarations. Its Shipping Expert form says declared value influences duties and VAT, recommends the system reference for customs clearance and notes that final taxes are decided by customs.

That is more useful than any forum rule claiming every parcel should be declared at a fixed value. Customs rules differ by destination, service type and current policy.

Describe goods in plain commercial language. “Men’s cotton T-shirts, 3 pieces” is more defensible than “gift” when the parcel is a purchase. “Wireless keyboard with built-in battery” is more useful than “accessory” when battery status affects the route.

Recent EU changes show why old habits fail

Superbuy’s current public notices are a clear example of rule volatility. A June 2026 notice described new EU low-value tariff handling from July 1, tied to product categories and HS codes for affected services. A later June 30 notice covering resumed EMS and EUB services to EU countries introduced service-specific declaration and value requirements, including stricter treatment for certain destinations.

France also has a Superbuy notice effective from March 1, 2026 describing a provisional low-value import tax for specified UPS-related routes, with the recipient responsible for payment.

The useful lesson is not to memorize one Europe-wide number. Check the notice attached to the route you are actually submitting. Tax rules can be destination-specific and route-specific at the same time.

Know what parcel splitting can and cannot fix

Splitting is useful when it solves a real constraint: route incompatibility, maximum weight, oversize limits, fragile-item protection or clearly different customs treatment. It is not automatically a tax-avoidance strategy, and it can increase first-weight charges and handling complexity.

Superbuy’s Shipping Expert form lets users say whether separate parcels are acceptable and specify declaration preferences. The page says Shipping Expert fees exclude international shipping and packaging or handling charges, the service fee is non-refundable once requested, and warehouse specialists generally process requests within one to three working days.

Use that service when the parcel is genuinely complicated, not simply because you want someone else to choose the cheapest line.

Calculate landed cost before release

For each candidate parcel, calculate four numbers: merchandise value, warehouse or packaging add-ons, international shipping estimate and a tax reserve. The tax reserve is not a guaranteed customs bill; it is a planning buffer based on the destination and route information available when you submit.

Compare alternative parcel structures. One mixed parcel may have a lower first-weight burden but fewer route choices. Two smaller parcels may cost more in base freight but keep battery goods separate from apparel. A bulky shoe-box parcel may become expensive through volumetric weight even when actual weight looks reasonable.

The right answer is the lowest expected landed cost that still respects route and declaration rules.

A seven-step pre-submit customs check

Confirm every warehouse item using the order record and QC images.

Mark batteries, liquids, powders, food, medicine and unusual materials.

Decide which original packaging is necessary.

Group items by route compatibility.

Run each group through the live shipping calculator using realistic dimensions and weight.

Prepare truthful item descriptions, quantities and values.

Read any current route notice before paying the shipping deposit.

If the parcel is difficult to classify, resolve the uncertainty before submission. Returning or splitting an item while it is still in the warehouse is usually simpler than solving a customs problem after international dispatch.

Frequently asked buyer questions

Should I always declare below a certain value?

No. There is no universal Superbuy declaration number that is correct for every country and route. Use truthful item and value information, then follow the live route and destination guidance.

Does Superbuy pay customs tax for me?

Superbuy’s public fee information says import duties and VAT imposed by destination customs are borne by the recipient. Some shipping products may pre-collect specific tax or clearance charges, but that depends on the route and current rules.

Do three free QC photos prove customs value?

No. QC photos help confirm what arrived. Customs value is a separate issue tied to the goods and transaction. Keep order records and purchase information as well as images.

Can I mix batteries with clothes?

Sometimes, but route choice can change because Superbuy treats battery products as a separate goods attribute. Check the calculator before consolidating.

Will a CN¥560 new-user coupon reduce declared value?

No. The current promotion is a coupon benefit for eligible Superbuy charges. It does not reduce the underlying value of purchased goods for customs declaration.

Is Shipping Expert the same as customs brokerage?

No. Shipping Expert helps plan and submit parcels according to the service form. Final duties and VAT are still determined by customs, and its service fee does not include international freight or packaging and handling charges.

Why did a route that worked last month disappear or change price?

Superbuy publishes route closures, resumptions, price adjustments and tax-related notices as logistics conditions change. Recheck the live calculator and current notices every time you submit a new parcel.

The final rule

Good customs planning is boring on purpose. The item description matches the item, the declared value is supportable, restricted goods are separated when necessary, the route is current, and the buyer has budgeted for freight and possible tax. That discipline is more reliable than chasing a secret declaration formula or assuming last month’s route rules are permanent.

Use the warehouse as the decision point. Once the parcel is released internationally, your ability to fix classification, packing and route mistakes becomes much smaller.
