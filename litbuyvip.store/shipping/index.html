PARCEL DESIGN / AFTER QC

Build the parcel after you know what you are keeping.

Shipping decisions become clearer when unresolved items are removed from the equation. Review QC first, identify the items you actually want to ship, then evaluate consolidation, chargeable weight, route compatibility and timing as one parcel-level decision.

Shipping cost is a parcel problem, not just an item problem

Two items with similar scale weight can behave differently once packaging and dimensions are considered. Routes can also apply different restrictions and billing methods. That is why a product-by-product shipping guess is less reliable than planning around the final group of approved warehouse items.

Use estimates as planning inputs, not guaranteed final charges. The final choice should be based on the current parcel data and routes available when you submit.

01 / CONTENT

Finalize approved items

Resolve returns and exchanges before designing the parcel. An unresolved product can change both total weight and timing.

02 / PACKAGE

Think about chargeable weight

Consider actual weight, dimensions and packaging together. Bulky but light products may behave differently from compact dense items.

03 / ROUTE

Check current eligibility

Choose among routes that are actually available for the destination and parcel contents at submission time.

PARCEL CONTROL

Four checks before submission

WEIGHT

Review the current warehouse weights and understand whether packaging changes could materially affect the shipment.

DIMENSIONS

Large outer dimensions can matter when a route uses volumetric or dimensional calculations. Do not evaluate parcel efficiency from scale weight alone.

ELIGIBILITY

Confirm that the route accepts the destination and the types of goods in the parcel. Route availability and restrictions can change.

TIMING

Balance the benefit of waiting for more items against storage age, unresolved orders and the value of shipping the approved group now.

FAQ

LitBuy shipping questions

Why wait until QC is resolved before parcel submission?

Because exchanges and returns change the set of items you are actually shipping. Planning too early can create unnecessary recalculation or force you to ship around unresolved orders.

Is actual weight always the billed weight?

Not necessarily. Shipping methods may consider dimensions or other route-specific rules. Review the current calculation shown for the route and parcel rather than assuming scale weight is the only factor.

Should every warehouse item go into one parcel?

Not automatically. The right consolidation decision depends on route eligibility, parcel characteristics, timing and the items you actually want to ship.

Can a shipping estimate change?

Yes. Estimates depend on the parcel information and current route conditions. Treat them as planning guidance and review the final parcel information before submission.
