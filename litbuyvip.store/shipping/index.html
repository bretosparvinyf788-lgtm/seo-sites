Parcel planning

LitBuy shipping: design the parcel before choosing the route

The cheapest-looking line is not automatically the best parcel decision. Start with what is actually in the warehouse, understand which items add physical or volumetric bulk, then compare eligible routes using the same parcel assumptions.

Know the parcel

Count items, note fragile or structured pieces, and identify packaging that may add volume. A route comparison is only meaningful when the parcel assumptions are consistent.

Compare like with like

Do not compare one estimate based on physical weight with another based on a different packed size. Keep weight, dimensions, destination and service level aligned.

Optimize deliberately

Packaging changes should solve a specific problem: unnecessary volume, protection, organization or route eligibility. Do not remove protection blindly just to chase a smaller number.

A four-part shipping check

First, finish QC so you are not paying to ship an item that still has an unresolved problem. Second, group items by handling needs: soft apparel behaves differently from footwear, boxes and fragile pieces. Third, estimate both weight and likely packed volume. Fourth, compare available routes only after the parcel definition is stable.

Why volumetric size matters

International parcel pricing can be affected by more than scale weight. Large low-density packaging may change chargeable weight on some routes. That is why consolidating orders without thinking about box shape can produce a surprise. The useful question is not simply “how many kilograms?” but “what will the finished parcel look like?”

Split or consolidate?

One larger parcel can simplify handling, while splitting can isolate bulky, fragile or differently timed items. There is no universal answer. Compare the total cost and operational tradeoffs of each structure rather than assuming that more consolidation is always cheaper.

Route availability, pricing rules, restrictions and delivery conditions can change. Check the live platform quote and destination-specific requirements before submission.
