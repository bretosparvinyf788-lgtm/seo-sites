Hipobuy Size Verification Protocol 2026: Seller Charts, QC Measurements and Exchange Gates

Turn clothing size labels into measurable evidence before international shipping. This five-gate workflow connects seller charts, purchase notes, targeted warehouse QC and return timing.

Buying clothing through a China shopping agent is not difficult because the labels are unfamiliar. It is difficult because the same label can describe very different garments. One seller’s XL hoodie may be narrower than another seller’s M, while an 1688 chart may show flat garment measurements rather than body measurements. The mistake is treating S, M, L, and XL as dimensions. They are only names.

Hipobuy’s current official app descriptions say the service can help users purchase from platforms such as Taobao and 1688, offers professional buyers around the clock, provides 90 days of free storage, and ships to more than 200 countries. Its official app landing page also emphasizes QC-photo search and promotes a sign-up coupon bundle. Those features create a useful buying workflow, but they do not make sizing automatic. A buyer still needs a repeatable method for translating a listing into a warehouse decision.

This guide uses five gates: Reference, Seller, Order, Warehouse, and Release. A garment passes all five before it enters an international parcel.

Build a reference sheet before opening the listing

Start with a garment you already own that fits the way you want. Lay it flat without stretching the fabric. For a top, record chest width, shoulder width, body length, and sleeve length. For trousers, record waist width, rise, inseam, outseam, thigh width, and leg opening. Use centimeters because Chinese listings commonly use metric measurements.

Write down the fit intention beside the numbers: fitted, regular, relaxed, or oversized. The same 60-centimeter chest width can work for a loose hoodie and fail for a structured jacket.

Use acceptable ranges rather than one perfect number. A buyer might accept chest width from 58 to 61 cm, length from 69 to 72 cm, and sleeve length of at least 62 cm. Ranges prevent needless returns over tiny differences while still catching a clearly wrong size.

Add one “non-negotiable measurement.” Long-armed buyers may prioritize sleeve length; trouser buyers may prioritize thigh width or rise. That field tells you where seller clarification and warehouse evidence matter most.

Turn the W2C listing into an evidence record

Save the product link, seller, listing title, chosen color, chosen size, quantity, and the date you checked the page. Product pages can change, so save screenshots of the selected variant and size chart.

Identify what the chart measures. Some charts show the body; others show the finished garment. Some list full chest circumference, while others list the width of one side. A 120 cm chest circumference can correspond to a 60 cm flat chest width. Never compare those values without converting them.

Check the unit and measurement method. Do not assume every chart uses centimeters or the same sleeve starting point. Height-and-weight tables are only rough suggestions; they do not replace garment dimensions.

Create a short purchase note with four elements:

  1. Exact color, size, and quantity.
  2. Target or minimum measurements.
  3. No-substitution instruction.
  4. Stop instruction if current seller information differs materially.

Example: “Black, size XL, quantity one. Target flat chest about 60 cm and length at least 71 cm. Do not substitute another color or size. Pause before purchase if the current batch differs from the displayed chart.”

Ask seller questions that produce numbers

Hipobuy’s official store descriptions advertise professional buyers available 24/7. This gives buyers a route for purchase clarification, although it does not guarantee that every marketplace seller will answer quickly or provide custom measurements.

Avoid asking, “Will this fit me?” A seller cannot reliably answer that from height and weight alone. Ask: “For black XL, what are the finished garment measurements for flat chest width, body length, shoulder width, and sleeve length?”

Useful questions include:

  • Is the chart for the body or the garment?
  • Is chest shown as circumference or flat width?
  • Is this chart current for the available batch?
  • Is the garment intentionally oversized?
  • Are different colors cut from the same pattern?
  • Will the seller accept a return if the item is materially outside the published chart?

Ask only questions that could change the purchase decision. Save the answer. If the seller does not reply, decide whether the listing evidence, price, and return risk justify proceeding. Silence is not confirmation.

Set an order-stage stop rule

Define the conditions that stop the order before payment: selected variant unavailable, proposed substitution, price above your cap, changed chart, or inability to confirm the critical measurement.

This is especially useful for 1688 listings, which may include wholesale quantities and several specifications under one page. Confirm quantity and variant for every line instead of relying on the main image.

A cheap item is not automatically a cheap mistake. A wrong jacket may create domestic return costs, delays, payment deductions, and possibly a second international parcel if the replacement arrives after the rest of the haul is ready.

Hipobuy’s official app landing page currently promotes more than $300 in sign-up coupons. Treat this as a bundle headline, not as cash assigned to one order. The usable reduction can depend on the coupon, threshold, account, route, and expiry. Record only the discount actually accepted at checkout.

Use warehouse QC as a measurement gate

A tag photo proves the label attached to the garment. It does not prove that the garment matches the chart. A normal front photo can confirm color and obvious condition, but it rarely proves fit.

For a top, useful measurement photos show the garment laid flat, the tape across the chest at the underarm points, body length from a stated starting point, shoulder width, and sleeve length. For trousers, prioritize waistband width without stretching, rise, thigh, inseam, outseam, and leg opening.

The tape endpoints must be visible and readable. If the tape position or image angle is unclear, ask for a retake before deciding.

Use tiers so you do not over-inspect every item:

  • Tier A: expensive or fit-critical garments receive full measurement evidence.
  • Tier B: repeat sellers or familiar products receive the non-negotiable measurement plus identity checks.
  • Tier C: flexible, low-risk items receive normal identity and condition review.

Hipobuy’s public landing page emphasizes QC photos, but the public pages do not establish one universal number of included photos or one fixed price for every custom image. Check the current order interface before relying on a photo allowance.

Compare with tolerance, not emotion

Compare the warehouse result with the seller chart, your acceptable range, and the intended fit.

If a hoodie was advertised at 60 cm flat chest and the warehouse photo shows 59 cm, it may pass a 58–61 cm range. If it shows 55 cm, the difference is likely large enough to change the fit.

Use one of five decisions:

  • Pass: correct identity and within range.
  • Clarify: measurement method or photo is uncertain.
  • Exchange: wrong variant or a verified replacement is practical.
  • Return: materially outside range and exchange is not worthwhile.
  • Accept with note: small variance that does not change expected fit.

Do not approve an item only because returning feels inconvenient. International shipping makes correction harder. At the same time, do not dispute a minor difference that still meets the range you set before buying.

Separate warehouse storage from seller return timing

Hipobuy advertises 90 days of free storage. That is consolidation capacity, not a 90-day seller-return promise. Marketplace return eligibility can be shorter and may depend on seller acceptance, product condition, domestic transport, and order status.

Set an internal QC deadline as soon as each garment reaches the warehouse. Track warehouse arrival, QC review, clarification request, return or exchange request, replacement arrival, and refund status. This prevents an unresolved item from being forgotten while other purchases continue arriving.

Re-check replacements from zero. Confirm color, tagged size, critical measurements, condition, and quantity. An exchange is not finished merely because a replacement tracking number exists.

Release only approved garments into the parcel

Before parcel submission, freeze an approved inventory list. Exclude anything marked hold, clarify, exchange, return, or refund pending.

Sizing decisions also affect packaging. Removing retail packaging may reduce volume, but do not remove support from structured garments or shoes without considering deformation. Compression may suit T-shirts and basic knitwear, but not every padded jacket, pleated item, cap, or structured piece.

Use the live route options for the real destination and parcel. Hipobuy’s official app descriptions advertise thousands of shipping options and delivery to more than 200 countries, with some services promoted as fast as five days. Treat “as fast as” as a best-case statement, not a universal promise. Save the displayed estimate, route name, parcel weight and dimensions, coupon reduction, and paid amount.

Keep a personal fit library

After delivery, save the seller, product link, color, tagged size, warehouse measurements, fabric notes, and actual fit. Over time, this library becomes more reliable than generic size labels. You may learn that one seller’s XL fits like another seller’s M, or that a particular trouser cut needs more thigh width.

The goal is not to eliminate every sizing risk. It is to move the decision from labels and hope to measurements and evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Does Hipobuy guarantee seller size charts?

No public platform description guarantees that every marketplace chart is exact. Treat it as seller-provided evidence and verify important dimensions at the warehouse.

Should I choose from height and weight alone?

No. Use those figures as secondary guidance. Compare flat garment measurements with a piece you already own.

Are tag photos enough?

No. A tag confirms the printed size, not the actual chest, length, waist, or inseam.

How much difference is acceptable?

Set the range before ordering. The right tolerance depends on garment type, fabric, fit intention, and your non-negotiable measurement.

Does 90-day storage mean 90 days to return?

No. Storage capacity and seller return eligibility are separate. Review warehouse evidence quickly.

What if the seller does not answer?

Proceed only if the listing evidence, price, and return risk are acceptable. Do not treat silence as confirmation.

Should I exchange or return?

Compare replacement certainty, domestic costs, seller cooperation, timing, and your parcel deadline.

What records should I keep?

Keep the W2C link, variant screenshot, chart, seller response, order note, QC images, measurements, return timeline, refund evidence, parcel details, and final fit notes.
