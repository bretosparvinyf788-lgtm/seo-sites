Sugargoo DIY Order Guide 2026: Build a Clear Manual Purchase Brief

A field-by-field system for turning a broken, short or unsupported product link into a purchase instruction another person can execute accurately.

August 26, 2026•12 min read•1,618 words
← All Buyer Guides

Automatic product import is convenient because the marketplace page carries the title, variants, images and price into one screen. When that import fails, many buyers treat a DIY order as a blank checkout form. It is better understood as a procurement brief: a compact record that must tell another person exactly which product to buy, from which seller, at what price and under which conditions.

Sugargoo’s current official DIY Order guidance describes the feature as a manual fallback when a link is not recognized or comes from a less common platform such as Xiaohongshu or Xianyu. The form accepts a goods link when available, product and store names, remarks, product images, unit price, quantity and local shipping cost. Those fields are simple. The difficult part is making them agree with one another at the moment the purchasing agent reviews the request.

Try to repair the source link first

Before creating a manual order, confirm that the problem is really the product and not the URL. Sugargoo’s official link guidance says a strong Taobao link points to one product detail page, contains the item ID and can open independently. Search-results pages, store homepages, campaign pages and live-stream landing pages often fail because they do not identify one stable SKU.

Open the listing in a normal browser, remove unnecessary tracking text if the core product address remains intact, and check that the page still shows the intended item. If the mobile share link redirects badly, use the full product page. A working direct link is preferable because it lets the system display seller information, price, variants and details together. Use DIY Order when that cleaner route still cannot represent the purchase.

Create a source snapshot before the page changes

A manual request should survive a listing edit. Save the seller name, store name, product title, visible price, selected variant, included pieces and timestamp. Capture one image showing the entire product and separate images showing the exact option and specification. If the seller provided a negotiated price or stock confirmation in chat, preserve that evidence beside the request.

Do not upload a collage that hides small text. Give each image one job: identify the product, identify the chosen variant, prove the price or show a decision-critical detail. The purchasing agent should be able to compare the form with the source without guessing which screenshot contains the real instruction.

Write a product name that identifies, not advertises

The product-name field should distinguish the item in an order list. Use category, model and one defining attribute. “Women’s jacket” is weak; “cropped nylon jacket, model A17, black” is useful. Avoid copying a long promotional title filled with claims and search keywords. It can obscure the exact object and create a poor warehouse label later.

The store-name field is part of seller identity, not decoration. Record it exactly as shown and keep a screenshot of the shop header or seller profile. Similar photos can appear across multiple stores with different prices, dispatch terms and return policies. A product image alone does not prove which seller should receive the order.

Turn remarks into acceptance criteria

Sugargoo’s official form invites remarks for style, size, color and other details. Write them as a short specification rather than a conversation. Start with the exact choice: “Color: dark green. Size: XL. Version: lined. Quantity: one.” Then state included pieces, acceptable substitution and any seller question that must be resolved before purchase.

Use explicit negative instructions only when they prevent a likely mistake: “Do not replace dark green with army green” or “Do not purchase without the detachable strap.” Avoid vague notes such as “best quality,” “same as photo” or “please check carefully.” They do not define a measurable buying decision. If no substitution is acceptable, say that the order should pause for confirmation instead.

Separate variant, bundle and quantity

Chinese listings often combine many products under one page. A thumbnail may represent a color, a bundle, a model generation or only an optional accessory. Write each layer separately. Variant describes the chosen SKU. Bundle states what the SKU includes. Quantity tells the agent how many units of that complete bundle to buy.

For example, “blue” cannot explain whether the order is one blue case, one blue device or a device-and-case set. Record model compatibility, capacity, measurements or pack count when relevant. If the seller uses a numbered option, preserve both the option number and visible description because either one can change.

Reconcile unit price and domestic shipping

The DIY form asks for unit price, purchase quantity and Chinese domestic shipping. Copy the price that applies to the selected variant, not the cheapest headline price on the listing. Check whether the displayed figure depends on a minimum quantity, coupon, membership level, deposit or time-limited promotion. If the seller quoted a negotiated amount, identify it in the remarks and attach the evidence.

Sugargoo’s official guidance says buyers who do not know the local shipping fee can mention that uncertainty in the remarks and let the purchasing team confirm it. Do not invent zero. A missing domestic charge can change the payable amount after review, while a falsely precise value makes it harder to see what changed.

Add a decision hierarchy for uncertain orders

A useful brief tells the agent what to do when the listing and form disagree. Set three priorities: first, the exact product and variant; second, the maximum acceptable total; third, the response if either condition fails. A sample instruction is: “Purchase only model V2 in white at no more than CNY 188 before domestic shipping; otherwise pause and ask.”

This hierarchy prevents an agent from solving a price difference by buying the wrong version or solving a stock problem with an unwanted substitute. It also shortens follow-up messages because the boundary was defined before the agent reached the seller.

Screen restrictions before pressing Agent Buy

Sugargoo’s DIY Order guide tells buyers to review shipping restrictions before submission. Ordinary goods are generally straightforward, sensitive items such as batteries or liquids carry route risk, oversized cargo may need special lines, and prohibited products such as weapons or chemicals cannot be shipped. Manual entry does not bypass those rules.

Describe sensitive characteristics openly. Include a battery configuration, liquid volume, material or packed dimensions when known. Confirm that the item has at least one viable route to the destination before committing meaningful money. A successful domestic purchase is not a successful reverse-purchase plan if the product cannot travel internationally.

Design the brief for warehouse verification

Sugargoo explains that a DIY order follows the normal sequence after submission: the seller sends the item to the warehouse, the product is inspected, the buyer chooses consolidation and an international route, and then pays delivery. Your manual brief should therefore predict what the warehouse can verify visibly.

Ask for visible attributes such as label, model number, color, measurement or included accessory. Do not expect a standard photograph to prove authenticity, hidden functionality or material composition. When a decision requires a special angle or measurement, plan the appropriate photography request while the domestic remedy remains practical.

Audit the order before payment

Read the completed form from the agent’s perspective. Can the link and store identify one seller? Do product name, images and remarks point to the same item? Does the unit price match that variant? Is quantity applied to a unit or a bundle? Is domestic freight known or clearly marked for confirmation? Is the fallback decision explicit?

Save the submitted version after payment. If the listing changes or the warehouse receives something unexpected, the source snapshot and purchase brief show what was authorized. That record is far more useful than trying to reconstruct a disappearing page from memory.

Ten practical buyer questions

When should I use Sugargoo DIY Order?

Use it when a product link cannot be recognized, when a short or removed page fails, or when a less common platform cannot be imported automatically.

Which product details does the form require?

Current official guidance lists the goods link when available, product name, store, remarks, images, unit price, quantity and domestic shipping fee.

Can I submit a DIY order without a working link?

Yes, but provide strong seller, product, image, price and variant evidence so the purchasing team can identify the intended offer.

What should I write in remarks?

State exact style, color, size, version, included pieces, substitution rule, price boundary and any question that must be answered before purchase.

What if I do not know Chinese domestic shipping?

Sugargoo’s official guide says to mention the uncertainty in remarks so the purchasing team can confirm the amount.

Should I use the lowest price shown on the listing?

No. Enter the price for the selected SKU and verify whether coupons, deposits, minimum quantities or promotions affect it.

Can DIY Order bypass shipping restrictions?

No. Sensitive, oversized and prohibited-item rules still apply. Check route eligibility before the seller is paid.

Can the agent choose a similar substitute automatically?

Only authorize that if you genuinely accept it. Otherwise instruct the order to pause when the exact variant is unavailable.

What evidence should I keep?

Save the listing, store identity, selected option, price, seller messages, uploaded images and final submitted order.

What happens after a DIY order is submitted?

It follows the normal purchasing flow: seller dispatch, warehouse intake and inspection, consolidation, route selection and international shipping payment.

Bottom line: a DIY order should remove ambiguity, not merely replace automation. Preserve the source, identify one seller and one exact SKU, reconcile price and domestic freight, define what happens when conditions change, and write details that can be verified at the warehouse. The form is short, but the quality of its purchase brief determines how safely the decision survives every later handoff.
