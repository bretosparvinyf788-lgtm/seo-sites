Superbuy Order Exceptions: Build a Spreadsheet for Price Changes, Returns and After-Sales Evidence

A useful Superbuy spreadsheet should do more than collect product links. This field guide shows how to record requirements, control seller changes, review warehouse evidence and prepare a clean return or after-sales file when an order stops following the original plan.

Why exception handling deserves its own sheet

A normal order is easy to describe: choose a listing, pay, wait for the seller, review the warehouse photos and submit the item for international delivery. Real orders are often less tidy. A color disappears, a seller asks for extra money, a size is substituted, domestic delivery stalls, or the warehouse photos reveal something that was never visible on the marketplace page. The expensive mistake is not the exception itself. It is making the next decision without a reliable record of what you originally approved.

Superbuy's current user guidance says buyers can enter destination and requirement remarks while submitting an order. During purchasing, the order enquiry function is the channel for contacting the shopping agent, and the agent may also request missing information by message or email. Those details suggest a practical rule: important requirements should be written before the agent buys, then copied into your own sheet. A memory, a translated chat fragment or an old thumbnail is not a strong specification.

Create one exception log beside the product table

Keep the familiar product row, but add a separate log in which every event receives its own line. Use these fields: order number, item reference, event time, current status, original requirement, new seller information, evidence received, money affected, deadline, decision owner, requested action and final resolution. The product table tells you what you wanted. The exception log tells you why the outcome changed.

Translate preferences into acceptance rules

“Good quality,” “same as the photo” and “please check carefully” sound helpful but are not measurable. Replace them with facts an agent or warehouse worker can observe: black color, size 42, two units, silver buckle, no cracked edge, printed logo centered, power plug type specified, or chest width within an acceptable range. Divide requirements into three levels. A must-have causes cancellation or return if it fails. A preference is desirable but negotiable. A cosmetic tolerance can be accepted without delay.

Handle out-of-stock and price-change messages

Superbuy's published terms recognize that a seller may report an item out of stock, change the price or fail to deliver on time, and that Superbuy will communicate with the buyer when this affects the service. Treat that message as a decision request, not an instruction to approve automatically. Record the old and new price, exact replacement variation, domestic freight difference, expected delay and whether the seller is changing the product itself.

Use a three-way decision: accept, request clarification or cancel. Accept only when the new facts stay inside your rules. Request clarification when the seller's proposal lacks a precise variation, measurement or total charge. Cancel when the substitute no longer solves the original need. A small price increase can become significant after currency conversion and international shipping, while a cheap substitute can be a worse loss if it cannot be returned later.

Use order enquiry as a structured ticket

Write one question per message and put the requested outcome first. For example: “Please confirm whether the seller can provide the black, size 42 version at the original item price. If not, do not purchase and ask me before changing the order.” This is easier to execute than a paragraph containing several alternatives. Add a response-by date to your sheet, but do not invent a guaranteed answer time. Seller speed, working hours and the type of request can all vary.

Review warehouse evidence against the approved specification

Superbuy says items are inspected and photographed after arriving at the warehouse, and problem cases may trigger contact. Use those photos as a comparison stage, not as a general quality guarantee. Start with identity: product, variation, color and quantity. Then test the must-have fields. Finally check visible condition, accessories and seller packaging. Photographs cannot reliably prove authenticity, internal electronics, long-term durability, material composition or a hidden component.

Separate warehouse storage from seller return time

Superbuy currently advertises 90 days of free warehouse storage, but that storage period is not the same thing as a seller's return window or a platform after-sales deadline. The service agreement describes a five-day no-reason return or exchange rule for qualifying purchases, with conditions and limitations, while special categories can follow different rules. Therefore, do not use the storage countdown as your return calendar.

Add three dates: warehouse entry, last verified return-request date and internal review deadline. The internal deadline should be earlier so there is time to ask a question and receive an answer. If eligibility is unclear, ask through the order channel before assuming a return is available. Seller policy, product category, condition, prior service use and domestic return freight may affect the result.

Build a return decision packet

A return request becomes easier to understand when it is a compact evidence packet. Include the order number, original specification, warehouse arrival date, relevant QC image, concise defect description, requested remedy and the deadline shown in your records. State only what the evidence proves. “Right sleeve seam is open for approximately three centimeters” is stronger than “terrible quality.” If the problem is the wrong variation, place the approved option and received option side by side.

Flag special products before payment

Not every purchase receives the same inspection or return protection. Superbuy's current second-hand marketplace information says used items are not eligible for return or exchange, warns that sellers may delay shipment, not ship or change the price, and describes a separate service fee. Its general terms also identify categories where professional inspection may be unavailable or limited, including sealed collectibles, some electronics and custom-made goods. These are pre-purchase risks, not surprises to discover after arrival.

Create a “special rule” column with values such as used, customized, sealed, fragile, deposit order, sensitive or professional inspection unavailable. For any flagged row, require a manual review before payment. Record what can be checked, what cannot be checked, whether returns are possible and what shipping restrictions might follow. Never copy the assumptions from an ordinary clothing order into a second-hand collectible or battery-powered product.

Prepare evidence for parcel after-sales

The evidence trail continues after international delivery. Superbuy's user guidance directs buyers with parcel problems to select the relevant parcel and apply for after-sales service. The official terms emphasize checking the outer package at receipt and applying within the valid after-sales period. Another current terms page states a short 72-hour period after delivery for certain missed, damaged or flawed item claims, although the wording on the page is imperfect. Treat this as a reason to act promptly and verify the applicable rule for your case.

Before opening, photograph all sides of the parcel, the shipping label, seals and visible damage. If possible, record a continuous opening video. Compare the contents with the warehouse photos and packing list. Preserve tracking events, parcel number, delivery time, item order numbers and close views of damage. Do not discard the carton, internal protection or labels until the case is resolved.

Frequently asked questions

1. What should I write in the Superbuy order remark?

Write only requirements that can be acted on: exact variation, quantity, observable must-haves and what the agent should do if the seller cannot comply. Separate mandatory rules from preferences. Avoid long background stories and vague requests such as “best quality.”

2. Should I approve a seller's replacement immediately?

No. Compare the replacement's exact listing, variation, measurements, price, domestic freight and return conditions with your original specification. Ask for missing facts. Approve only after the substitute remains inside the limits you recorded before purchase.

3. How do I track a price increase?

Keep the original price unchanged and create an exception entry containing the new amount, reason, date and total difference. Include currency conversion and any effect on service or shipping budgets before accepting. Mark who approved the change.

4. When should I use order enquiry?

Use it when the agent needs clarification, a seller changes terms, dispatch appears delayed, or you need a specific action. Put one request and one fallback in each message, then retain the factual reply and status change in your log.

5. Are warehouse photos proof of full product quality?

No. They are evidence of visible identity and condition from the angles provided. They generally cannot prove authenticity, internal function, durability or hidden materials. Ask for a targeted view or measurement only when it can change your decision.

6. Does 90-day free storage mean I have 90 days to return an item?

No. Warehouse storage and return eligibility are separate clocks. Record the warehouse date, the verified seller or platform deadline and an earlier internal review date. Start a question or return request promptly instead of waiting to consolidate.

7. Is every item eligible for a no-reason return?

No. Eligibility can depend on seller policy, category, condition and service rules. Superbuy publishes special limitations, and used products are presented as not returnable or exchangeable. Verify the rule applying to the exact order before payment.

8. What evidence makes a warehouse return request clearer?

Provide the order number, approved specification, arrival date, relevant QC image, objective description, desired remedy and deadline. Show expected and received details side by side. Avoid claims that the available photographs cannot demonstrate.

9. Who normally pays domestic return shipping?

It depends on the reason, seller and applicable rules. Do not assume it is free. Ask the agent for the expected domestic freight and any service charge, then compare that amount with the item's usable value and replacement delay.

10. What should I do with a second-hand or custom item?

Flag it for manual review before paying. Confirm return eligibility, inspection limits, seller evidence, measurements and any special fee. For a custom item, preserve the exact approved design and seller conversation because ordinary listing photos may not define the finished product.

11. What should I record when the international parcel arrives damaged?

Before opening, photograph the carton, label, seals and every damaged area. Preserve packaging, record the opening when possible, compare contents with warehouse evidence, and save parcel number, delivery time, tracking events and close views of each affected item.

12. When is an exception truly resolved?

Only when the promised operational result is visible: the corrected item is in storage, the refund appears in the account record, the return status is complete, or the after-sales decision and amount are documented. A message promising action is progress, not closure.
