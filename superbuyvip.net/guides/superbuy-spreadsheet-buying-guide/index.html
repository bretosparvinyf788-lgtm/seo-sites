Spreadsheet Field Guide · August 2026

Build a Superbuy spreadsheet that prevents expensive mistakes

A useful buying sheet is not a wall of links. It is a compact decision system that keeps product evidence, warehouse status and shipping assumptions connected.

Start with the decision, not the product count

Most public spreadsheets compete on volume. Scale can make discovery faster, but it does not necessarily make a purchase safer. A buyer needs to answer a smaller set of questions: is the listing active, which variation produced the displayed price, what evidence supports the quality claim, how much weight will the item add, and when does the after-sales window close? A sheet that cannot answer those questions is an index, not a workflow.

Build columns around decisions: product name, original listing, seller, chosen variation, item price, domestic freight, estimated weight, warehouse weight, QC status, return deadline and parcel assignment. Keep the original marketplace URL even when you also store an agent link.

Separate catalog facts from judgments

A product title, price and seller are observable facts at a particular time. “Best quality” and “safe seller” are judgments. Keep them in different fields and date volatile information. A price checked three months ago should not silently look current.

FieldWhat belongs thereWhy it matters
Listing snapshotPrice, seller, option, datePrevents stale-price confusion
EvidenceQC link, measurements, defect notesShows why a decision was made
DeadlineReturn or exchange cutoffKeeps action time visible
Parcel planWeight, box choice, route classConnects item to landed cost

Use status labels that demand action

Statuses such as “ordered” or “warehouse” are too broad. Use labels that tell you what must happen next: seller dispatch pending, QC review required, measurement requested, return decision due, approved for consolidation, or held for a second parcel. For QC, record both the outcome and its basis.

Calculate landed cost in layers

The item price is only the first layer. Add Chinese domestic delivery, optional services, estimated international allocation and payment conversion cost. International shipping should initially be a range because the final charge depends on measured weight, dimensions, packing and available routes. When warehouse weight arrives, replace the estimate but keep the forecast in a separate column.

Audit before parcel submission

Filter for approved items. Check that each has a recorded variation, warehouse weight and QC decision. Then review the parcel as a whole: value, chargeable weight, restricted-item mix, packing requests and route availability. If one item removes most shipping choices, compare its separate-parcel cost.

The best spreadsheet is not the largest one. It is the one that lets you reconstruct why you bought an item, what you saw at the warehouse, and how you estimated the final cost.

Maintain it like a small system

Archive dead links instead of deleting them. Review active rows while items are in storage, and close each order with actual delivery date, final parcel cost and outcome notes. Over time, your own history becomes more valuable than a public list because it reflects your destination and risk tolerance.

Decision boundary

A working sheet should preserve the reason behind every approval, return and shipping decision.
