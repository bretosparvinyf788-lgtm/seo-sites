Landed-Cost Field Guide · August 21, 2026

Superbuy fees explained: build a real landed-cost spreadsheet before you buy

A low product price can create an expensive parcel. This guide turns Superbuy's two-stage fee structure into a practical spreadsheet that reveals purchasing charges, optional QC services, chargeable weight and the final delivered-cost range before a haul becomes difficult to change.

The product price is only the first invoice

Most buying mistakes begin with a correct number used in the wrong context. The marketplace price may be accurate, yet it is not the amount that lands at your door. A Superbuy order moves through two main cost stages. Stage one brings the item from the Chinese seller to the warehouse. Stage two sends the consolidated parcel from the warehouse to the destination country.

Superbuy's current fee page describes the total as product cost, international shipping and optional value-added service fees. That structure is more useful than a single “total” column because each layer behaves differently. Product price is tied to a seller and variation. Domestic freight may change by seller. International freight depends on route, destination and chargeable weight. Optional services should be purchased only when they answer a specific risk.

Build the sheet in five cost blocks

Create one row per product, but divide the columns into five blocks: acquisition, warehouse evidence, parcel allocation, payment record and final outcome. Acquisition includes item price, Chinese domestic freight and any purchasing service charge. Warehouse evidence includes optional photos or inspection. Parcel allocation holds actual weight, estimated volume and the share of international freight. The payment record stores deposits, adjustments and refunds. Final outcome contains the delivered cost per item.

Know when purchasing service is free

Superbuy currently states that standard purchasing from mainstream platforms such as Taobao, Tmall, JD.com and 1688 has no purchasing service fee. That does not make the complete transaction free. The item price and seller's domestic delivery still belong in stage one, and optional services remain separate.

Other sources can use different rules. The current fee information lists a flat CN¥20 charge per order for certain secondhand platforms, a percentage-based fee for some other secondhand or unlisted platforms, and a separate Shipping Expert fee for special purchases such as limited editions or presales. Record the source platform before applying any percentage. A formula that assumes every product uses the mainstream-platform rule will understate cost as soon as one exceptional order enters the haul.

Purchase QC evidence, not decoration

Standard warehouse photos are a starting point. Extra services earn their place only when they reduce a decision risk. Superbuy's current value-added list displays Detailed Photo at CN¥2 per picture and Detailed Inspection at CN¥5.50 per item. It also lists specialized options such as power-on inspection for certain electronics, product video, model try-on photographs and packaging documentation.

There is a useful caution in the same official fee material: a worked stage-one example still uses CN¥3 for detailed photography, while the current service list displays CN¥2 per photo. That inconsistency is exactly why a spreadsheet should include a “live price checked” date and copy the amount shown at checkout. Do not preserve an old guide price as though it were a permanent tariff.

Before buying extra evidence, write the question it must answer. “Measure insole length from heel to toe” has a decision value. “Take more photos” does not. Add a column named “changed decision?” after the result. If a service never changes approval, return or packing choices, it may be recurring cost without operational benefit.

Forecast international freight with chargeable weight

International shipping is not simply kilograms multiplied by one universal rate. Superbuy's fee structure describes first-weight charges, continued-weight charges and possible customs, fuel or operation components. The shipping calculator defines the first weight unit as the minimum billable unit and applies continued units above that threshold. Routes also have different limits and restricted-item rules.

For large but light goods, volume can matter more than scale weight. Superbuy's calculator currently explains a common air-cargo formula: length in centimeters multiplied by width and height, divided by 6,000. Its shopping page also notes that route conditions can change when a side reaches certain dimensions. Treat those figures as current planning inputs, not promises across every line.

Create three estimates. The compact case assumes removable packaging and actual weight. The working case uses warehouse dimensions and a realistic outer-packaging allowance. The stress case uses volumetric weight and a less favorable eligible route.

Allocate parcel cost fairly

Dividing international freight equally across ten products is simple and usually misleading. A lightweight accessory should not receive the same freight allocation as a boxed pair of shoes. Use weight-based allocation for dense goods and volume-based allocation for bulky goods. If a restricted item forces the whole parcel onto a more expensive route, assign the route premium to that item rather than hiding it across the haul.

Test packaging services as cost trades, not automatic upgrades. The official list currently shows free package removal and simple packaging, while vacuum packaging, folding shoe boxes and several reinforcement options have separate prices. Removing volume can lower freight, but insufficient protection can increase damage risk. Record both the service charge and the expected change in chargeable weight.

Reconcile the deposit with the final bill

When a parcel is submitted, Superbuy explains that the international shipping fee is collected from estimated weight, route and destination. The logistics provider later confirms actual size and weight. Overpayment is refunded to the account balance, while an underpayment requires the difference. The parcel-success guidance currently says the shipping difference is generally reconciled after dispatch.

Your spreadsheet therefore needs three separate cells: quoted deposit, final shipping charge and settlement difference. Never overwrite the estimate. The variance is valuable data. If the estimate repeatedly misses high for vacuum-packed clothing or low for boxed footwear, the next spreadsheet can use category-specific correction factors rather than a vague contingency.

A practical landed-cost formula

For each item, calculate delivered cost as item price plus domestic freight plus purchasing service fee plus optional evidence services plus packaging services plus allocated international freight plus payment or customs costs actually borne by the buyer. Keep taxes as a scenario until the destination's treatment is known.

Finally, calculate variance twice: forecast versus warehouse estimate, then warehouse estimate versus final delivered result. The first reveals poor product assumptions. The second reveals parcel and route assumptions. This turns every completed shipment into a more accurate model for the next one.

Frequently asked questions

1. Does Superbuy charge a service fee on every purchase?

No. Its current fee page says standard purchasing is free for mainstream platforms including Taobao, Tmall, JD.com and 1688. Secondhand, unlisted and special expert purchases can follow different fee rules, so record the source platform before calculating.

2. What belongs in the first payment?

Stage one normally includes the product price, Chinese domestic shipping, any applicable purchasing service fee and optional item-level services selected before international shipment. Keep each component separate so a seller charge is not confused with an agent service.

3. Are standard QC photos the same as detailed inspection?

No. Standard photos document basic visible condition. Detailed photographs target a requested angle, while detailed inspection covers more areas and defect types. Choose the service that answers the actual uncertainty rather than buying both automatically.

4. How much does an extra detailed photo cost?

The current value-added service list displays CN¥2 per detailed photo, although one worked example on the fee page still uses CN¥3. Treat the live checkout price as controlling and date the amount recorded in your spreadsheet.

5. What is chargeable weight?

Chargeable weight is the weight a route uses for billing. Depending on the line and parcel dimensions, it may be actual scale weight or calculated volumetric weight. Bulky light goods can therefore cost more than their measured kilograms suggest.

6. How is volumetric weight estimated?

Superbuy's calculator currently describes a common formula of length multiplied by width multiplied by height in centimeters, divided by 6,000. Route-specific rules can differ, so use the formula for planning and confirm the selected line's live calculation.

7. Should international freight be split equally across products?

Usually not. Allocate by weight, volume or the restriction premium created by an item. Equal division can make small products appear unprofitable and hide the true delivery burden of bulky or route-limiting goods.

8. Is package removal always the cheapest choice?

No. Removing unnecessary retail packaging may reduce chargeable volume, but protective structure can matter for fragile goods or collectible boxes. Compare potential freight savings with damage risk and record exceptions at item level.

9. Why can the final freight differ from the deposit?

The deposit uses estimated parcel data. Final billing follows the logistics provider's verified package size and weight. Superbuy then refunds an overpayment to the account balance or asks for the underpaid difference.

10. How should coupons be recorded?

Keep coupons in a separate discount column with an expiry date and eligible stage. Do not reduce the underlying product or route rate. This preserves the true cost baseline after a temporary promotion disappears.

11. What contingency should a first-time buyer use?

Use a range rather than one universal percentage. Model compact, working and stress cases based on packaging, volume and route eligibility. The distance between working and stress cases is a more defensible contingency than an arbitrary markup.

12. Which number matters most after delivery?

The variance between forecast and final delivered cost is the most useful learning number. Tag its cause as seller freight, service choice, weight, volume, route, settlement or tax so the next haul corrects the right assumption.

Working rule: every cost in the spreadsheet must have a source, a status and a date. If a number is not clearly fixed, estimated or final, it is not ready to guide a purchase.
