USFans Parcel Submission Guide 2026: 90-Day Storage, Shipping Deposit & Packing Services

Use USFans warehouse time, packing requests and shipping settlement rules to build a more predictable international parcel.

19 August 202612 min readEnglish

Most USFans guides focus on finding products or checking QC photos, but costly mistakes often happen after an item has already passed inspection. The warehouse-to-parcel stage is where storage time, consolidation, packaging requests, shipping deposits and final carrier measurements start interacting.

USFans' current beginner guidance makes this stage concrete. It states that stored items receive 90 days of free warehouse storage, that buyers can combine items for international shipping, and that parcel submission can include package removal, reinforcement and insurance. It also explains that the international shipping payment starts as a deposit based on estimated weight, shipping method and destination, while the final fee is calculated from package size and weight verified by the shipping company.

1. Use the 90-day storage window deliberately

USFans currently says it provides 90 days of free storage for warehouse items. That matters because products from Taobao, 1688 and Weidian rarely arrive at exactly the same time. You can inspect one item, leave it stored, then wait for later orders before shipping everything.

Do not treat 90 free days as permission to forget the clock. Record when the first item arrives and decide what will trigger shipment: the last planned order arriving, a target parcel weight, or a personal deadline well before the storage limit. A fixed rule prevents the haul from expanding endlessly just because the warehouse still has space.

2. Consolidate compatible items, not simply every item

USFans encourages buyers to stock up and consolidate stored items for overseas shipping. Consolidation can reduce the need to send several separate international parcels, but it should still be selective.

Group products by route compatibility. Ordinary clothing and soft accessories are easier to combine than liquids, fragile glass, large rigid boxes or battery-containing products, which may face restrictions. One sensitive product can reduce the lines available to an otherwise simple parcel. The best consolidated parcel is therefore not always the biggest one.

3. Finish QC before you start building the parcel

USFans product pages currently advertise three to seven free HD inspection photos after warehouse arrival and state that dissatisfied buyers can apply for a return within five days. That return window is much shorter than the 90-day storage period.

Review QC as soon as an item arrives. Check model, color, size label, quantity and visible defects, then look for parcel clues such as oversized retail boxes, rigid packaging or fragile parts. If something is wrong, resolve it before the item becomes part of the final shipping plan.

4. Replace seller estimates with warehouse reality

Early product pages can show estimated weight and dimensions, but parcel submission needs more realistic information. USFans' shipping estimator asks for destination, weight, item category, postal code and package dimensions, and its public guidance says final cost can change after consolidation.

A haul with 4.5 kilograms of listed product weight will not necessarily become a 4.5-kilogram outbound parcel. Cartons and protective material add mass, while the finished dimensions can create volumetric weight that exceeds actual weight. Use seller figures for early planning, then move toward warehouse and packed-parcel data as submission gets closer.

5. Understand the shipping deposit

USFans' beginner guide says the international shipping fee deposit is calculated from the product's estimated weight, selected shipping method and destination. The key word is deposit: it is not necessarily the final carrier charge.

Budget with a buffer instead of assuming the first amount is exact. If a parcel is only affordable when the estimate is perfect to the cent, the plan has no room for a heavier carton, larger final dimensions or carrier adjustment.

6. Know how the final shipping amount is settled

USFans states that the final shipping fee is calculated from the package size and weight verified by the shipping company. It also says that if the final fee differs from the deposit, the difference is returned to the user's USFans account after shipment.

The current shipping estimator adds useful context: USFans says estimates use real routes and historical data, while final cost may commonly vary within roughly five to ten percent because of actual versus volumetric weight, final package dimensions, route availability and carrier adjustments. Treat the estimate as a planning range rather than a guaranteed invoice.

7. Decide whether package removal helps

USFans lists package removal among the additional services buyers can request during parcel submission. This can matter when bulky retail packaging adds volume without adding much useful protection.

USFans currently explains volumetric weight as length × width × height in centimeters divided by 6000, with the higher of actual and volumetric weight becoming chargeable weight. Removing a large shoe box can therefore matter even if it barely changes actual kilograms.

Package removal is not always correct. Collectible boxes may have value, and rigid packaging can protect products. Compare shipping efficiency with protection and presentation before requesting removal.

8. Use reinforcement only for a specific risk

Reinforcement is another service USFans says can be requested at parcel submission. Its purpose is different from package removal: removal tries to eliminate unnecessary bulk, while reinforcement adds protection.

Structured bags, fragile accessories or crush-sensitive items may justify extra support. A soft clothing parcel may not. Additional protection can also affect weight or dimensions, so ask what risk the reinforcement is solving rather than selecting it automatically.

9. Consider insurance before dispatch

USFans' beginner guide also lists insurance as an additional parcel service. Evaluate it while the shipment is still a warehouse decision, not after tracking becomes worrying.

Think about the combined replacement difficulty of the parcel, not only the price of one item. At the same time, do not assume every problem is automatically covered. Where insurance is available for the chosen route, read the applicable terms and keep the order and parcel records that could support a claim.

10. Separate restricted goods before choosing a route

USFans currently warns that liquids, powders, pastes, gels, fragile glass or ceramics, certain battery products, food and other categories may be prohibited or restricted depending on regulations and route. Product pages also identify medicines, liquids, creams, powders and cosmetics as limited items.

If a haul includes one of these categories, check compatibility before mixing it into a clothing parcel. Sometimes two simpler shipments are more practical than one large parcel with very limited route options.

11. Choose the delivery method after defining the parcel

USFans tells buyers to select stored items, enter delivery details and choose from available or recommended delivery methods. This sequence matters. Route comparison becomes meaningful only after you know which items are actually traveling together.

Compare routes using the same destination, realistic weight, dimensions and product categories. If a line is unavailable, look for the operational reason first: the destination, parcel dimensions, category mix or current carrier availability may be narrowing the choices.

12. Save the final shipping data

After USFans consolidates, packs, weighs and dispatches the shipment, its beginner guide says buyers can track the parcel through the Parcels area in the account.

Keep the final parcel record, verified weight, route and tracking information together. If the final charge was much higher than expected, review whether packaging, retained boxes or volumetric size caused the difference. Your own shipment history becomes a useful planning dataset for the next haul.

13. A practical parcel-submission workflow

Start when the first item enters the warehouse. Record its arrival date and inspect the free QC photos promptly. Resolve any return decision within the stated five-day window. Continue receiving planned orders while keeping the 90-day free-storage period in view.

When the haul is nearly complete, separate ordinary items from restricted or fragile categories. Decide which products belong together. Review whether bulky retail packaging should be removed, whether specific items need reinforcement, and whether insurance makes sense.

Then estimate shipping with realistic destination, weight, dimensions and category data. Compare the available routes, treat the payment as a deposit, and leave room for final carrier measurements. After dispatch, save the actual parcel data so the next estimate can be better.

Final takeaway

The USFans warehouse stage gives buyers more control than it first appears. The 90-day free-storage period creates time to combine planned orders, but the shorter five-day return window means QC decisions must happen much earlier. Package removal can reduce unnecessary bulk, reinforcement can protect vulnerable items, and insurance can be considered before the parcel leaves.

The most important financial distinction is between the shipping deposit and the final carrier-verified charge. Build estimates from realistic parcel data, keep a sensible buffer, and use final shipment records to improve the next haul. That turns parcel submission from a last-minute checkout step into a repeatable shipping system.
