Restricted Goods & Reverse Shopping

USFans Restricted Items Guide 2026: Batteries, Liquids, Cosmetics & Food Before You Buy

Screen sensitive products before payment so route restrictions do not become a warehouse problem.

26 August 202613 min readEnglish

Buying a low-cost item from Taobao, Tmall, 1688 or another Chinese marketplace is only useful if the item can actually move from the USFans warehouse to your country. Restricted-goods problems often appear late because a marketplace seller can ship something domestically in China even when an international carrier will not accept it. A cosmetic liquid, power bank, jar of cream, snack, glass decoration or magnetic product can behave like an ordinary purchase at first and then remove many shipping options.

USFans currently distinguishes goods it does not accept from goods that may be limited by route, export rules, carrier rules or destination customs. Its shipping estimation page lists liquids, powders, pastes and gels; fragile glass or ceramic items; screens and large glass panels; battery products, especially loose or high-capacity batteries; food, supplements and perishables; and unsafe or improperly packaged goods among common restricted or prohibited categories. Product notices also say tobacco, alcohol and contraband are not accepted, while medicines, liquids, creams, powders and cosmetics can be limited in shipping.

The practical rule is simple: check international compatibility before payment, not after warehouse arrival.

1. Start with the destination, not the product price

A restricted item is not evaluated in a vacuum. Customs rules, carrier networks and available lines differ by destination. USFans' Beginner's Guide tells buyers to enter the destination country during ordering, and its shipping guidance says some items can be restricted for certain delivery types while recommended delivery types are shown from the available list.

Before comparing sellers, define the destination. A product with an acceptable route to one country may have fewer choices to another. For cosmetics, batteries, food and fragile products, treat route compatibility as part of the product specification alongside size, color and price.

2. Separate “not accepted” from “limited”

USFans product notices currently say tobacco, alcohol and contraband are not accepted. That differs from a category being limited in shipping.

The same notices describe medicines, liquids, creams, powders and cosmetics as limited, and the shipping estimator gives broader examples of goods that can face restrictions. “Limited” does not mean every route accepts the item, but it also does not mean every destination rejects it. It means ordinary-line availability should never be assumed.

This distinction prevents paying for something USFans clearly will not accept while also avoiding the assumption that one unavailable standard route makes every sensitive item impossible to ship.

3. Give battery products their own check

Battery products are easy to underestimate because the battery can be hidden inside an ordinary-looking item. USFans' current shipping guidance specifically calls out battery-containing goods, especially loose or high-capacity batteries.

Before buying electronics, identify what is actually inside the product. A wireless accessory, light, appliance or toy may contain a rechargeable cell even when the marketplace title focuses on the device. A replacement battery sold by itself can also present a different shipping situation from a battery installed in equipment.

Do not infer eligibility from small size. The battery configuration matters more than whether the item fits in your hand.

4. Plan liquids, creams, powders and cosmetics early

Beauty products create a common reverse-shopping trap. USFans currently identifies liquids, creams, powders and cosmetics as limited items, while its shipping estimator also lists pastes and gels.

That means “cosmetics” is not one shipping category. A solid accessory, powder compact, liquid fragrance and cream can face different transport limits even when a marketplace puts them in the same department.

When a listing is unclear, identify the physical form before paying. Look for capacity, ingredients, packaging type or a product description showing whether the contents are liquid, powder, paste or gel. If the form remains uncertain, clarify it before purchase rather than hoping warehouse classification will solve the problem later.

5. Treat food and supplements as special purchases

USFans' shipping guidance includes food, health supplements and perishable goods among categories that can be restricted or prohibited. Their risk is not primarily weight. Destination rules may care about ingredients, animal or plant content, labeling, expiration dates and personal-import conditions.

Do not add snacks or supplements simply to use spare parcel space. First determine whether a suitable line exists for the destination. A cheap lightweight product is not a bargain if it makes the parcel harder to submit.

6. Separate fragile-item acceptance from damage risk

USFans lists fragile glass and ceramic goods, screens, monitors and large glass panels in its restricted-goods guidance. There are two decisions: whether a route can accept the item and whether the item can survive the route.

USFans' Beginner's Guide says additional parcel services can include package reinforcement, but reinforcement cannot turn every fragile object into a low-risk shipment. Before buying, consider dimensions, shape and replaceability. An inexpensive glass item can require substantial protective packing and consume more parcel volume than its price suggests.

7. Watch for blades, lighters, magnets and seeds

Current USFans product notices give restricted-item examples including knives and cutting tools, lighters, magnetic components and seeds. These examples show why marketplace category labels can be misleading.

A kitchen accessory can contain a blade. A collectible can include a lighter. A phone accessory can contain a strong magnet. A gardening item can contain seeds. Read what the product physically is, not only what the seller calls it. If it has a blade, ignition source, magnet, biological material or another special characteristic, verify the shipping situation before ordering.

8. Use the estimator as a screen, not a promise

The USFans shipping estimation page helps buyers think about destination and parcel conditions, but an estimate should not be treated as a guarantee that a specific sensitive item is accepted by every displayed service.

A better workflow is to identify the item's sensitive characteristic, set the real destination, check current shipping guidance, and clarify uncertain cases before payment. After warehouse arrival, use the delivery choices actually recommended for the stored goods.

In reverse shopping, shipping eligibility belongs upstream in the buying decision.

9. Do not confuse warehouse QC with shipping approval

USFans currently advertises three to seven free HD quality-inspection photos after warehouse arrival. Those images can confirm model, color, visible condition and packaging.

They do not make a restricted item shippable.

A QC photo can reveal a leaking bottle or damaged glass, but transport eligibility comes from product type, route rules and destination requirements. Treat QC and shipping classification as separate checkpoints.

10. Use the five-day return application window defensively

Current USFans product pages say buyers who are dissatisfied after warehouse arrival can apply for a return within five days, with the agent negotiating with the seller.

For sensitive goods, review the item and practical route options quickly. If the product type is incompatible with realistic shipping choices, investigate immediately rather than leaving it in storage while planning the rest of the haul.

A return application is not a guaranteed seller-approved return. Seller rules still affect the outcome, so checking restrictions before payment remains safer than using returns as a fallback shipping strategy.

11. Keep sensitive goods separate in a mixed-haul plan

A mixed parcel can inherit the limitations of its most difficult item. Five ordinary T-shirts do not make a bottle of liquid ordinary cargo. If that bottle requires a special line, it can change the choices available for the whole parcel.

Divide purchases into standard goods and sensitive goods. Plan the standard group around weight, volume and consolidation. Plan the sensitive group around route eligibility first, then cost. Sometimes combining everything is efficient; sometimes separating one sensitive product creates a cleaner shipping decision. Use the live route choices for the destination and stored items rather than a fixed rule.

12. Understand the downside of a wrong classification

USFans' current shipping guidance warns that packages containing unconfirmed prohibited or restricted items may be delayed, returned, seized by customs or destroyed, and such situations may not be covered by shipping insurance.

The downside is therefore larger than a slightly higher fee. A classification mistake can create a parcel-level problem after product and domestic-shipping costs have already been paid.

Never try to hide a sensitive characteristic behind a generic description. Surface the issue early and choose a route that actually supports the item.

13. Use a five-question screen before payment

Before buying anything that might be sensitive, ask: What is the item physically: solid, liquid, powder, gel, battery, food, fragile glass, blade, magnet or something else?

Does USFans currently describe the category as not accepted, restricted or limited?

Which country will receive it, and do current delivery choices support the category?

Could the item force an otherwise normal haul onto a narrower shipping route?

If shipping becomes impractical, can you still address the issue within the after-sales timing and seller rules?

If any answer is unclear, resolve it before payment. That habit removes much of the avoidable risk from sensitive-goods reverse shopping.

Frequently asked questions

Can USFans ship liquids?
USFans currently identifies liquids as limited or restricted goods. Availability depends on the applicable route and destination, so compatibility should be checked before purchase.

Can I buy cosmetics through USFans?
Cosmetics can be limited in shipping. The physical form matters, especially for liquids, creams, powders, pastes and gels.

Are battery products allowed?
USFans' shipping guidance lists battery-containing items among common restricted categories, especially loose or high-capacity batteries. Check the exact configuration and route first.

Does USFans accept tobacco or alcohol?
Current product notices state that tobacco and alcohol are not accepted.

Can I ship food or supplements?
USFans lists food, health supplements and perishables among categories that can be restricted or prohibited. Destination rules and route availability matter.

Will reinforcement make glass safe to ship?
Reinforcement can add protection, but it does not guarantee carrier acceptance or eliminate breakage risk.

Can I return a sensitive item after warehouse arrival?
Current product pages say buyers can apply for return within five days after arrival, with USFans negotiating with the seller. Seller rules still affect the result.

Final takeaway

The best time to solve a restricted-goods problem is before the item becomes warehouse inventory. Start with the destination, identify the product's real physical characteristics, distinguish goods USFans does not accept from goods that are merely limited, and check route compatibility before payment.

For batteries, liquids, creams, powders, cosmetics, food, glass, blades, lighters, magnets and other sensitive categories, avoid assumptions based on price, size or marketplace department. A small product can still create a large routing constraint.

Once the item arrives, use QC photos to verify condition, review the delivery types available for the actual stored goods, and act quickly if the shipment no longer makes sense. The goal is not to chase exceptions. It is to build international shipping into the purchase decision so the warehouse becomes a checkpoint rather than the first place you discover a problem.
